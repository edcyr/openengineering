Typing answers

Answer boxes understand arithmetic, so you can type the expression you wrote on paper instead of working it out on a calculator first. Each box shows how it read your input (for example = 23.16), so you can catch a typo before you check.

Give each answer in the unit shown next to its box: meters, seconds, meters per second or meters per second squared. Signs matter where the problem names a positive direction: type the minus sign for a velocity or an acceleration the other way. Derivative problems ask for an expression in \(t\): use ^ for powers, e^(...) for the exponential and ln for the natural logarithm; angles inside sin and cos are in radians.

Tip

Typing the whole expression is faster and safer than rounding at each step: (5+sqrt(25+240))/2 is exact, while a rounded square root carried into later steps can drift past the 1% tolerance.
