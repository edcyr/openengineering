A

Back to top
Absolute dependent motion
The motion of particles tied together by inextensible cords, analyzed with positions measured from fixed datums. The length of each cord, written in those positions, is constant; differentiating it relates the velocities and the accelerations.
See: Lesson 7Related: Total cord length \(l_T\), Datum, Block and tackle
Acceleration \(a\)
The rate of change of the velocity, \(a = dv/dt = d^2s/dt^2\), in \(\text{m/s}^2\). Its sign tells which way the velocity is changing, not whether the particle speeds up.
See: Lesson 3Related: Velocity \(v\), Speeding up and slowing down, \(a\,ds = v\,dv\)
Acceleration due to gravity \(g\)
The acceleration of a freely falling body near the Earth's surface, \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\), directed downward.
See: Lesson 4Related: Free fall
\(a\,ds = v\,dv\)
The kinematic relation without time, from dividing \(a = dv/dt\) by \(v = ds/dt\); also written \(a = v\,dv/ds\). Use it when the acceleration or the velocity is known as a function of position.
See: Lesson 3, Lesson 5Related: Acceleration \(a\), \(a\)–\(s\) graph, Separation of variables
Antiderivative
A function \(F(t)\) whose derivative is \(f(t)\). Any two antiderivatives of the same function differ by a constant.
See: Lesson 2Related: Indefinite integral, Constant of integration \(C\)
\(a\)–\(s\) graph
A graph of acceleration against position. The area under it between two positions equals \(\tfrac12(v_2^2 - v_1^2)\), from \(a\,ds = v\,dv\).
See: Lesson 6Related: Graphical method, \(a\,ds = v\,dv\)
Average speed
The distance traveled divided by the time taken, \(s_T/\Delta t\). Never negative, and at least as large as the magnitude of the average velocity.
See: Lesson 3Related: Average velocity, Distance traveled \(s_T\)
Average velocity
The displacement divided by the time taken, \(\Delta s/\Delta t\). Signed: it is zero for a round trip.
See: Lesson 3Related: Velocity \(v\), Displacement \(\Delta s\), Average speed

B

Back to top
Block and tackle
A load hung from movable pulleys held by \(n\) parts of one rope. With positions measured down from the fixed pulleys, \(n\,s_B + s_A = l_T\), so the load moves at \(1/n\) of the speed of the free end.
See: Lesson 7Related: Absolute dependent motion, Total cord length \(l_T\)
Braking and stopping distance
The braking distance is covered while the brakes decelerate a vehicle to rest, \(v_0^2/(2\lvert a_c\rvert)\) for a constant deceleration. The stopping distance adds the distance covered during the reaction time, \(v_0 t_r\).
See: Lesson 4Related: Reaction time, Constant acceleration \(a_c\)

C

Back to top
Chain rule
The derivative of a function of a function: \(\dfrac{d}{dt}f\big(u(t)\big) = \dfrac{df}{du}\dfrac{du}{dt}\). It gives \((\sin kt)' = k\cos kt\), \(a = v\,dv/ds\), and the derivative of a constraint such as \(x^2 + y^2 = L^2\).
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 1Related: Product rule, Quotient rule, Derivative
Constant acceleration \(a_c\)
An acceleration that does not change. Then \(v = v_0 + a_c t\), \(s = s_0 + v_0 t + \tfrac12 a_c t^2\) and \(v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a_c(s - s_0)\). These hold only when the acceleration is constant.
See: Lesson 4Related: Free fall, Braking and stopping distance, Acceleration \(a\)
Constant of integration \(C\)
The arbitrary constant added to an indefinite integral, since every constant has zero derivative. An initial condition fixes its value; integrating with limits avoids it.
See: Lesson 2Related: Initial condition, Indefinite integral

D

Back to top
Datum
A fixed point or line from which a position coordinate is measured, such as a ceiling or the axle of a fixed pulley. Its time derivative is then an absolute velocity.
See: Lesson 7Related: Absolute dependent motion, Position coordinate \(s\)
Deceleration
An acceleration that reduces the speed, whatever its sign. A particle decelerates when its velocity and acceleration have opposite signs.
See: Lesson 3Related: Speeding up and slowing down, Acceleration \(a\)
Definite integral
An integral with limits, \(\int_a^b f\,dt = F(b) - F(a)\) by the fundamental theorem of calculus. It is a number: the signed area between the graph and the axis.
See: Lesson 2Related: Signed area, Antiderivative
Derivative
The instantaneous rate of change of a function, \(df/dt = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\Delta f/\Delta t\), and the slope of the tangent to its graph. A dot over a symbol is a derivative with respect to time.
See: Lesson 1Related: Tangent line, Secant, Second derivative
Displacement \(\Delta s\)
The change in position over an interval, \(\Delta s = s_2 - s_1\). Signed; it equals the area under the velocity–time graph.
See: Lesson 3Related: Distance traveled \(s_T\), Position coordinate \(s\)
Distance traveled \(s_T\)
The total length of path covered, always positive. When the particle turns back, add the length of each leg; it differs from the displacement.
See: Lesson 3Related: Displacement \(\Delta s\), Turning point
Drag
A force that opposes the motion and grows with speed, giving an acceleration that depends on velocity, such as \(a = -kv\) or \(a = -kv^2\).
See: Lesson 5Related: Terminal velocity, Separation of variables

E

Back to top
Erratic motion
Motion that changes in stages, so that no single formula describes it. It is described by graphs and analyzed with their slopes and areas.
See: Lesson 6Related: Graphical method, \(v\)–\(t\) graph
Escape velocity
The smallest launch speed with which a body never falls back, found by integrating \(v\,dv = a\,ds\) with gravity that weakens with height: \(\sqrt{2gR}\), about \(11.2\ \text{km/s}\) for the Earth.
See: Lesson 5Related: \(a\,ds = v\,dv\), Free fall

F

Back to top
Free fall
Motion under gravity alone near the Earth's surface: a constant acceleration \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\) downward, at every instant, including at the top of a vertical throw.
See: Lesson 4Related: Constant acceleration \(a_c\), Acceleration due to gravity \(g\)

G

Back to top
Graphical method
Analyzing a motion with its graphs: slopes take you from \(s\)–\(t\) to \(v\)–\(t\) to \(a\)–\(t\), and areas take you back up.
See: Lesson 6Related: \(v\)–\(t\) graph, \(a\)–\(s\) graph, Erratic motion

Back to top
Indefinite integral
The family of all antiderivatives, \(\int f\,dt = F(t) + C\).
See: Lesson 2Related: Antiderivative, Constant of integration \(C\), Definite integral
Initial condition
A known value at one instant, usually \(s_0\) and \(v_0\) at \(t = 0\). It fixes the constant of integration, or becomes the lower limit of an integral.
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 2Related: Constant of integration \(C\)
Instantaneous velocity
The velocity at one instant, the limit of the average velocity over a shrinking interval: \(v = ds/dt\).
See: Lesson 3Related: Velocity \(v\), Average velocity

P

Back to top
Position coordinate \(s\)
The signed distance from a fixed origin \(O\) to the particle along its straight-line path, in meters, positive in the direction you choose.
See: Lesson 3Related: Displacement \(\Delta s\), Datum
Power rule
\(\dfrac{d}{dt}t^n = n\,t^{n-1}\) for any constant \(n\). Run backward, \(\int t^n\,dt = t^{n+1}/(n + 1) + C\) for \(n \ne -1\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Derivative, Antiderivative
Product rule
\((fg)' = f\,'g + fg'\): differentiate each factor in turn, holding the other.
See: Lesson 1Related: Quotient rule, Chain rule

Q

Back to top
Quotient rule
\(\left(\dfrac{f}{g}\right)' = \dfrac{f\,'g - fg'}{g^2}\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Product rule, Chain rule

R

Back to top
Reaction time
The time between seeing a hazard and acting on it. During it a vehicle keeps its speed, so it adds \(v_0 t_r\) to the stopping distance.
See: Lesson 4Related: Braking and stopping distance
Rectilinear motion
Motion along a straight line, described by one position coordinate \(s\) and its derivatives.
See: Lesson 3Related: Position coordinate \(s\), Velocity \(v\), Acceleration \(a\)
Relative position \(s_{B/A}\)
The position of \(B\) as seen from \(A\): \(s_{B/A} = s_B - s_A\), with both measured from the same origin in the same direction. Its derivatives are the relative velocity \(v_{B/A}\) and acceleration \(a_{B/A}\).
See: Lesson 8Related: Relative velocity \(v_{B/A}\), Translating frame
Relative velocity \(v_{B/A}\)
The velocity of \(B\) as seen from \(A\): \(v_{B/A} = v_B - v_A\), and \(v_{A/B} = -v_{B/A}\). Likewise \(a_{B/A} = a_B - a_A\).
See: Lesson 8Related: Relative position \(s_{B/A}\), Translating frame

S

Back to top
Secant
The straight line through two points of a graph. Its slope is the average rate of change over the interval; as the interval shrinks, it becomes the tangent.
See: Lesson 1Related: Tangent line, Derivative
Second derivative
The derivative of the derivative, \(d^2f/dt^2\) or \(\ddot f\). The second derivative of position is the acceleration.
See: Lesson 1Related: Derivative, Acceleration \(a\)
Separation of variables
Rearranging a rate equation so that each side holds one variable, then integrating both sides: \(dv/dt = g(v)\) becomes \(\int dv/g(v) = \int dt\).
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 5Related: Drag, \(a\,ds = v\,dv\)
Signed area
The area between a graph and the horizontal axis, counted positive above the axis and negative below it. It is what a definite integral measures.
See: Lesson 2Related: Definite integral, \(v\)–\(t\) graph
Speed
The magnitude of the velocity, never negative.
See: Lesson 3Related: Velocity \(v\), Average speed
Speeding up and slowing down
A particle speeds up when its velocity and acceleration have the same sign, and slows down when they have opposite signs.
See: Lesson 3Related: Acceleration \(a\), Deceleration
Substitution
Integrating by changing the variable: replace a function \(u(t)\) and \(u'(t)\,dt\) by \(u\) and \(du\). It reverses the chain rule.
See: Lesson 2Related: Chain rule, Antiderivative

T

Back to top
Tangent line
The straight line that touches a graph at a point and has the same slope there. Its slope is the derivative.
See: Lesson 1Related: Derivative, Secant
Terminal velocity
The constant velocity a falling body approaches when the drag grows to balance its weight, so that \(a = 0\).
See: Lesson 5Related: Drag
Total cord length \(l_T\)
The length of a cord written as the sum of its segments in terms of the position coordinates. It is constant, so its derivatives are zero; segments around pulleys add only a constant.
See: Lesson 7Related: Absolute dependent motion, Block and tackle
Translating frame
A frame of reference that moves with a particle without rotating. Seen from it, the particle is at rest and others move with their relative motion.
See: Lesson 8Related: Relative position \(s_{B/A}\), Relative velocity \(v_{B/A}\)
Turning point
An instant where the velocity is zero and changes sign, so the particle reverses. Locate the turning points first when you compute a distance traveled.
See: Lesson 3Related: Distance traveled \(s_T\), Velocity \(v\)

V

Back to top
Velocity \(v\)
The rate of change of position, \(v = ds/dt\), in m/s: positive when the particle moves in the positive direction. Its magnitude is the speed.
See: Lesson 3Related: Speed, Acceleration \(a\), Instantaneous velocity
\(v\)–\(t\) graph
A graph of velocity against time. Its slope is the acceleration, and the area under it between two instants is the displacement.
See: Lesson 6Related: Graphical method, Signed area
