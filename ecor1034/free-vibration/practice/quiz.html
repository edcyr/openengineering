Self-Check Quiz

Fifteen questions on the core skills of Lessons 1–8: describing a harmonic motion, its velocity and acceleration, the natural frequency of a spring–mass system, the response from initial conditions, vertical springs, springs in parallel and in series, the simple pendulum and energy. Each question has a worked solution that you see after you submit.

How it works

  • Work on your own with a calculator and scrap paper. Try it without notes first: that shows you what you really know.
  • Nothing is marked until you press Submit quiz. Until then you can move between questions and change any answer.
  • Each question is either right or wrong. When a question has several boxes, every box must be right.
  • After you submit, you see your score, a review of every question with a worked solution, and a results sheet you can print or save as a PDF.
  • Use \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\). Give each answer in the unit shown next to its box: meters, seconds, meters per second or meters per second squared. Question 1 asks for an expression in \(t\); angles inside sin and cos are in radians.

Each box shows how it read your entry (for example = 2.537), so you can check it before you submit. Type only the number or expression; the unit is shown next to the box.

How close is close enough?

Numbers are accepted within \(1\%\) of the exact value, so three or four significant figures are plenty. Typing the whole expression, such as 2*pi*sqrt(1.6/9.81), is always safe: nothing is rounded along the way.

Two slips cost the most marks: mixing up the circular frequency \(\omega\) (rad/s) and the frequency \(f\) (Hz), which differ by \(2\pi\), and taking a phase angle straight from a calculator's \(\tan^{-1}\) without checking its quadrant. Keep your calculator in radians for \(\sin(\omega t + \phi)\).
