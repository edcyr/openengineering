ReferenceFree Vibration

Formula Sheet

The relations of free undamped vibration with one degree of freedom. Conventions: Hibbeler notation (displacement \(x\) or \(y\) from the static equilibrium position, \(\dot x = dx/dt\), \(\ddot x = d^2x/dt^2\), natural frequency \(\omega_n\), amplitude \(C\), phase angle \(\phi\)); angles in radians inside sines and cosines; SI units with \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\).

Fits on two pages of Letter or A4. For a digital copy, choose “Save as PDF” as the printer.

Simple harmonic motion

Displacement

\[ x = C\sin(\omega t + \phi) \qquad x(0) = C\sin\phi \]
Period, frequency and circular frequency
\(f = \dfrac{1}{\tau}\) (Hz)\(\omega = 2\pi f = \dfrac{2\pi}{\tau}\) (rad/s)

The projection of a point turning steadily around a circle of radius \(C\) (the reference circle).

Velocity and acceleration

Derivatives

\[ \colVel{\dot x} = C\omega\cos(\omega t + \phi) \qquad \colAcc{\ddot x} = -C\omega^2\sin(\omega t + \phi) = -\omega^2 x \]
  • \(v_{\max} = C\omega\) at \(x = 0\); \(a_{\max} = C\omega^2\) at \(x = \pm C\), where \(\dot x = 0\).
  • Speed at a position: \(\dot x^2 = \omega^2(C^2 - x^2)\).
  • \(\ddot x = -\omega^2 x\) is the signature of simple harmonic motion.

Spring–mass system

Equation of motion and natural frequency

\[ m\ddot x + kx = 0 \qquad \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \qquad \tau = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} \]

Solution: \(x = A\sin\omega_n t + B\cos\omega_n t = C\sin(\omega_n t + \phi)\). The period does not depend on the amplitude.

More in Lesson 3

Initial conditions

Response from \(x_0\) and \(\dot x_0\)

\[ x = \frac{\dot x_0}{\omega_n}\sin\omega_n t + x_0\cos\omega_n t \]
Amplitude and phase angle
\(A = \dot x_0/\omega_n,\ \ B = x_0\)\(C = \sqrt{A^2 + B^2}\)
\(\sin\phi = B/C,\ \ \cos\phi = A/C\)\(A \lt 0\): add \(180^\circ\) to \(\tan^{-1}(B/A)\)

Vertical springs

Static deflection

\[ k\,\delta_{st} = mg \qquad \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{g}{\delta_{st}}} \]

Measure \(y\) from the static equilibrium position: the weight drops out, \(m\ddot y + ky = 0\). Spring force \(k(\delta_{st} + y)\).

More in Lesson 5

Springs in parallel and series

Parallel: same displacement

\[ k_{eq} = k_1 + k_2 \]

Series: same force

\[ \frac{1}{k_{eq}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2} \]

Springs on opposite sides of a mass are in parallel. Half a spring is twice as stiff. Then \(\omega_n = \sqrt{k_{eq}/m}\).

More in Lesson 6

Simple pendulum

Equation of motion; small angles

\[ \ddot\theta + \frac{g}{l}\sin\theta = 0 \;\;\xrightarrow{\ \sin\theta\,\approx\,\theta\ }\;\; \omega_n = \sqrt{\frac{g}{l}} \qquad \tau = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}} \]

Independent of the mass. Within 1% up to about \(20^\circ\); larger swings take longer. Tension: \(T - mg\cos\theta = ml\dot\theta^2\).

More in Lesson 7

Energy

Conservation and the energy method

\[ \tfrac12 m\dot x^2 + \tfrac12 kx^2 = \tfrac12 kC^2 = \tfrac12 m v_{\max}^2 \qquad \frac{d}{dt}(T + V) = 0 \]

Pendulum: \(T = \tfrac12 ml^2\dot\theta^2\), \(V = mgl(1 - \cos\theta)\).

Common mistakes

  • \(\omega\) and \(f\) mixed up: they differ by \(2\pi\).
  • Degrees in \(\sin(\omega t + \phi)\): use radians.
  • The quadrant of \(\phi\) not checked when \(A \lt 0\).
  • \(\dot x_0\) used for \(A\) without dividing by \(\omega_n\).
  • Displacement from the unstretched length of a vertical spring instead of from static equilibrium.
  • Parallel and series swapped: ask whether the springs share the displacement or the force.
  • \(\sin\theta \approx \theta\) used for large swings.

Symbols and units

  • \(x,\ y\) displacement from equilibrium (m)
  • \(\dot x,\ \ddot x\) velocity, acceleration (m/s, m/s²)
  • \(C\) amplitude; \(\phi\) phase angle (m; rad)
  • \(\tau\) period; \(f\) frequency (s; Hz)
  • \(\omega,\ \omega_n\) circular, natural frequency (rad/s)
  • \(k,\ k_{eq}\) stiffness, equivalent stiffness (N/m)
  • \(\delta_{st}\) static deflection (m)
  • \(l,\ \theta\) pendulum length, angle (m, rad)
