Systems Lab

Pick a scene, change its numbers, and watch a system of particles at work. Follow one particle from another, release blocks on cords, let a wedge slide out from under a block, burst a shell in flight, walk along a boat, and collide carts, bullets and pucks. In every scene, look for what the internal forces cannot change: the motion of the center of mass and the total momentum.

Things to try

  1. A collision course. Give car \(B\) \(24\ \text{m/s}\): both cars now reach the intersection at \(10\ \text{s}\), and seen from \(A\), \(B\)'s straight line runs right through \(A\).
  2. A balance that is not. With \(m_A = m_B + m_C\), the blocks balance only if \(m_B = m_C\). Try \(6, 3, 3\), then \(6, 2, 4\).
  3. A heavy wedge is a fixed incline. With \(m_W = 50\ \text{kg}\) and \(m_B = 2\ \text{kg}\), the wedge hardly moves and \(a_{B/W}\) is close to \(g\sin\theta\).
  4. The center of mass leaves the parabola. Burst the shell early into unequal pieces with a vertical kick: one piece lands first, the ground pushes on it, and \(G\) stops following the dashed curve.
  5. A light boat. On a \(40\ \text{kg}\) boat an \(80\ \text{kg}\) person moves only a third of the walk over the water; the boat slides back the other two thirds.
  6. Half the energy. With equal masses, the block and the plank end at half the block's speed, and half the kinetic energy is lost to friction.
  7. Swapping velocities. Equal masses with \(e = 1\) exchange velocities: \(A\) leaves at \(B\)'s velocity, and \(B\) at \(A\)'s.
  8. Where the bullet's energy goes. A light block keeps a larger share of the bullet's energy, \(m/(m + M)\), and swings higher.
  9. A right angle. Equal pucks with \(e = 1\) leave at \(90^\circ\) to each other, whatever the angle of impact.

How the lab works

Each scene is solved exactly, with the equations of the lessons: relative velocities add as vectors (Lesson 1); the blocks on cords and the wedge have constant accelerations from their FBDs and kinetic diagrams (Lesson 3); the shell's pieces fly on parabolas after a burst that leaves \(\vvec_G\) unchanged (Lesson 4); the plank slides with constant friction until the two move together (Lesson 5); and the impacts use momentum with the coefficient of restitution (Lessons 7 and 8). The ballistic pendulum's swing is integrated in small time steps.

Tip

Pause at any instant and check a readout by hand: add the momenta, or the kinetic energies, and compare them with the values before the event.
