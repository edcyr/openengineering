ReferenceSystems of Particles

Formula Sheet

The general laws and relations that every solution in the module is built from. Results the lessons derive for particular cases, such as the accelerations of a block on a free wedge or the tensions of a double Atwood machine, are left out on purpose: derive them from these. Conventions: Hibbeler notation (center of mass \(G\) with \(\rvec_G\), \(\vvec_G\), \(\avec_G\); \(\Lvec = m\vvec\); \(\int\Fvec\,dt\) for an impulse; \((v_A)_1\) before and \((v_A)_2\) after an impact; free-body and kinetic diagrams); one positive direction for each axis; cords light and inextensible, pulleys light and smooth; SI units with \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\).

Fits on two pages of Letter or A4. For a digital copy, choose “Save as PDF” as the printer.

Relative motion

In axes that translate with \(A\)

\[ \begin{gathered} \colRB{\rvec_B} = \colRA{\rvec_A} + \colRel{\rvec_{B/A}} \\ \colRB{\vvec_B} = \colRA{\vvec_A} + \colRel{\vvec_{B/A}} \qquad \colRB{\avec_B} = \colRA{\avec_A} + \colRel{\avec_{B/A}} \end{gathered} \]

\(\vvec_{A/B} = -\vvec_{B/A}\). A vector triangle: any two give the third. In a current or a wind, \(\vvec = \vvec_{\text{medium}} + \vvec_{/\text{medium}}\). With constant velocities, \(B\) seen from \(A\) moves in a straight line; the closest approach is where \(\rvec_{B/A}\cdot\vvec_{B/A} = 0\).

Dependent motion

Degrees of freedom

\[ \text{DOF} = n - c \]

An inextensible cord

\[ \textstyle\sum_i k_i s_i + \text{const} = l_T \]

\(n\) coordinates, \(c\) independent constraints. Measure each \(s_i\) from a fixed datum along body \(i\)'s line of motion; \(k_i\) counts the straight parts of the cord that change with it; \(l_T\) is the cord's total length, and the constant parts wrap the pulleys. Differentiate: \(\sum k_i v_i = 0\), \(\sum k_i a_i = 0\).

A constraint that is not linear, such as a link \(x_A^2 + y_B^2 = L^2\), is differentiated with the chain rule: \(x_A v_A + y_B v_B = 0\). A rigid link keeps \(\vvec_{B/A}\) perpendicular to itself.

Equations of motion for a system

Each particle \(i\) of the system

\[ \Fvec_i + \textstyle\sum_{j \ne i} \colInt{\Fvec_{ij}} = m_i\colMA{\avec_i} \qquad \colInt{\Fvec_{ji}} = -\colInt{\Fvec_{ij}} \]

\(\Fvec_i\): external forces; \(\Fvec_{ij}\): internal force of particle \(j\) on \(i\). Procedure: (1) kinematics: the constraints and relative accelerations; (2) an FBD and a kinetic diagram for each particle, internal forces on both particles they join; (3) FBD = kinetic diagram, by components; (4) count: unknowns = equations; (5) solve and check the signs.

A light pulley or cord has no mass: its FBD must balance.

The center of mass

\[ m\rvec_G = \textstyle\sum m_i\rvec_i \qquad m\vvec_G = \sum m_i\vvec_i \qquad m\avec_G = \sum m_i\avec_i \]

Equation of motion of the center of mass

\[ \textstyle\sum\Fvec = m\colMA{\avec_G} \]

External forces only: the internal forces cancel in pairs. If \(\sum F_x = 0\), then \((v_G)_x\) is constant; a center of mass at rest stays at rest.

Work and energy for a system

Principle of work and energy

\[ \textstyle\sum T_1 + \sum U_{1-2} = \sum T_2 \]

Conservative systems

\[ T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2 \]

\(\sum U_{1-2}\): the work of all forces, external and internal. An internal pair does \(dU = \colInt{\Fvec_{ij}}\cdot d\rvec_{i/j}\): zero for an inextensible cord or a rigid link; \(-\Delta V_e\) for a spring, \(V_e = \tfrac12 ks^2\); \(-F_f d\) for kinetic friction, with \(d\) the relative sliding distance. \(V_g = mgy\).

Kinetic energy of a system

\[ T = \textstyle\sum\tfrac12 m_i v_i^2 = \tfrac12 mv_G^2 + \sum\tfrac12 m_i v_{i/G}^2 \]

Impulse and momentum

Principle of linear impulse and momentum

\[ \Lvec_1 + \textstyle\sum\displaystyle\int_{t_1}^{t_2}\Fvec\,dt = \Lvec_2 \qquad \colL{\Lvec} = \sum m_i\vvec_i = m\vvec_G \]

External forces only. Conservation: if \(\sum\int\Fvec\,dt = \mathbf 0\) (in all directions, or in one), \(\Lvec_1 = \Lvec_2\) (or \((L_x)_1 = (L_x)_2\)). Add momenta with velocities over the ground: convert relative velocities first.

Impact

Along the line of impact \(n\)

\[ \begin{gathered} m_A(v_A)_1 + m_B(v_B)_1 = m_A(v_A)_2 + m_B(v_B)_2 \\ e = \frac{(v_B)_2 - (v_A)_2}{(v_A)_1 - (v_B)_1} = \frac{\int R\,dt}{\int P\,dt} \end{gathered} \]
  • Plastic (\(e = 0\)): the bodies move on together, \((v_A)_2 = (v_B)_2 = v_2\). Perfectly elastic: \(e = 1\), no energy lost.
  • Oblique, smooth bodies: each body's \(t\) component is unchanged; the \(n\) components obey the two equations above.
  • A fixed surface: \((v_n)_2 = -e\,(v_n)_1\); a bounce gives \(e = \sqrt{h_2/h_1}\).
  • The kinetic energy lost is \(T_1 - T_2\); never use energy across an impact.

More in Lessons 7 and 8

Impulsive forces

  • Keep impulsive forces: contact forces in collisions, cords that snap taut, rigid supports and stops that prevent a motion.
  • Drop nonimpulsive forces: weights, springs, friction from a finite normal force. Over \(\Delta t \to 0\) their impulse vanishes.
  • During the impact the positions do not change; the velocities do.

Common mistakes

  • Energy across an impact. Use momentum across it, energy before and after.
  • A relative velocity in a momentum sum. Convert to velocities over the ground first.
  • Internal forces in \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\) or in \(\sum\int\Fvec\,dt\): they cancel.
  • Internal forces left out of \(U_{1-2}\) when a spring or sliding friction acts between particles.
  • One acceleration for every body when a movable pulley or a moving wedge ties them unequally.
  • Momentum conserved in a direction in which a support or the ground pushes impulsively.
  • The sign of \(e\): separation after over approach before, along one positive direction.

A solution, step by step

A \(20\ \text{g}\) bullet embeds in a \(4\ \text{kg}\) block hanging on \(1.5\ \text{m}\) cords, which swing to \(25^\circ\). Find the bullet's speed.

  1. Split the problem: the impact (plastic, brief) and the swing (smooth).
  2. Swing, energy: \(h = 1.5(1 - \cos 25^\circ) = 0.1405\ \text{m}\); \(v_2 = \sqrt{2gh} = 1.661\ \text{m/s}\).
  3. Impact, momentum: the cords pull vertically, so \(0.02v = 4.02(1.661)\), \(v = 334\ \text{m/s}\).
  4. Check: the impact keeps \(m/(m + M) = 0.5\%\) of the energy, so energy across it would be badly wrong.

Symbols and units

  • \(\rvec_{B/A},\ \vvec_{B/A},\ \avec_{B/A}\) position, velocity, acceleration of \(B\) relative to \(A\) (m, m/s, m/s²)
  • \(G\); \(\rvec_G,\ \vvec_G,\ \avec_G\) the center of mass; its position, velocity, acceleration (m, m/s, m/s²)
  • \(m = \sum m_i\) total mass of the system (kg)
  • \(\colInt{\Fvec_{ij}}\) internal force of particle \(j\) on particle \(i\) (N)
  • \(U_{1-2}\) work done from position 1 to 2 (J)
  • \(T,\ V\) kinetic and potential energy (J)
  • \(\colL{\Lvec}\) linear momentum (kg·m/s)
  • \(\Ivec = \int\Fvec\,dt\) linear impulse of a force (N·s)
  • \(e\) coefficient of restitution (no unit)
  • \(n,\ t\) line of impact and its tangent
  • \((v_A)_1,\ (v_A)_2\) velocity of \(A\) just before and just after an impact (m/s)
