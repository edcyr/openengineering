A

Absolute motion
Motion measured from a fixed point with axes fixed to the ground, an inertial frame. Newton's second law, the work-energy principle and the impulse-momentum principle all use absolute velocities and accelerations.
See: Lesson 1Related: Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\), Translating frame

B

Back to top
Ballistic pendulum
A block hanging on cords that a bullet embeds in. The impact is plastic and conserves momentum; the swing that follows conserves \(T + V\). The height of the swing gives the speed just after the impact, and momentum then gives the bullet's speed.
See: Lesson 7Related: Plastic impact, Impulsive motion

C

Back to top
Center of mass \(G\)
The mass-weighted average position of a system, \(m\rvec_G = \sum m_i\rvec_i\); its velocity and acceleration are \(\vvec_G\) and \(\avec_G\). It need not lie on any particle.
See: Lesson 4Related: Motion of the center of mass \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\), Linear momentum \(\Lvec\)
Closest approach
The smallest distance between two moving particles. With constant velocities, \(B\) seen from \(A\) moves in a straight line, and the closest approach is where \(\rvec_{B/A}\) is perpendicular to \(\vvec_{B/A}\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Relative position \(\rvec_{B/A}\), Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\)
Coefficient of restitution \(e\)
The ratio of the restitution impulse to the deformation impulse in an impact, \(e = \int R\,dt/\int P\,dt\), equal to the relative velocity of separation over the relative velocity of approach along the line of impact: \({e = [(v_B)_2 - (v_A)_2]/[(v_A)_1 - (v_B)_1]}\). From \(0\) (plastic) to \(1\) (perfectly elastic).
See: Lesson 8Related: Direct central impact, Plastic impact, Perfectly elastic impact
Conservation of linear momentum
When the external forces on a system give no impulse, its momentum does not change: \(\Lvec_1 = \Lvec_2\). It may hold in one direction only, the one in which no external force acts.
See: Lesson 6Related: Linear momentum \(\Lvec\), Linear impulse \(\Ivec = \int\Fvec\,dt\), Internal force
Conservation of mechanical energy
For a system on which only conservative forces do work, \(T_1 + V_1 = T_2 + V_2\), with \(V\) the gravitational energy of every particle and the elastic energy of every spring, those between particles included.
See: Lesson 5Related: Conservative system, Principle of work and energy \(\sum T_1 + \sum U_{1-2} = \sum T_2\)
Conservative system
A system in which every force that does work is conservative: gravity and springs. Forces that do no work, such as the tension of an inextensible cord, may act; friction between sliding parts may not.
See: Lesson 5Related: Conservation of mechanical energy, Work of internal forces
Constraint (kinematic)
A geometric condition the positions of the particles must satisfy at every instant: a cord keeps its length, a collar stays on its rod. Written in position coordinates and differentiated, it ties the velocities and accelerations together.
See: Lesson 2Related: Degrees of freedom, Position coordinate, Rigid link

D

Back to top
Datum
A fixed point or line, such as the axle of a fixed pulley, from which position coordinates are measured. Every coordinate in a constraint equation must be measured from a fixed datum.
See: Lesson 2Related: Position coordinate, Constraint
Deformation and restitution (phases of an impact)
During deformation the bodies squash together until they share one velocity, with impulse \(\int P\,dt\); during restitution they spring back apart, with impulse \(\int R\,dt\). Their ratio is the coefficient of restitution.
See: Lesson 8Related: Coefficient of restitution \(e\), Impulsive force
Degrees of freedom
The number of coordinates that can be chosen freely: the number of coordinates minus the number of independent constraints, \(\text{DOF} = n - c\). Two blocks on one cord have one; three blocks on two cords have two.
See: Lesson 2Related: Constraint, Position coordinate
Direct central impact
An impact in which the velocities lie along the line of impact and the line passes through both centers of mass. Momentum and the coefficient of restitution give the two velocities after the impact.
See: Lesson 8Related: Line of impact, Coefficient of restitution \(e\), Oblique impact
Double Atwood machine
Three blocks on two cords: one block on a cord over a fixed pulley, balanced against a movable pulley that carries the other two on a second cord. It has two degrees of freedom; a light movable pulley needs \(T_1 = 2T_2\).
See: Lesson 2 Lesson 3Related: Degrees of freedom, Equations of motion for a system

E

Back to top
Elastic impact
An impact in which the bodies separate, with \(0 \lt e \le 1\). Momentum is conserved across it; kinetic energy is not, unless \(e = 1\).
See: Lesson 8Related: Coefficient of restitution \(e\), Perfectly elastic impact, Plastic impact
Energy lost in an impact
The kinetic energy before minus after, \(T_1 - T_2\). Only the energy of the motion relative to the center of mass can be lost; a plastic impact loses all of it.
See: Lesson 7 Lesson 8Related: Plastic impact, Kinetic energy relative to the center of mass, Coefficient of restitution \(e\)
Equations of motion for a system
Newton's second law applied to each particle of a system, \(\Fvec_i + \sum_j \Fvec_{ij} = m_i\avec_i\), with the constraint equations, and a count of unknowns against equations before solving.
See: Lesson 3Related: Free-body diagram (FBD), Kinetic diagram, Constraint
External force
A force on a particle of the system from a body outside it: gravity, the floor, a support, a hand. Only external forces appear in \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\) and in the impulse of a system.
See: Lesson 3 Lesson 4Related: Internal force, Motion of the center of mass \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\)

F

Back to top
Free-body diagram (FBD)
A sketch of one particle, or one part of a system, isolated from everything else, with every force on it. For a system, draw one FBD per particle, with each internal force on both particles it joins.
See: Lesson 3Related: Kinetic diagram, Equations of motion for a system, Internal force

I

Back to top
Impulsive force
A very large force that acts for a very short time and gives a finite impulse: a contact force in a collision, a cord that snaps taut, a rigid stop. Keep impulsive forces in an impact; drop the others.
See: Lesson 7Related: Nonimpulsive force, Impulsive motion
Impulsive motion
Motion during an impact, while impulsive forces act. The positions do not change, the velocities change abruptly, and only impulsive forces enter the impulse-momentum equations.
See: Lesson 7Related: Impulsive force, Nonimpulsive force, Plastic impact
Internal force
A force between two particles of the same system, such as the tension of a cord that joins them or the push between a block and a wedge. Internal forces come in equal and opposite pairs, \(\Fvec_{ji} = -\Fvec_{ij}\), so they cancel in \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\) and in the impulse, but not always in the work.
See: Lesson 3Related: External force, Third law, Work of internal forces

K

Back to top
Kinetic diagram
The companion of the free-body diagram: the particle with its \(m\avec\), drawn in terms of the accelerations chosen in the kinematics. FBD = kinetic diagram gives the equations of motion. (Pytel and Kiusalaas call it the mass-acceleration diagram, MAD.)
See: Lesson 3Related: Free-body diagram (FBD), Equations of motion for a system
Kinetic energy of a system \(T\)
The sum over its particles, \(T = \sum\tfrac12 m_i v_i^2\). It splits into the energy of the center of mass and the energy of the motion relative to it.
See: Lesson 5 Lesson 5Related: Kinetic energy relative to the center of mass, Principle of work and energy \(\sum T_1 + \sum U_{1-2} = \sum T_2\)
Kinetic energy relative to the center of mass
In \(T = \tfrac12 mv_G^2 + \sum\tfrac12 m_i v_{i/G}^2\), the second part. Internal forces, friction and collisions can change only this part; the first is fixed by \(\vvec_G\).
See: Lesson 5Related: Kinetic energy of a system \(T\), Center of mass \(G\), Energy lost in an impact

L

Back to top
Line of impact
The common normal to the surfaces of two colliding bodies at the point of contact, the axis \(n\). Smooth bodies push each other only along it; the tangent \(t\) is perpendicular to it.
See: Lesson 8 Lesson 8Related: Direct central impact, Oblique impact
Linear impulse \(\Ivec = \int\Fvec\,dt\)
The time integral of a force, \(\Ivec = \int_{t_1}^{t_2}\Fvec\,dt\). Units: \(\text{N·s}\). The impulse of all the external forces on a system, \(\sum\int\Fvec\,dt\), equals the change of its momentum.
See: Lesson 6Related: Linear momentum \(\Lvec\), Principle of impulse and momentum
Linear momentum \(\Lvec\)
Mass times velocity: \(\Lvec = m\vvec\) for a particle, and \(\Lvec = \sum m_i\vvec_i = m\vvec_G\) for a system. Units: \(\text{kg·m/s}\).
See: Lesson 6Related: Linear impulse \(\Ivec = \int\Fvec\,dt\), Conservation of linear momentum, Center of mass \(G\)

M

Back to top
Motion of the center of mass \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\)
The sum of the equations of motion of all the particles: the internal forces cancel, and the center of mass moves like a single particle of mass \(m\) acted on by all the external forces.
See: Lesson 4Related: Center of mass \(G\), External force, Internal force

N

Back to top
Nonimpulsive force
A force that stays finite during an impact: a weight, a spring, ordinary friction. Its impulse over the brief impact is negligible, so it is left out of the impact equations.
See: Lesson 7Related: Impulsive force, Impulsive motion

O

Back to top
Oblique impact
An impact in which the velocities do not lie along the line of impact. For smooth bodies each body keeps its \(t\) component; along \(n\) it is a direct central impact.
See: Lesson 8Related: Line of impact, Direct central impact

P

Back to top
Perfectly elastic impact
An impact with \(e = 1\): the bodies separate as fast as they approached, and no kinetic energy is lost. Equal masses then exchange their velocities in a head-on impact.
See: Lesson 8Related: Coefficient of restitution \(e\), Elastic impact
Plastic impact
An impact in which the bodies do not separate, \(e = 0\): they move off with one common velocity, \(m_A(\vvec_A)_1 + m_B(\vvec_B)_1 = (m_A + m_B)\vvec_2\), and all the energy of their relative motion is lost.
See: Lesson 7Related: Coefficient of restitution \(e\), Energy lost in an impact, Ballistic pendulum
Position coordinate
The distance \(s\) of a body from a fixed datum, measured along its own line of motion. Cord lengths are written in position coordinates; parts of fixed length add only a constant.
See: Lesson 2Related: Datum, Constraint
Principle of impulse and momentum (for a system)
\(\Lvec_1 + \sum\int\Fvec\,dt = \Lvec_2\): the impulse of the external forces equals the change of the system's momentum. Internal impulses cancel in pairs.
See: Lesson 6Related: Linear impulse \(\Ivec = \int\Fvec\,dt\), Linear momentum \(\Lvec\), Conservation of linear momentum
Principle of work and energy \(\sum T_1 + \sum U_{1-2} = \sum T_2\) (for a system)
The work of all the forces on all the particles, external and internal, equals the change of the system's kinetic energy.
See: Lesson 5Related: Work of internal forces, Kinetic energy of a system \(T\), Conservation of mechanical energy

R

Back to top
Relative acceleration \(\avec_{B/A}\)
The acceleration of \(B\) seen from axes that translate with \(A\): \(\avec_B = \avec_A + \avec_{B/A}\). For a block sliding on a moving wedge, \(\avec_{B/W}\) lies along the face.
See: Lesson 1Related: Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\), Translating frame
Relative position \(\rvec_{B/A}\)
The vector from particle \(A\) to particle \(B\): \(\rvec_B = \rvec_A + \rvec_{B/A}\). Read "\(B\) relative to \(A\)".
See: Lesson 1Related: Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\), Closest approach
Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\)
The velocity of \(B\) seen from axes that translate with \(A\): \(\vvec_{B/A} = \vvec_B - \vvec_A\), and \(\vvec_{A/B} = -\vvec_{B/A}\). A boat's velocity relative to the water and an aircraft's airspeed are relative velocities.
See: Lesson 1 Lesson 1Related: Relative position \(\rvec_{B/A}\), Velocity triangle, Translating frame
Rigid link
A light bar that keeps two particles a fixed distance apart. Its constraint, such as \(x_A^2 + y_B^2 = L^2\), is not linear in the coordinates, so the speed ratio changes with position; \(\vvec_{B/A}\) is perpendicular to the link.
See: Lesson 2Related: Constraint, Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\)

S

Back to top
System of particles
Any group of particles chosen to be studied together. Drawing its boundary decides which forces are internal and which are external.
See: Lesson 1 Lesson 3Related: Internal force, External force, Center of mass \(G\)

T

Back to top
Third law
Two particles exert equal, opposite and collinear forces on each other. It is why internal forces cancel in \(\sum\Fvec = m\avec_G\) and in the impulse of a system.
See: Lesson 3Related: Internal force
Translating frame
A set of axes that moves with a particle but does not turn. In it, velocities and accelerations simply subtract: \(\vvec_{B/A} = \vvec_B - \vvec_A\), \(\avec_{B/A} = \avec_B - \avec_A\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\), Absolute motion

V

Back to top
Velocity triangle
The vector relation \(\vvec_B = \vvec_A + \vvec_{B/A}\) drawn head to tail. Any two of the three vectors give the third, graphically or by components.
See: Lesson 1Related: Relative velocity \(\vvec_{B/A}\)

W

Back to top
Work of internal forces
An internal pair does work \(dU = \Fvec_{ij}\cdot d\rvec_{i/j}\), set by the relative motion of the two particles: zero for an inextensible cord or a rigid link, \(-\Delta V_e\) for a spring, \(-F_f d\) for kinetic friction between sliding parts.
See: Lesson 5 Lesson 5Related: Internal force, Principle of work and energy \(\sum T_1 + \sum U_{1-2} = \sum T_2\)
