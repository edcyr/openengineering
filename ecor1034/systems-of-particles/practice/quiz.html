How it works

  • Work on your own with a calculator and scrap paper. Try it without notes first: that shows you what you really know.
  • Nothing is marked until you press Submit quiz. Until then you can move between questions and change any answer.
  • Each question is either right or wrong. When a question has several boxes, every box must be right.
  • After you submit, you see your score, a review of every question with a worked solution, and a results sheet you can print or save as a PDF.
  • Use \(g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\). Give each answer in the unit shown next to its box: meters per second, meters per second squared, newtons, joules, kilojoules, meters, seconds or degrees.

Each box shows how it read your entry (for example = 2.178), so you can check it before you submit. Type only the number or expression; the unit is shown next to the box.

How close is close enough?

Numbers are accepted within \(1\%\) of the exact value, so three or four significant figures are plenty. Typing the whole expression, such as (12*3+8*1)/20, is always safe: nothing is rounded along the way.

Two slips cost the most marks: using energy across an impact (use momentum across it, energy before and after), and adding momenta with a velocity measured relative to a moving body. Convert relative velocities to velocities over the ground first.
