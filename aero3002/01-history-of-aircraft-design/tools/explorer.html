Design Trends Explorer

Every aircraft in this module in one place: the Wright Flyer and the 90 aircraft of Laurence Loftin's Quest for Performance (NASA SP-468). Plot any parameter against any other, filter by era and role, fit a trend line as a designer would, compare planforms to scale, and download the data for your own analysis.

About the data

  • Source. Laurence K. Loftin Jr., Quest for Performance: The Evolution of Modern Aircraft, NASA SP-468 (1985), Appendix A, Tables I–III (propeller aircraft, 1914–1980), V (jet fighters) and VII (jet transports). The values were transcribed from the published tables and converted from US units; the original US values are kept in the CSV file.
  • The Wright Flyer (1903) is added from the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum (span, length, weights, 12 hp) and NASA Glenn Research Center (wing area 510 ft²).
  • Years are the first flight of the type. Loftin gives them for the jets; for the propeller aircraft of Tables I–III they come from general references and may differ by a year for a particular variant.
  • \(\CDz\) and \(\LDmax\) are Loftin's estimates from published performance (his Appendix C), with \(e = 0.70\) to \(0.75\). For jet transports he gave no \(\CDz\), and his \(\LDmax\) estimates come from his text (707-320B 19 to 19.5, DC-8-63 17.9, 747-200B and 767-200 about 18, L-1011 and DC-10 17 to 17.5).
  • Wing loading and thrust-to-weight ratio are computed here from the gross weight; for the jet fighters, \(\TW\) uses the maximum (afterburning) thrust. Variable-sweep aircraft are listed with their wings spread.
  • Range for jet transports is Loftin's maximum-payload range with no fuel reserve.

Ideas for your own analysis

Fit \(\We/\Wo = A\,\Wo^{\,C}\) separately for jet fighters, jet transports and general-aviation aircraft, and compare with Raymer's Table 3.1. Plot \(\TW\) against \(\WS\) for the jets: do fighters and transports form separate clusters? Add a modern aircraft from its published data and see whether it continues Loftin's trends.
