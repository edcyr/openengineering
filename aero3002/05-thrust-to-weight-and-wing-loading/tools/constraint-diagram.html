How it is calculated

Jets

  • Every \(\TW\) refers to the takeoff weight and the total sea-level static thrust; every \(\WS\) is the takeoff mass over the reference area, in kg/m².
  • Landing (Lesson 3): \(\WSo \le (s_L - S_a)\,\sigma\,C_{L_{\max},L}/(4.996\,k_r\,W_L/\Wo)\), with \(k_r = 0.66\) for thrust reversers. Stall: \(\WSo \le \rho V_s^2\CLmax/(2g)\) divided by the weight fraction.
  • Takeoff (Lesson 4): \(\TWo \ge k\,\WSo/(s_{TO}\,\sigma\,C_{L_{\max},TO})\), with \(k = 2.34\) for jet transports and business jets [10]; other aircraft may need their own constant.
  • Cruise, ceiling and sustained turn (Lessons 5 and 6): the master equation, \(\TWo = (\beta/\alpha)[q\CDz/(\beta g\,\WSo) + n^2K\beta g\,\WSo/q + (\dd h/\dd t)/V]\), with \(K = 1/(\pi Ae)\), the standard atmosphere and \(\alpha = \sigma\) unless you enter a thrust lapse.
  • Engine out (Lesson 6): \(\TWo \ge [N/(N-1)](1/(L/D) + G)\). Instantaneous turn: \(\WSo \le q\CLmax/(ng\beta)\).

Propeller aircraft

  • Every \(\PW\) is the total sea-level takeoff shaft power over the takeoff mass, in kW/kg. Large turboprops certified to the transport rules follow the preliminary sizing method for large propeller aircraft of [12], with its statistical factors for turboprop transports.
  • Speeds: the approach speed follows from the landing field length, \(V_{APP} = k_{APP}\sqrt{s_{LFL}}\) with \(k_{APP} = 1.64\ \text{m}^{1/2}/\text{s}\); then \(V_{S,L} = V_{APP}/1.3\), \(V_{S,TO} = V_{S,L}\sqrt{C_{L_{\max},L}/C_{L_{\max},TO}}\), \(V_2 = 1.2\,V_{S,TO}\), and the average takeoff speed \(\bar V = V_2/\sqrt2\).
  • Landing: \(\WSo \le k_L\,\sigma\,C_{L_{\max},L}\,s_{LFL}/(W_L/\Wo)\), with \(k_L = 0.137\ \text{kg/m}^3\) and \(s_{LFL}\) the landing field length.
  • Takeoff (transport rules): \(\PW \ge k_{TO}\,\WSo\,g\,\bar V/(1000\,s_{TOFL}\,\sigma\,C_{L_{\max},TO}\,\eta_{TO})\), with \(k_{TO} = 2.25\ \text{m}^3/\text{kg}\): a straight line through the origin. Light aircraft (Lesson 4): the ground roll \(s_G = 0.503\,\text{TOP}_P + 3.11 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{TOP}_P^{\,2}\) solved for \(\text{TOP}_P\), then \(\PW \ge \WSo/(\sigma\,C_{L_{\max},TO}\,\text{TOP}_P)\).
  • Engine out: \(\PW \ge [N/(N-1)](1/(L/D) + G)\,g\,V_2/(1000\,\eta)\). Missed approach: the same at \(V_{APP}\) with the landing \(L/D\), times \(W_L/\Wo\); minimum gradients 2.1%, 2.4% and 2.7% for two, three and four engines [8], §25.121(d).
  • Cruise, ceiling and sustained turn: the master equation in power, \(\PW = (\beta/\alpha_P)[\ldots]\,gV/(1000\,\eta)\) (Lesson 2's \(P = TV/\eta_p\)), with \(\alpha_P = \sigma\) unless you enter a power lapse.

Design point and presets

  • Design point (Lesson 7): the lowest point of the envelope at or below the smallest wing loading limit, at the highest wing loading that reaches it. Wing area \(S = \Wo/\WSo\); thrust \(T_0 = \TWo\,\Wo\,g\) or power \(P_0 = (P/W)_0\,\Wo\) (Lesson 8).
  • Dash 8-400: maximum takeoff and landing weights, takeoff and landing field lengths, maximum cruise speed and altitude, and engine ratings from De Havilland Canada's specification sheet [14]; wing area 63.1 m² and span 28.42 m from published operator data. Estimated with the statistics of [12] and its ATR 72 example: the drag polar (\(\LDmax = 11.22\sqrt{A/(S_{wet}/S)} = 16.1\) with \(S_{wet}/S = 6.2\) and \(e = 0.85\)), \(C_{L_{\max},L} = 2.6\), \(C_{L_{\max},TO} = 2.1\), the climb \(L/D\) values and the propeller efficiencies (0.64 takeoff, 0.73 climb, 0.86 cruise). The cruise power lapse is the maximum cruise power over the maximum takeoff power, 3947/5071 = 0.78, with the flat-rated engines holding their cruise rating at 7620 m; the cruise weight fraction is 0.95.
  • The business jet preset is the design you follow through the lessons and the Dash 8-400 is drawn from published data; the other four presets are illustrative designs, not real types. Your inputs are saved in this browser.
