A

Active constraint
A requirement that the design point meets exactly, so it sizes the aircraft. The business jet's are the landing and the ceiling; the others are met with margin.
See: Lesson 7Related: Design point, Envelope

B

Back to top
Balanced field length
The runway length at which an engine failure at the decision speed needs the same distance whether the pilot stops or continues the takeoff. A transport's takeoff field length is at least this.
See: Lesson 4Related: Takeoff field length \(s_{TO}\), Engine-out climb
Best loiter wing loading
The wing loading for the longest endurance: \((q/g)\sqrt{\CDz/K}\) for a jet, at \(\LDmax\), and \((q/g)\sqrt{3\CDz/K}\) for a propeller aircraft.
See: Lesson 5Related: Best-range wing loading
Best-range wing loading
The wing loading that puts the start of a cruise at the best lift coefficient for range: \((q/g)\sqrt{\CDz/(3K)}\) for a jet, \((q/g)\sqrt{\CDz/K}\) for a propeller aircraft. Divide by \(\beta\) for the takeoff value.
See: Lesson 5Related: Best loiter wing loading, Cruise constraint

C

Back to top
Ceiling
The altitude where the rate of climb falls to a set value: 0 for the absolute ceiling, 100 ft/min (0.51 m/s) for the service ceiling, 300 ft/min (1.52 m/s) for the cruise ceiling.
See: Lesson 6Related: Master equation, Climb gradient \(G\)
Climb gradient \(G\)
The height gained per distance flown, \(G = (\dd h/\dd t)/V\), the sine of the climb angle. Transports need 2.4%, 2.7% or 3.0% with one engine failed [8].
See: Lesson 6, Lesson 6Related: Engine-out climb, Master equation
Constraint diagram
A chart of \(\TW\) against \(\WS\) on which every requirement is drawn as a curve or a limit, leaving the feasible region and the design point.
See: Lesson 7Related: Feasible region, Design point
Cruise constraint
The takeoff \(\TW\) a level cruise needs at each wing loading: \((\beta/\alpha)[q\CDz/(\beta g\WSo) + K\beta g\WSo/q]\), a U-shaped curve.
See: Lesson 5Related: Master equation, Best-range wing loading

D

Back to top
Density ratio \(\sigma\)
The air density as a fraction of the sea-level standard value, \(\rho/1.225\). It falls with altitude and with temperature, and enters every field-length and flight requirement.
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 4Related: Thrust lapse \(\alpha\), Hot and high
Design point
The chosen pair of \(\WS\) and \(\TW\): the lowest thrust-to-weight ratio in the feasible region, at the highest wing loading that gives it. Times \(\Wo\), it gives the wing area and the thrust.
See: Lesson 7, Lesson 8Related: Feasible region, Active constraint
Dynamic pressure \(q\)
\(q = \tfrac12\rho V^2 = \tfrac12\gamma pM^2 = 0.7pM^2\). In level flight the lift coefficient is \(C_L = (W/S)\,g/q\) with \(W/S\) in kg/m².
See: Lesson 5Related: Cruise constraint, Master equation

E

Back to top
Engine-out climb
The climb a multi-engine aircraft must hold after an engine fails at takeoff. It needs \(\TWo = [N/(N-1)](1/(L/D) + G)\), a horizontal line on the constraint diagram.
See: Lesson 6Related: Second segment, Climb gradient \(G\)
Envelope
The highest of the \(\TW\) constraints at each wing loading: the lower boundary of the feasible region.
See: Lesson 7Related: Feasible region, Constraint diagram

F

Back to top
Feasible region
The part of the constraint diagram above every \(\TW\) constraint and left of every wing loading limit: the designs that meet every requirement.
See: Lesson 7Related: Envelope, Design point
Flat rating
An engine rating held constant up to a set outside temperature, so the takeoff thrust does not fall on warm days until that temperature is passed.
See: Lesson 4Related: Hot and high, Thrust lapse \(\alpha\)

H

Back to top
High-lift devices
Flaps on the trailing edge and slats on the leading edge that raise \(\CLmax\) for takeoff and landing, allowing a smaller wing for the same field lengths.
See: Lesson 3Related: Maximum lift coefficient \(\CLmax\)
Hot and high
An airport at altitude on a hot day, where the low density lengthens the takeoff and landing and the engines may give less thrust.
See: Lesson 4Related: Density ratio \(\sigma\), Flat rating

I

Back to top
Instantaneous turn
The tightest turn the wing can pull for a moment, trading speed for it, limited only by \(\CLmax\): \(\WSo \le q\CLmax/(ng\beta)\).
See: Lesson 6Related: Sustained turn, Load factor \(n\)

L

Back to top
Landing distance \(s_L\)
The distance from 50 ft above the threshold to a stop: \(s_L = 4.996(W/S)_L/(\sigma C_{L_{\max},L}) + S_a\) in m, with \((W/S)_L\) in kg/m².
See: Lesson 3Related: Obstacle allowance \(S_a\), Landing field length
Landing field length
The runway a transport needs to land: the landing distance divided by 0.6, because it must stop within 60% of the runway [9].
See: Lesson 3Related: Landing distance \(s_L\)
Load factor \(n\)
The lift as a multiple of the weight, \(n = L/W\). A turn needs \(n > 1\), which multiplies the induced drag by \(n^2\).
See: Lesson 6Related: Sustained turn, Instantaneous turn

M

Back to top
Master equation
The \(\TW\) a flight condition needs at each wing loading: \((\beta/\alpha)[q\CDz/(\beta g\WSo) + n^2K\beta g\WSo/q + (\dd h/\dd t)/V]\). It covers cruise, climb, ceiling and sustained turns.
See: Lesson 6Related: Specific excess power \(P_s\), Cruise constraint
Maximum lift coefficient \(\CLmax\)
The largest lift coefficient the wing reaches before it stalls. Flaps and slats raise it: landing flaps the most, takeoff flaps about 80% as much.
See: Lesson 3Related: Stall speed \(V_s\), High-lift devices

O

Back to top
Obstacle allowance \(S_a\)
The part of the landing distance spent clearing the 50 ft obstacle and flaring: about 305 m for airliners, 183 m for light aircraft and 137 m for short-takeoff-and-landing aircraft [1].
See: Lesson 3Related: Landing distance \(s_L\)

P

Back to top
Power lapse \(\alpha_P\)
The power available as a fraction of the sea-level power: about \(\sigma\) for a turboprop, \(\sigma - (1 - \sigma)/7.55\) for a piston engine without a supercharger.
See: Lesson 2Related: Thrust lapse \(\alpha\), Power loading \(P/W\)
Power loading \(P/W\)
The installed sea-level power per unit of takeoff mass, in kW/kg; the propeller aircraft's counterpart of \(\TW\).
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 2Related: Thrust-to-weight ratio \(T/W\), Propeller efficiency \(\eta_p\)
Propeller efficiency \(\eta_p\)
The fraction of the shaft power a propeller turns into thrust power, \(TV = \eta_pP\); about 0.8 to 0.85 in cruise.
See: Lesson 2Related: Power loading \(P/W\)

R

Back to top
Reference landing speed \(V_{\text{REF}}\)
The speed at 50 ft on approach, \(V_{\text{REF}} = 1.23\,V_{SR}\) at the landing weight [8]. A limit on it limits the wing loading.
See: Lesson 3Related: Stall speed \(V_s\)
Rubber engine
An engine of a known type scaled up or down to give exactly the thrust the design point needs, used until a real engine is chosen.
See: Lesson 8Related: Design point

S

Back to top
Sea-level static thrust \(T_0\)
The total thrust of all the engines at sea level, standing still, on a standard day: the thrust every \(\TW\) in these lessons refers to.
See: Lesson 1Related: Thrust-to-weight ratio \(T/W\), Thrust lapse \(\alpha\)
Second segment
The part of the takeoff climb with the landing gear up and takeoff flaps, at the takeoff safety speed; usually the hardest climb with one engine failed.
See: Lesson 6Related: Engine-out climb, Climb gradient \(G\)
Specific excess power \(P_s\)
\(P_s = V(T - D)/W\): the rate at which the aircraft can gain energy height, by climbing or accelerating. The master equation is built on it.
See: Lesson 6Related: Master equation
Stall speed \(V_s\)
The slowest steady flight speed, where the wing reaches \(\CLmax\): \(W/S = \rho V_s^2\CLmax/(2g)\). Transports use the reference stall speed \(V_{SR}\).
See: Lesson 3Related: Reference landing speed \(V_{\text{REF}}\), Maximum lift coefficient \(\CLmax\)
Statistical fit
An equation fitted to aircraft of one class, such as \(\TWo = aM_{\max}^{\,C}\) or \((P/W)_0 = aV_{\max}^{\,C}\), used for a first estimate before any requirement is analyzed.
See: Lesson 2Related: Thrust-to-weight ratio \(T/W\), Power loading \(P/W\)
Sustained turn
A turn held without losing speed or height: the master equation with \(n > 1\) and no climb.
See: Lesson 6Related: Instantaneous turn, Load factor \(n\)

T

Back to top
Takeoff field length \(s_{TO}\)
The runway a takeoff needs, including the climb over a 35 ft obstacle (50 ft for light aircraft) and the margins for an engine failure. For jets, \(s_{TO} \approx 2.34\,\text{TOP}\) m [10].
See: Lesson 4, Lesson 4Related: Takeoff parameter (TOP), Balanced field length
Takeoff parameter (TOP)
\(\text{TOP} = (W/S)/(\sigma C_{L_{\max},TO}\,T/W)\): the group of variables the takeoff distance grows with. The distance per unit of TOP is a statistical fit to the field lengths of similar aircraft, about 2.34 m per kg/m² for jet transports [10]. Light propeller aircraft use the power loading in place of \(T/W\), and a curve fitted to their ground rolls.
See: Lesson 4, Lesson 4, Lesson 4Related: Takeoff field length \(s_{TO}\), Statistical fit
Thrust lapse \(\alpha\)
The thrust available in flight as a fraction of the sea-level static thrust, \(\alpha = T/T_0\). The lessons take \(\alpha = \sigma\); high-bypass turbofans also lose thrust as their speed rises.
See: Lesson 2Related: Density ratio \(\sigma\), Sea-level static thrust \(T_0\)
Thrust matching
Setting the thrust equal to the drag in cruise: \((T/W)_{\text{cruise}} = 1/(L/D)\), or \(\TWo = (\beta/\alpha)/(L/D)\) at takeoff conditions.
See: Lesson 2Related: Thrust lapse \(\alpha\), Weight fraction \(\beta\)
Thrust reverser
A device that turns the engine's thrust forward after touchdown. It cuts the ground roll by about a third, but certification field lengths usually do not count it.
See: Lesson 3Related: Landing distance \(s_L\)
Thrust-to-weight ratio \(T/W\)
The total sea-level static thrust over the takeoff weight, \(T_0/(\Wo g)\). It sets the takeoff, climb, ceiling and maneuver performance.
See: Lesson 1Related: Wing loading \(W/S\), Sea-level static thrust \(T_0\)

W

Back to top
Weight fraction \(\beta\)
The weight at a flight condition as a fraction of the takeoff weight, \(\beta = W/\Wo\), from the mission segment weight fractions of Module 3.
See: Lesson 2Related: Thrust lapse \(\alpha\), Master equation
What-if study
Changing one requirement or technology on the constraint diagram to see how far the design point moves, and what the change is worth.
See: Lesson 7Related: Constraint diagram
Wing loading \(W/S\)
The takeoff mass over the reference wing area, in kg/m² (multiply by \(g\) for N/m², divide by 4.882 for lb/ft²). It sets the stall speed, the field lengths, the cruise lift coefficient and the ride.
See: Lesson 1Related: Thrust-to-weight ratio \(T/W\), Design point
