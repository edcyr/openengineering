Form Factors and Interference

A wing is not a flat plate. Its thickness speeds the air up over its surface, which raises the friction, and the boundary layer leaves a wake behind it, which adds pressure drag. A form factor scales the flat-plate friction up for these effects, and an interference factor adds what the components do to each other where they meet. This lesson estimates both for the business jet.

Learning objectives

  • Explain what a form factor accounts for, and how it depends on thickness, fineness, Mach number and sweep.
  • Compute the form factors of a wing or tail, a fuselage and a nacelle.
  • Choose interference factors for the components of a layout.
  • Judge the spread between published form-factor methods.

Wings and tails

Over a thick airfoil the air accelerates: the local velocities, and the friction with them, are higher than the free stream's, and the pressure recovery toward the trailing edge thickens the boundary layer into a wake. The form factor grows with the thickness ratio, faster when the maximum thickness is far forward, and with the Mach number, which strengthens the supervelocities; sweep weakens them.

Form factor of a wing, tail, strut or pylon

\[ FF = \left[1 + \frac{0.6}{(x/c)_m}\frac tc + 100\left(\frac tc\right)^4\right]\left[1.34\,M^{0.18}\cos^{0.28}\Lambda_m\right] \]

\((x/c)_m\) is the chordwise position of the maximum thickness (about 0.3 for older airfoils, 0.4 to 0.5 for laminar-flow and supercritical ones) and \(\Lambda_m\) the sweep of the line of maximum thickness.

Example 4.1 — The business jet's wing

The business jet's wing is 12% thick with its maximum thickness at 30% of the chord, swept \(25^\circ\) at the quarter chord (\(A = 8\), \(\lambda = 0.35\)). Find the sweep of the 30% line and the form factor at Mach 0.80.

Show solution
\[ \tan\Lambda_m = \tan 25^\circ - \frac{4}{8}(0.30 - 0.25)\frac{1 - 0.35}{1 + 0.35} = 0.4543, \qquad \Lambda_m = 24.4^\circ \] \[ FF = [1 + 2(0.12) + 100(0.12)^4]\,[1.34(0.80)^{0.18}\cos^{0.28} 24.4^\circ] \] \[ FF = 1.261(1.254) = 1.581 \]

The wing's drag is 58% more than a flat plate's of the same area and length. The tails, 10% thick, have form factors of 1.50 (horizontal) and 1.48 (vertical).

The methods disagree by tens of percent at high subsonic Mach numbers, because each was fitted to different data. A designer picks one, applies it consistently, and calibrates it against a similar aircraft whose drag is known. This module uses Raymer's throughout.

Fuselages and nacelles

A body's form factor depends on its fineness ratio \(f = l/d\) (for a non-circular body, \(d\) is the diameter of a circle of the same cross-sectional area). A short, fat body has a large wake; a long, thin one is almost a flat plate but has more wetted area for its volume.

Form factors of bodies

\[ FF_{\text{fuselage}} = 0.9 + \frac{5}{f^{1.5}} + \frac{f}{400}, \qquad FF_{\text{nacelle}} = 1 + \frac{0.35}{f} \]

Example 4.2 — The business jet's fuselage and nacelles

The fuselage is 15.5 m long and 1.597 m across; each nacelle 1.770 m long and 0.797 m across (Module 7). Find their form factors.

Show solution
\[ f = \frac{15.5}{1.597} = 9.71, \qquad FF = 0.9 + \frac{5}{9.71^{1.5}} + \frac{9.71}{400} = 1.090 \] \[ f = \frac{1.770}{0.797} = 2.22, \qquad FF = 1 + \frac{0.35}{2.22} = 1.158 \]

Interference

Where two components meet, the flow around each disturbs the other: the boundary layers merge in the corners, and the velocities add. An interference factor \(Q\) multiplies the drag of the component concerned.

Interference factors (source: Raymer, Ch. 12)
Component and installation\(Q\)

The business jet's low wing is well filleted into the fuselage (\(Q = 1.0\)), its conventional tails take \(Q = 1.04\), and its nacelles, whose pylons hold them 0.29 m from the fuselage, less than a diameter, take \(Q = 1.3\).

Key takeaways

  • The form factor scales a flat plate's friction for thickness: supervelocities and the wake.
  • Wings and tails: \(FF\) from \(t/c\), \((x/c)_m\), \(M\) and \(\Lambda_m\); bodies from the fineness ratio; nacelles \(1 + 0.35/f\).
  • Published methods differ by tens of percent; use one consistently and calibrate it.
  • Interference: 1.0 for a fuselage and a filleted wing, about 1.04 for conventional tails, 1.5 to 1.0 for nacelles as they move away from the surface.
  • The business jet: wing 1.581, tails 1.50 and 1.48, fuselage 1.090, nacelles 1.158 with \(Q = 1.3\).
