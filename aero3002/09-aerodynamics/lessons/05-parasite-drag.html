The Parasite Drag Build-Up

With each component's friction, form factor and interference in hand, the zero-lift drag is a sum, plus the drags that no component's formula catches: the upswept tail cone, blunt bases, the gaps and bumps of a real airframe. This lesson adds up the business jet's, and compares the result with the single number Module 3 assumed.

Learning objectives

  • Write the component build-up of the zero-lift drag coefficient.
  • Estimate the upsweep drag of an aft fuselage and allow for leakage and protuberances.
  • Build up an aircraft's \(C_{D0}\) and the equivalent skin-friction coefficient, and compare them with a first estimate.

The sum

The component build-up of the zero-lift drag

\[ C_{D0} = \frac{\sum_c C_{f,c}\,FF_c\,Q_c\,S_{\text{wet},c}}{\Sref} + C_{D,\text{misc}} + C_{D,\text{L\&P}} \]

The sum runs over the wing, the tails, the fuselage, the nacelles and any pods or struts; \(C_{D,\text{misc}}\) collects the drags of upsweep, bases, open gear and the like, and \(C_{D,\text{L\&P}}\) the leakage and protuberances.

The wetted areas are Module 6's (with Module 7's nacelles); the friction, form and interference factors are Lessons 3 and 4's.

Drags the components miss

  • Upsweep. The tail cone of most transports rises toward the tail, for rotation clearance (Module 8) and loading doors. The flow cannot follow it fully, and the resulting vortices and separation add drag that grows steeply with the upsweep angle \(u\) of the tail cone's centerline.
  • Bases. A blunt base, such as a cut-off tail cone or a fixed gear's wheel fairing, has a low pressure on it.
  • Leakage and protuberances. Air leaking through door and panel seals, antennas, lights, drains, steps and gaps: a few percent of the parasite drag on a clean jet transport, more on propeller aircraft and military aircraft. This module takes 5% for the business jet.

Upsweep drag

\[ \left(\frac Dq\right)_{\text{upsweep}} = 3.83\,u^{2.5}A_{\max} \]

\(u\) is the upsweep of the tail cone's centerline in radians and \(A_{\max}\) the fuselage's largest cross-sectional area; divide by \(\Sref\) for the coefficient.

Example 5.1 — The business jet's upsweep

The business jet's tail cone is 5.59 m long, and its centerline rises 0.727 m along it (Module 6's layout). The fuselage is 1.597 m across. Find the upsweep drag coefficient.

Show solution
\[ u = \arctan\frac{0.727}{5.59} = 7.41^\circ = 0.1293\ \text{rad}, \qquad A_{\max} = \frac{\pi(1.597)^2}{4} = 2.003\ \text{m}^2 \] \[ \left(\frac Dq\right) = 3.83(0.1293)^{2.5}(2.003) = 0.0461\ \text{m}^2 \] \[ C_{D,\text{upsweep}} = \frac{0.0461}{28} = 0.00165 \]

About as much as both nacelles: the upsweep that gives the tail-down angle of Module 8 has a price in cruise.

Example 5.2 — Adding it up

From the table, find the business jet's \(C_{D0}\) with the upsweep and 5% for leakage and protuberances. Compare it with Module 3's estimate \(C_{fe}\,\Swet/\Sref\) with \(C_{fe} = 0.0030\) and \(\Swet/\Sref = 5.119\) (Module 7), and find the equivalent skin-friction coefficient of the build-up.

Show solution
\[ \sum = 0.00778 + 0.00544 + 0.00189 + 0.00114 + 0.00137 = 0.01762 \] \[ C_{D0} = 1.05(0.01762 + 0.00165) = 0.0202 \]

Module 3's estimate was \(0.0030(5.119) = 0.0154\): the build-up is 32% higher. Its equivalent skin-friction coefficient is

\[ C_{fe} = \frac{C_{D0}}{\Swet/\Sref} = \frac{0.0202}{5.119} = 0.0040 \]

which is more typical of small aircraft than of the airliners whose average Module 3 used. The business jet's short components run at low Reynolds numbers (Lesson 3), its thick, high-subsonic wing has a large form factor, and its upswept tail cone and close-coupled nacelles add the rest.

Other aircraft

The same build-up for the three illustrative aircraft of Modules 6 and 7 shows how the first estimate fares across classes. The equivalent skin friction comes out between about 0.0034 and 0.0040: lower for the large airliner, whose long components run at high Reynolds numbers, higher for the small aircraft.

Key takeaways

  • \(C_{D0} = \sum C_f\,FF\,Q\,\Swet/\Sref + C_{D,\text{misc}} + C_{D,\text{L\&P}}\).
  • Upsweep: \(D/q = 3.83u^{2.5}A_{\max}\); leakage and protuberances add a few percent.
  • The business jet: \(C_{D0} = 0.0202\) in cruise, 32% above Module 3's 0.0154; the wing gives 38% of it, the fuselage 27%.
  • The equivalent skin friction of the build-up, 0.0040, is that of a small aircraft.
