How it is calculated

  • Airframe (Module 6): the fuselage, wing, tails and engines of the chosen layout; stations \(x\) aft of the nose, heights from the fuselage axis. The CG range is a share of the mean aerodynamic chord, an assumption until Module 11's weights.
  • Angles (Lesson 2): tail-down \(\theta_{TD}\), the lowest line from the main wheels' contact to the fuselage or nacelles behind them; tip-back \(\arctan[(x_m - x_{cg,a})/h_{cg}]\); lateral tip-over \(\psi = \arctan[h_{cg}/((x_{cg} - x_n)\sin\delta)]\), \(\tan\delta = y_m/B\), at both ends of the CG range; bank about the main wheels to the wing tip, nacelle or propeller.
  • Loads (Lesson 3): \(W_{m,\max} = W(x_{cg,a} - x_n)/B\) shared by the legs and wheels; \(W_{n,\max} = W(x_m - x_{cg,f})/B\); braking at 0.31 g adds \(0.31\,W h_{cg}/B\) to the nose.
  • Tires (Lesson 4): the Goodyear Aviation Data Book 2022 (251 tires). Main tires must carry the load per wheel (1.07 times the rating on a multiple-wheel axle), nose tires the static load and 1.5 times the rating in braking (CS/FAR 25.733); the rated speed must reach the value given, and the inflation for the load, \(p_{\text{rated}}W_w/W_{\text{rated}}\), must not exceed the highest inflation. Auto picks the smallest diameter that passes. The statistical size is \(A W_w^B\) (source: Raymer, Table 11.1).
  • Shock absorbers (Lesson 5): \(S = V_v^2/(2g\eta N_g) - (\eta_T/\eta)S_T + 25\ \text{mm}\), with the main tire's deflection \(S_T\) from the catalog; piston \(\sqrt{4L/(\pi P)}\) at 1800 psi, outside diameter 1.3 times the piston, length 2.5 times the stroke.
  • The guidelines (tail-down angle, tip-back at least 15°, nose share 5 to 20%, tip-over at most 63°, bank at least 5°, nacelle clearance about a quarter of the fan diameter, propeller clearance 7 in) are those of Lesson 2.
