Gear Loads and Wheels

Once the legs are placed, statics gives their loads: the main gear is most loaded at the aft CG, the nose gear at the forward CG, and braking shifts more weight onto the nose. These loads choose the tires of Lesson 4 and the shock absorbers of Lesson 5, and they decide how many wheels the aircraft needs to spare the runway.

Learning objectives

  • Compute the maximum static loads on the main and nose gear and the minimum nose load.
  • Add the nose load from braking at a given deceleration.
  • Choose the number of wheels from the load per wheel and the runway's strength.

Static loads

On the ground and at rest, the weight \(W\) at the CG is carried by the nose gear at \(x_n\) and the main gear at \(x_m\). Moments about each gear give the loads, and the CG range gives their extremes (Chapter 11 of [1]; [8]):

Static gear loads

\[ W_{m,\max} = W\,\frac{x_{cg,a} - x_n}{B} \] \[ W_{n,\max} = W\,\frac{x_m - x_{cg,f}}{B} \] \[ W_{n,\min} = W\,\frac{x_m - x_{cg,a}}{B} \]

\(W_{m,\max}\) is the total on the main gear, shared by its legs; \(B = x_m - x_n\) is the wheelbase. Use the maximum takeoff weight (strictly, the maximum ramp weight, a little higher because of the fuel burned while taxiing).

Example 3.1 — The business jet's static loads

The business jet weighs \(W_0 = 6943\ \text{kg}\). Its gear is at stations 1.80 m and 7.65 m, and its CG between 6.773 m and 7.177 m (Lesson 2). Find the maximum load on each main leg, and the maximum and minimum loads on the nose gear, in newtons and in pounds (1 lbf = 4.448 N).

Show solution
\[ W = 6943(9.81) = 68\,110\ \text{N}, \qquad B = 5.85\ \text{m} \] \[ W_{m,\max} = 68\,110\,\frac{7.177 - 1.80}{5.85} = 62\,600\ \text{N} \]

so each of the two main legs carries 31 300 N, or 7036 lb, 46% of the weight. For the nose gear:

\[ W_{n,\max} = 68\,110\,\frac{7.65 - 6.773}{5.85} = 10\,210\ \text{N}\ (2294\ \text{lb}) \] \[ W_{n,\min} = 68\,110\,\frac{7.65 - 7.177}{5.85} = 5510\ \text{N} \]

Braking

Braking decelerates the aircraft at \(a\), which needs a force \((a/g)W\) at the wheels, acting backward at ground level. The CG is \(h_{cg}\) above the ground, so the braking force pitches the aircraft nose-down and moves load from the main gear to the nose gear. Taking moments about the main wheels' contact ([1], Chapter 11; [7]):

The nose load while braking

\[ W_{n,\text{brake}} = W\,\frac{(x_m - x_{cg,f}) + (a/g)\,h_{cg}}{B} \]

CS/FAR 25.733 [3], [4] checks the nose tire with a deceleration of 0.31 g at the forward CG (and 0.20 g at the maximum ramp weight). In that condition the tire may carry up to 1.5 times its rated static load (Lesson 4).

Example 3.2 — The business jet's nose gear while braking

Find the business jet's nose load at the forward CG while braking at 0.31 g. The CG is 1.20 m above the ground.

Show solution
\[ W_{n,\text{brake}} = 68\,110\,\frac{(7.65 - 6.773) + 0.31(1.20)}{5.85} = 14\,530\ \text{N}\ (3267\ \text{lb}) \]

That is 21% of the weight, against 15% at rest: braking adds 4320 N, about 40% more than the static load.

How many wheels

A single wheel per leg is the lightest and simplest gear, but a tire's size grows with its load, and a heavy wheel load damages the runway. Airports grade their pavements' strength, and each aircraft's loading is graded on the same scale: the aircraft classification number (ACN) of an aircraft must not exceed the pavement classification number (PCN) of the runway it uses, or the airport must give permission. As a rough guide the ACN is about twice the load on a single wheel in tonnes, increased for wheels close enough together for their loads to overlap in the ground [2].

  • Light aircraft and business jets carry a few tonnes per wheel and use one wheel per leg.
  • Narrow-bodies such as the A320 and 737 carry about 20 t on each main wheel, on two wheels per leg; long-range wide-bodies carry about 30 t per wheel on four- or six-wheel bogies [2]. Two wheels per leg also let a transport land safely on a flat tire.
  • On a multiple-wheel axle, CS/FAR 25.733 [3], [4] lets each main tire carry up to 1.07 times its rating, because the loads never share out perfectly.

Example 3.3 — Wheels for an airliner and for the business jet

A 73.5 t narrow-body has two main legs, each carrying 46% of the weight. How many wheels does it need on each leg at about 20 t per wheel? How heavily is each business jet main wheel loaded?

Show solution
\[ 0.46(73.5) = 33.8\ \text{t per leg} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{33.8}{20} = 1.7 \]

so two wheels per leg, 16.9 t each. Each business jet main wheel carries 31 300 N, or 3.19 t: one wheel per leg is plenty, and its ACN of about 6 is far below that of any paved runway.

Key takeaways

  • The main gear's load is largest at the aft CG, the nose gear's at the forward CG; the nose's smallest load is at the aft CG.
  • Braking at \(a\) adds \((a/g)W\,h_{cg}/B\) to the nose load; the nose tire is checked at 0.31 g.
  • The business jet: 31 300 N (7036 lb) on each main leg; 10 210 N (2294 lb) on the nose at rest and 14 530 N (3267 lb) while braking.
  • The load per wheel and the runway's strength set the number of wheels: about 20 t per wheel on narrow-bodies, one wheel per leg on business jets.
