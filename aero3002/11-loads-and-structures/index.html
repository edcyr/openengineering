Interactive module Aerospace Design & Practice · Module 11

Loads and Structures

The business jet has a size, a layout and a drag polar; before its weight can be estimated part by part, its structure needs to know what it must carry. In this module you choose its materials and lay out its structure, find the loads it must be designed for, in maneuvers and in gusts, and size its wing box to carry them.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the module you can:

  • Compare aircraft materials by their specific properties and material indices, and explain where each is used. (Lesson 1)
  • Lay out a wing box and a fuselage structure, and trace the load paths between them. (Lesson 2)
  • Apply limit and ultimate loads, the factor of safety and the special factors, and compute margins of safety. (Lesson 3)
  • Find the limit load factors and design speeds, and draw the maneuvering envelope. (Lesson 4)
  • Compute gust load factors with Pratt's formula, draw the gust envelope and find which governs. (Lesson 5)
  • Find the wing and tail loads in balanced maneuvers across the flight envelope. (Lesson 6)
  • Compute the wing's shear and bending along its span, with the relief of its structure and fuel. (Lesson 7)
  • Size the wing box's panels and webs, compare materials, and state the design loads for the weight estimate. (Lesson 8)

How to use this module

Before you start: the business jet as Modules 3 to 10 left it: its sizing (3), wing and airfoil (4), wing loading (5), layout (6), fuel tanks (7), landing gear and cabin pressure (8) and maximum lift (10), and first-year statics and strength of materials. The course text is Raymer's Aircraft Design: A Conceptual Approach [1]; the lessons use its notation.

Work through the lessons in order. The times allow for working the examples on paper and doing the checks. A suggested plan in four sittings:

  1. Lessons 1–270 min

    Materials and structural layout.

  2. Lessons 3–460 min

    Design loads and margins of safety; the V-n diagram.

  3. Lessons 5–670 min

    Gust loads; balanced maneuver loads.

  4. Lessons 7–870 min

    Wing shear and bending; sizing the wing box.

  5. Practice Lab and Self-Check Quiz65 min

    About 30 min of practice, then the 35-minute quiz.

About 5½ h in total:270 min of lessons, 30 min of practice, 35 min for the quiz

Get the most out of it

  • Keep pencil and paper handy

    Try each worked example yourself before you open its solution, and keep track of units: speeds are equivalent airspeeds unless stated, stresses in MPa with forces in newtons, and loads are limit loads until you multiply by 1.5.

  • Do the checks

    Every lesson has “Check your understanding” questions with hints and full solutions. You can type answers like 1/cos(70.8*pi/180) or 0.81*sqrt(1.053e6*71.7e9/0.6)/1e6.

  • Practice until it is routine

    The Practice Lab generates unlimited problems, each with a worked solution.

  • Test yourself at the end

    The Self-Check Quiz gives a score, solutions and a results page you can print or save as a PDF.

  • Track your progress

    Tick “Mark this lesson complete” at the end of each lesson. A ✓ appears here and in Contents; it is saved in this browser only.

  • Keep the formulas close

    Print the Formula Sheet and look up terms in the Glossary. The Loads Envelope tool draws the envelopes of the business jet and three other aircraft at any weight and altitude, and sizes their wing boxes.
