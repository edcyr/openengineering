How it is calculated

  • Load factors (Lessons 3 to 5): \(n_{\text{limit}}\) from the design takeoff weight (14 CFR 25.337 [2]), \(-1\) to \(V_C\) and 0 at \(V_D\), unless the aircraft sets its own (the light aircraft: 3.8 and \(-1.52\)). Gusts by Pratt's formula with the derived gust velocities of 66, 50 and 25 ft/s at \(V_B\), \(V_C\) and \(V_D\) to 20 000 ft, falling to 38, 25 and 12.5 ft/s at 50 000 ft [3]; the wing's lift-curve slope at the flight Mach number (Module 4's formula, half-chord sweep).
  • Speeds (Lessons 4 and 5): \(V_{S1}\) and \(V_G\) at the maximum and minimum lift coefficients; \(V_A = V_{S1}\sqrt{n}\); \(V_C\) and \(V_D = V_C/0.8\) in equivalent airspeed, limited by \(M_C\) and \(M_D\) at altitude; \(V_B\) the least of 25.335(d), and the least \(V_C\) of 25.335(a) with the reference gust velocity of 25.341 [2].
  • Design load factor: the largest of the maneuvering limit and the gust load factors at the design takeoff weight, over altitudes from sea level to the aircraft's ceiling.
  • Balance (Lesson 6): wing lift at the wing's aerodynamic center with \(C_{M,ac} = c_{m0}A\cos^2\Lambda/(A + 2\cos\Lambda)\), tail load at the tail's; fuselage and thrust moments neglected.
  • Wing (Lessons 7 and 8): Schrenk's lift [4]; the wing's mass as a share of the takeoff weight, spread with the chord; wing-tank fuel up to its capacity (the rest in a center tank), spread with the chord squared to the end of the tanks; panels at ultimate load, the lower by strength, the upper by Farrar's buckling stress (\(F = 0.81\), a typical value); spars at 15% and 60%, mean depth \(0.9\,t\).
