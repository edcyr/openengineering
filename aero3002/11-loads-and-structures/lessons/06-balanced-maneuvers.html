Balanced Maneuver Loads

A load factor says how much lift the aircraft makes in all, not how that lift is shared. The wing makes most of it, but its lift acts at its aerodynamic center, not at the center of gravity, and it pitches the aircraft nose-down; the horizontal tail balances both. This lesson finds the wing and tail loads in a balanced maneuver at each corner of the business jet's envelopes.

Learning objectives

  • Write the equilibrium of an aircraft at a load factor, with the wing's lift and pitching moment and the tail load.
  • Find the tail load and the wing lift in a balanced maneuver.
  • Explain how the CG position and the speed change the tail load.
  • Find the largest balanced wing and tail loads over the business jet's flight envelope.

The aircraft in balance

14 CFR 25.331(b) [2] asks that the maneuvering conditions at the corners of the envelope be investigated "in equilibrium with zero pitching acceleration": a balanced maneuver, a steady pull-up or turn in which the aircraft does not rotate. Three things act in the aircraft's plane of symmetry:

  • the weight times the load factor, \(nW\), at the center of gravity \(x_{cg}\);
  • the wing's lift \(L_w\) at its aerodynamic center \(x_{ac}\), with its pitching moment about that point, \(M_{ac} = q S \bar c\,C_{M,ac}\), which does not change with the lift;
  • the horizontal tail's load \(L_t\) at its aerodynamic center, a tail arm \(l_t\) behind the wing's.

Taking forces up and moments nose-up as positive, and neglecting the fuselage's and the engines' pitching moments, which are small, equilibrium of forces and of moments about the wing's aerodynamic center give:

Wing and tail loads in a balanced maneuver

\[ L_t = \frac{nW\,(x_{cg} - x_{ac}) + M_{ac}}{l_t}, \qquad L_w = nW - L_t \]

\(x_{cg} - x_{ac}\) is positive when the CG is behind the wing's aerodynamic center; \(M_{ac}\) is negative (nose-down) for a cambered wing. A negative \(L_t\) is a download, which the wing must make up.

The business jet's wing has its aerodynamic center at the quarter of its MAC, station 6.975 m; its tail's is at 14.26 m, so \(l_t = 7.285\) m (Module 6). Its CG range, from Module 8, is 15 to 35% of the 2.015 m MAC: 0.202 m ahead of the aerodynamic center to 0.201 m behind it. Its supercritical airfoil pitches strongly nose-down: take \(c_{m,0} = -0.10\), a typical value, which for the wing's aspect ratio and sweep gives \(C_{M,ac} = c_{m,0}A\cos^2\Lambda/(A + 2\cos\Lambda) = -0.067\) (Module 13 uses the same value).

The tail load across the envelope

The tail load has two parts. The first, \(nW(x_{cg} - x_{ac})/l_t\), grows with the load factor and changes sign with the CG: a forward CG needs a download, an aft CG an upload. The second, \(M_{ac}/l_t\), is a download that grows with the dynamic pressure, so with the square of the speed, whatever the load factor. The largest download therefore comes at high speed with the CG forward, and the largest upload at high load factor and low speed with the CG aft.

Example 6.1 — Point A at the forward CG

Find the tail load and the wing lift at point A (\(n = 3.048\), \(V_A = 96.3\) m/s) at the takeoff weight of 6943 kg, with the CG at 15% of the MAC.

Show solution
\[ q = \tfrac12(1.225)(96.3)^2 = 5680\ \text{Pa} \] \[ M_{ac} = 5680(28)(2.0154)(-0.067) = -21.47\ \text{kN·m} \] \[ nW = 3.048(6943)(9.807) = 207.55\ \text{kN} \] \[ L_t = \frac{207.55(-0.2017) - 21.47}{7.285} = \frac{-41.86 - 21.47}{7.285} = -8.69\ \text{kN} \] \[ L_w = 207.55 + 8.69 = 216.2\ \text{kN} \]

The wing must lift 4.2% more than \(nW\), because the tail pushes down. At the aft CG the tail would instead lift 2.79 kN.

Example 6.2 — Point D at the forward CG

Repeat at point D: the same load factor at \(V_D = 173.6\) m/s.

Show solution
\[ q = \tfrac12(1.225)(173.6)^2 = 18\,460\ \text{Pa} \] \[ M_{ac} = -69.78\ \text{kN·m} \] \[ L_t = \frac{-41.86 - 69.78}{7.285} = -15.32\ \text{kN} \] \[ L_w = 222.9\ \text{kN} \]

The largest balanced download, 15.3 kN, at a tail lift coefficient of \(-15\,320/(18\,460 \times 5.42) = -0.153\): well within what the tail can make, so the tail does not limit this maneuver, but it is a load the tail and the rear fuselage must carry.

The wing's largest lift is in the upward gust at 20 000 ft with the CG forward, 233.9 kN; the tail's largest balanced download is at D, 15.3 kN. These are not yet the tail's design loads. 25.331(c) [2] adds pitching maneuvers, in which the pilot moves the elevator abruptly and the tail load leads the aircraft's response, and a gust also strikes the tail directly. Those dynamic cases usually size the horizontal tail; balanced maneuvers size the wing. The vertical tail is sized by yawing maneuvers and lateral gusts (25.351 and 25.341), and by the engine failure of Module 13.

Two results here depend on choices later modules revisit. The CG range will come from Module 12's weight statement, with the wing moved; and the tail arm and area will be set by Module 13's stability analysis. Both change these tail loads in proportion.

Key takeaways

  • In a balanced maneuver: \(L_t = [nW(x_{cg} - x_{ac}) + M_{ac}]/l_t\) and \(L_w = nW - L_t\).
  • The tail's download grows with the speed (from \(M_{ac}\)) and with the load factor at a forward CG; an aft CG at high load factor and low speed gives an upload.
  • The business jet: the wing's largest lift is 233.9 kN in the gust at 20 000 ft with the CG forward; the tail's largest balanced download is 15.3 kN at D.
  • Pitching maneuvers and direct gusts usually size the tail; balanced maneuvers size the wing.
