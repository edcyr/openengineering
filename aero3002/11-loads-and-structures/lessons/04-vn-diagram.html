The V-n Diagram

A pilot can pull a large load factor only at a high enough speed, and the structure must be designed for every combination of speed and load factor the aircraft can reach. The V-n diagram draws that region. This lesson finds the business jet's limit load factors and design speeds, and draws its maneuvering envelope at each weight and altitude.

Learning objectives

  • Find a transport aircraft's limit maneuvering load factors from its takeoff weight.
  • Find the design speeds \(V_{S1}\), \(V_A\), \(V_C\) and \(V_D\), in equivalent airspeed and Mach number.
  • Draw the maneuvering envelope and name its corners.
  • Explain how the envelope changes with weight and altitude.

The limit maneuvering load factors

For transport aircraft the positive limit maneuvering load factor depends only on the design maximum takeoff weight (14 CFR 25.337 [2]):

The limit maneuvering load factors

\[ n_{\text{limit}} = 2.1 + \frac{24\,000}{W + 10\,000}, \qquad 2.5 \le n_{\text{limit}} \le 3.8 \]

\(W\) is the design maximum takeoff weight in pounds. The negative limit is \(-1\) up to \(V_C\), rising linearly to 0 at \(V_D\). The same formula, without the lower bound, was the rule for light aircraft in Part 23 (normal category) until 2017 [3]; aerobatic aircraft take 6.0.

The business jet, at 6943 kg or 15 307 lb, has \(n_{\text{limit}} = 2.1 + 24\,000/25\,307 = 3.048\). Every transport above about 23 tonnes is designed for 2.5. The limit applies at every weight the aircraft flies at, from its lightest to its heaviest: a lighter aircraft reaches it more easily, so its lift, \(nW\), is smaller (25.321 [2]).

The design speeds

The envelope is drawn against the equivalent airspeed \(V_E = V\sqrt{\sigma}\), the speed at sea level that gives the same dynamic pressure. Air loads depend on the dynamic pressure, so in equivalent airspeed one diagram holds at every altitude, until the Mach number takes over. The design speeds of 14 CFR 25.335 [2] are:

  • \(V_{S1}\), the 1-g stall speed with the flaps up, and its negative counterpart at \(C_{L,\min}\).
  • \(V_A\), the design maneuvering speed, where the stall curve reaches the limit load factor: \(V_A = V_{S1}\sqrt{n_{\text{limit}}}\). Below it the wing stalls before it can overload the structure.
  • \(V_C\), the design cruising speed, chosen by the designer. It must be enough above the gust speed \(V_B\) to allow for being upset in turbulence (Lesson 5), and need not exceed the maximum level speed. Above the altitude where \(V_C\) reaches a chosen Mach number \(M_C\), \(M_C\) sets it.
  • \(V_D\), the design dive speed, the highest speed the structure is designed for: \(V_C/V_D \le 0.8\), or, where the Mach number limits it, \(M_D\) at least 0.07 above \(M_C\) (or 0.05 with a rational analysis).

The maximum operating speed \(V_{MO}/M_{MO}\) that pilots fly to is set a little below \(V_C/M_C\). The business jet's \(M_C = 0.80\), its cruise Mach number, and its \(V_C\) is 270 knots (138.9 m/s) equivalent, a typical value for business jets of its size, which Lesson 5 checks against the rule. Then \(V_D = 270/0.8 = 337.5\) knots and \(M_D = 0.87\).

Example 4.1 — Where the Mach number takes over

At what altitude does the business jet's \(V_C\) of 138.9 m/s equivalent reach \(M_C = 0.80\)? Find its \(V_C\) and \(V_D\) at 12 km (\(a = 295.1\) m/s, \(\sigma = 0.2537\)).

Show solution

\(V_E = M a\sqrt{\sigma}\). At 10 km, \(a = 299.5\) m/s and \(\sigma = 0.3369\):

\[ 0.80(299.5)\sqrt{0.3369} = 139.1\ \text{m/s} \]

so the crossover is at about 10.0 km (32 900 ft). At 12 km:

\[ V_C = 0.80(295.1)(0.5037) = 118.9\ \text{m/s} \] \[ V_D = 0.87(295.1)(0.5037) = 129.3\ \text{m/s} \]

231 and 251 knots equivalent. \(V_D\) becomes Mach-limited lower, at 8.1 km, where \(0.87\,a\sqrt{\sigma}\) falls to 173.6 m/s.

The maneuvering envelope

At low speed the wing stalls before it can make the limit load factor; the most it can make is

\[ n_{\max} = \frac{\rho_0 V_E^2\,\CLmax}{2(W/S)} = \left(\frac{V_E}{V_{S1}}\right)^2 \]

a parabola from the origin that reaches \(n_{\text{limit}}\) at \(V_A\). From \(V_A\) the limit holds to \(V_D\). The negative side is the same with the negative stall, at \(C_{L,\min} = -0.8\) (a typical value for the business jet's wing), up to \(n = -1\) at the speed \(V_G\), then \(-1\) to \(V_C\) and a straight line to 0 at \(V_D\). The corners are named in the regulation's figure (25.333 [2]): A at \(V_A\) on the limit, D at \(V_D\), E at \(V_D\) and \(n = 0\), F at \(V_C\) and \(-1\), G at \(V_G\) and \(-1\). The structure must carry every point on and inside the envelope, at every weight and altitude (25.321, 25.333).

Example 4.2 — The business jet's design speeds at takeoff weight

The business jet's clean \(\CLmax\) is 1.305 (Module 10) and its wing loading at takeoff \(6943/28 = 248.0\) kg/m². Find \(V_{S1}\), \(V_A\) and \(V_G\) at sea level.

Show solution
\[ V_{S1} = \sqrt{\frac{2(248.0)(9.807)}{1.225(1.305)}} = 55.16\ \text{m/s} \] \[ V_A = 55.16\sqrt{3.048} = 96.3\ \text{m/s} \] \[ V_G = \sqrt{\frac{2(248.0)(9.807)}{1.225(0.8)}} = 70.45\ \text{m/s} \]

107, 187 and 137 knots, with \(V_C = 270\) and \(V_D = 337.5\) knots. At its maximum zero-fuel weight of 4952 kg, \(V_{S1}\) falls to 46.6 m/s and \(V_A\) to 81.3 m/s: the lighter aircraft reaches 3.048 at lower speed.

With the flaps down the wing can make more lift, so 25.345 [2] sets a smaller envelope for that configuration: a limit of 2.0 up to the flap speed \(V_F\), which must be at least 1.8 times the landing-flap stall speed \(V_{S0}\). For the business jet at its landing weight, with \(\CLmax = 2.1\), \(V_{S0} = 41.25\) m/s and \(V_F \ge 74.3\) m/s (144 knots).

Key takeaways

  • \(n_{\text{limit}} = 2.1 + 24\,000/(W + 10\,000)\), \(W\) in pounds, from 2.5 to 3.8; the negative limit is \(-1\) to \(V_C\), 0 at \(V_D\). The business jet: \(+3.048\).
  • The envelope is drawn in equivalent airspeed: the stall parabola \(n = (V_E/V_{S1})^2\) to \(V_A = V_{S1}\sqrt{n}\), then the limit to \(V_D\).
  • \(V_C\) is the designer's choice, with \(V_D \ge V_C/0.8\); at altitude \(M_C\) and \(M_D \ge M_C + 0.07\) take over. The business jet: 270 and 337.5 knots, Mach 0.80 and 0.87, crossing over at 10.0 and 8.1 km.
  • At sea level and takeoff weight: \(V_{S1} = 55.2\), \(V_A = 96.3\), \(V_C = 138.9\), \(V_D = 173.6\) m/s.
