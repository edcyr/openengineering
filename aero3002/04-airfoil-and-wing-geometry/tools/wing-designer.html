How it is calculated

  • Planform (Lesson 6): \(b = \sqrt{AS}\), \(c_r = 2S/[b(1 + \lambda)]\), \(\bar c = \tfrac23c_r(1 + \lambda + \lambda^2)/(1 + \lambda)\), \(\bar y = (b/6)(1 + 2\lambda)/(1 + \lambda)\), and sweep at any chord line from the quarter-chord sweep.
  • Lift-curve slope (Lesson 4): Helmbold's formula with \(\eta = 0.95\), the sweep of the 30% chord line, at low speed and at the cruise Mach number.
  • Cruise (Lesson 3): \(C_L = W/(\tfrac12\gamma pM^2S)\) in the standard atmosphere; the Reynolds number on the MAC (Lesson 2); \(\MDD\) from the Korn equation with the mid-chord sweep and the thickness at the MAC, interpolated between root and tip.
  • Airfoil (Lessons 1 and 2): thin-airfoil theory for the NACA four-digit camber line gives \(\alpha_{L0}\) and the ideal \(c_l\); the incidence is \(\alpha_{L0} + C_L/\CLa\) (Lesson 7).
  • Span loading (Lesson 6): Schrenk's approximation for the untwisted, unswept planform. Dihedral (Lesson 7) and tail volume coefficients (Lesson 8) from the guideline tables; the tails use the middle of the tail aspect-ratio range for the class.
