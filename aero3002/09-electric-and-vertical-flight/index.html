Interactive module Aerospace Design & Practice · Module 9

Electric Aircraft and Vertical Flight

What if the business jet were electric, or took off vertically? These lessons take electric and hybrid propulsion apart, from batteries and motors to range, sizing and certification, then builds the momentum theory of hover and sizes four vertical-takeoff versions of the jet. Each change is measured against the design as Modules 3 to 8 left it: what it would cost in weight, range and speed.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the module you can:

  • Name the parts of an electric propulsion system, compute a power chain's efficiency, and compare the architectures. (Lesson 1)
  • Compute a pack's usable specific energy, its C-rate, and the battery mass set by energy or by power. (Lesson 2)
  • Compute the range and endurance of a battery aircraft, and its best-range and best-endurance speeds. (Lesson 3)
  • Size an electric aircraft for a range and a reserve, and find the battery wall and the specific energy a mission needs. (Lesson 4)
  • Measure hybrids and battery boost, and the cruise gain of a wing shrunk by blown high lift. (Lesson 5)
  • Relate a motor's torque, speed, mass and heat, and describe how electric engines are certified. (Lesson 6)
  • Use momentum theory to find the hover and climb power of a rotor, fan or jet. (Lesson 7)
  • Compare VTOL configurations, size their rotors, find what a vertical-lift system costs, and state the rules that apply. (Lesson 8)

How to use this module

Before you start: the business jet as Modules 3 to 8 left it: its sizing (3), wing (4), wing loading and constraints (5), layout (6), engines and polar (7) and landing gear (8), and first-year dynamics and fluid mechanics. The course text is Raymer's Aircraft Design: A Conceptual Approach [1]; the lessons use its notation.

Work through the lessons in order. The times allow for working the examples on paper and doing the checks. A suggested plan in four sittings:

  1. Lessons 1–265 min

    Electric propulsion systems; batteries.

  2. Lessons 3–465 min

    Electric range and endurance; sizing an electric aircraft.

  3. Lessons 5–670 min

    Hybrids and distributed propulsion; motors and their certification.

  4. Lessons 7–870 min

    Vertical flight; VTOL configurations and the business jet.

  5. Practice Lab and Self-Check Quiz65 min

    About 30 min of practice, then the 35-minute quiz.

About 5½ h in total:270 min of lessons, 30 min of practice, 35 min for the quiz

Get the most out of it

  • Keep pencil and paper handy

    Try each worked example yourself before you open its solution, and keep track of units: specific energies in Wh/kg are times 3600 in J/kg, powers in watts inside a formula, and shaft speeds in rad/s.

  • Do the checks

    Every lesson has “Check your understanding” questions with hints and full solutions. You can type answers like 0.95*0.98*0.95*0.80 or sqrt(800/(2*1.225)).

  • Practice until it is routine

    The Practice Lab generates unlimited problems, each with a worked solution.

  • Test yourself at the end

    The Self-Check Quiz gives a score, solutions and a results page you can print or save as a PDF.

  • Track your progress

    Tick “Mark this lesson complete” at the end of each lesson. A ✓ appears here and in Contents; it is saved in this browser only.

  • Keep the formulas close

    Print the Formula Sheet and look up terms in the Glossary. The Electric and VTOL Sizer sizes battery-electric and vertical-takeoff versions of the business jet for any range, battery and lift system.
