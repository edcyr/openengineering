A

Back to top
Actuator disk
A rotor idealized as a thin disk that adds momentum to the air through it. In hover the air reaches the disk at \(v_h\) and leaves at \(2v_h\), the slipstream contracting to half the disk's area.
See: Lesson 7Related: Induced velocity \(v_h\), Figure of merit \(FM\), Disk loading
All-electric
An aircraft whose only energy store is its battery: battery, inverter, motor and propulsor. It does not get lighter in flight, so its range equation has no logarithm.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 3Related: Architecture (propulsion), Battery range equation, Series hybrid
Arc fault
An electrical discharge across a damaged or loose high-voltage connection, which can start a fire. The FAA's electric-engine special conditions require high-voltage wiring to be protected against them.
See: Lesson 6Related: Special conditions, Electric engine
Architecture (propulsion)
The arrangement of energy stores, converters and propulsors, and the paths power takes between them: conventional, all-electric, series hybrid, parallel hybrid, turboelectric.
See: Lesson 1Related: All-electric, Series hybrid, Parallel hybrid, Turboelectric
ASTM F3338-18
Standard Specification for Design of Electric Propulsion Units for General Aviation Aircraft (ASTM International, 2018), whose criteria the FAA used for its electric-engine special conditions [4].
See: Lesson 6Related: Special conditions, Electric engine

B

Back to top
Battery boost
Electric power added to an engine's for a short phase of flight, so the engine can be sized for the rest. It saves weight only where that phase sizes the engine and the battery's specific power is high.
See: Lesson 5Related: Parallel hybrid, C-rate
Battery management system
The electronics that monitor and balance a pack's cells and protect them from overcharge, overdischarge and overheating. Part of the pack's overhead \(k_{\text{pack}}\).
See: Lesson 2Related: Pack, Usable specific energy
Battery range equation
\(R = (e_b\eta/g)(L/D)(m_b/m_0)\): the range of a battery aircraft at constant weight. Proportional to the battery fraction, and best at \(\LDmax\).
See: Lesson 3Related: Specific energy \(e_b\), Chain efficiency \(\eta\), Battery wall
Battery sized by power
\(m_b = \max(E/e_b,\ P/(C_{\max}e_b))\): the battery must both hold the mission's energy and deliver its largest power within the C-rate limit. Short, powerful flights are sized by power.
See: Lesson 2Related: C-rate, Specific energy \(e_b\)
Battery wall
The range beyond which a battery aircraft's takeoff weight runs away: the battery and empty-weight fractions approach one, and each added kilogram of battery needs more structure, wing and motor to carry it.
See: Lesson 4Related: Sizing equation with a battery, Battery range equation
Best-endurance speed \(V_{mp}\)
The speed of minimum power, \(V_{md}/3^{1/4} = 0.760\,V_{md}\), where \(L/D = 0.866\,\LDmax\). A battery aircraft stays aloft longest there, and longest of all at low altitude.
See: Lesson 3Related: Best-range speed \(V_{md}\), Endurance
Best-range speed \(V_{md}\)
The speed of minimum drag, \(\sqrt{2W/(\rho S\,C_L^*)}\), at \(\LDmax\). A battery aircraft flies farthest there, at any altitude.
See: Lesson 3Related: Best-endurance speed \(V_{mp}\), Battery range equation

C

Back to top
C-rate
Power drawn over energy stored, \(P/E\), per hour: at 1C a full battery lasts an hour. Cells are limited to about 2–3C without overheating [8], so high-power phases can size a battery by power.
See: Lesson 2Related: Specific power, Battery sized by power
Category Basic and Enhanced
SC-VTOL-01's two certification categories. Enhanced: continued safe flight and landing after a failure, required for commercial passenger transport and flight over congested areas. Basic: a controlled emergency landing [5].
See: Lesson 8Related: SC-VTOL-01, Continued safe flight and landing, Controlled emergency landing
Cell
The smallest unit of a battery. Its rated specific energy is for the full charge; a pack of cells delivers much less per kilogram.
See: Lesson 2Related: Pack, Usable specific energy
Chain efficiency \(\eta\)
The efficiency from the energy store to the thrust power, the product of its components': about 0.71 for an all-electric chain (battery, inverter, motor, propulsor) with typical values.
See: Lesson 1Related: Propulsive efficiency, All-electric
Continued safe flight and landing
SC-VTOL-01: the aircraft can continue controlled flight and land at a vertiport after a failure, possibly with emergency procedures, without exceptional piloting skill [5].
See: Lesson 8Related: Category Basic and Enhanced, Controlled emergency landing
Controlled emergency landing
SC-VTOL-01: the aircraft can make a controlled landing after a failure, possibly with emergency procedures and some damage, like a glide or an autorotation [5].
See: Lesson 8Related: Category Basic and Enhanced, Continued safe flight and landing
Cooling (of an electric drive)
Removing the drive's losses, \(P(1 - \eta)/\eta\), as low-temperature heat: a coolant flow \(\dot m = \dot Q/(c_p\Delta T)\) to radiators, which add mass and drag.
See: Lesson 6Related: Heat rejection, Electric engine

D

Back to top
Degree of hybridization
\(H_P\), the electric share of the installed power, and \(H_E\), the battery's share of the stored energy. A turboelectric aircraft has \(H_E = 0\), an all-electric one \(H_P = H_E = 1\).
See: Lesson 5Related: Series hybrid, Parallel hybrid
Disk loading
Thrust over disk area, \(T/A\). Hover power per unit thrust grows as its square root: about 150–500 N/m² for helicopters, 500–1000 for tiltrotors, near 100 000 for jets.
See: Lesson 7Related: Power loading (hover), Actuator disk
Distributed electric propulsion
Many electric propulsors placed where they help the airframe, such as high-lift propellers along the wing and propellers at the tips. Electric motors make it practical because they are about as efficient small as large [7].
See: Lesson 5Related: High-lift propellers, X-57 Maxwell (SCEPTOR)
Download
The part of a rotor's slipstream that strikes the wing or fuselage below it in hover, which the rotors must make up with extra thrust: typically about 10% of the weight for a tiltrotor.
See: Lesson 7Related: Disk loading, Tiltrotor
Duty cycle
The pattern of power over time an engine is rated for. The FAA's special conditions require an electric engine's ratings to be declared with their duty cycle, coolant and power supply [2], [3].
See: Lesson 6Related: Special conditions, Electric engine

E

Back to top
Electric drive specific power
Shaft power per kilogram of motor and controller. At a typical 5 kW/kg the electric business jet's 2083 kW weighs 417 kg, about what its jet engines did.
See: Lesson 4Related: Specific power, Sizing equation with a battery
Electric engine
In the FAA's special conditions, an electric motor, its controller and the high-voltage systems between them, used as the primary source of propulsion; certified to Part 33 with added standards [2], [3].
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 6Related: Special conditions, Inverter (motor controller)
Endurance
Time aloft, \(t = (e_b\eta/g)(L/D)(m_b/m_0)/V\) for a battery aircraft: best at minimum power and low altitude.
See: Lesson 3Related: Best-endurance speed \(V_{mp}\), Battery range equation
Energy density
Stored energy per unit volume (Wh/L): 9600 for jet fuel, roughly 300–400 for a lithium-ion pack.
See: Lesson 2Related: Specific energy \(e_b\)
Energy per distance
\(E/R = W/(\eta\,L/D)\): the stored energy used per kilometer, which depends on the weight, \(L/D\) and the chain, not the speed. The SCEPTOR project's measure of efficiency [7].
See: Lesson 1Related: Chain efficiency \(\eta\), X-57 Maxwell (SCEPTOR)

F

Back to top
Figure of merit \(FM\)
A rotor's ideal hover power over its real power: 0.68 to 0.74 for NASA's air-taxi concepts [8].
See: Lesson 7Related: Actuator disk, Power loading (hover)

H

Back to top
Heat rejection
A component's loss, \(P(1 - \eta)/\eta\) for an output \(P\). The electric business jet's drive would reject about 272 kW at takeoff, at temperatures low enough to need radiators.
See: Lesson 6Related: Cooling (of an electric drive)
Heating value
The heat a fuel releases when it burns: 43.2 MJ/kg, or 12 000 Wh/kg, for Jet A-1 (Module 7).
See: Lesson 2Related: Specific energy \(e_b\)
Helical tip Mach number
\(\sqrt{M^2 + (V_{\text{tip}}/a)^2}\), the Mach number at a propeller's tips (Module 7). A tiltrotor's would reach 0.92 at the business jet's Mach 0.80 cruise.
See: Lesson 8Related: Tiltrotor
High-lift propellers
Small propellers along a wing's leading edge that blow it at low speed, raising its maximum lift so it can be sized for cruise; they fold in cruise.
See: Lesson 5Related: Distributed electric propulsion, X-57 Maxwell (SCEPTOR)
Hot and high
A high, hot landing site, where thin air raises hover power as \(1/\sqrt\rho\): often the case that sizes a VTOL aircraft's engines.
See: Lesson 7Related: Disk loading
Hover power
\(P = T\sqrt{T/(2\rho A)}/FM\): the shaft power to hover. The business jet as a tiltrotor needs 1.805 MW at sea level.
See: Lesson 7Related: Actuator disk, Figure of merit \(FM\)

I

Back to top
Induced velocity \(v_h\)
The speed of the air through a hovering rotor, \(\sqrt{T/(2\rho A)}\); the far wake moves at twice it.
See: Lesson 7Related: Actuator disk, Disk loading
Inverter (motor controller)
The power electronics that turn a battery's direct current into the alternating current a motor needs, at the frequency its speed needs: typically about 98% efficient.
See: Lesson 1Related: Electric engine, Chain efficiency \(\eta\)

J

Back to top
Jet lift
Lifting with jet engines, as lift jets or by vectoring the thrust: a tiny disk, so about ten times a rotor's hover power and fuel, with hot gas that erodes the ground.
See: Lesson 7, Lesson 8Related: Vectored thrust, Disk loading

L

Back to top
Lift + cruise
A VTOL configuration with separate lift rotors, stopped in cruise, and a propeller or jet for cruise: simple control, but the lift system is dead weight and drag in cruise.
See: Lesson 8Related: Tiltrotor, Multicopter
Lift fan
A ducted fan buried in the wing or fuselage to lift in hover: compact, with a disk loading of thousands of N/m² and a high hover power.
See: Lesson 8Related: Jet lift, Disk loading

M

Back to top
Multicopter
An aircraft lifted and controlled by several fixed rotors, varying their speed or pitch; redundant and simple, but slow and short-ranged.
See: Lesson 8Related: Lift + cruise

P

Back to top
Pack
Cells with their structure, cooling, wiring, contactors and management system. NASA's study took a pack as 1.3 times its cells' mass.
See: Lesson 2Related: Cell, Usable specific energy, Battery management system
Parallel hybrid
An engine and a motor turning the same shaft, so either can drive the propulsor and the battery can add power when it is needed.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 5Related: Series hybrid, Battery boost, Degree of hybridization
Permanent-magnet motor
A motor with magnets on its rotor and windings on its stator, fed by an inverter; the usual aircraft choice. Its torque, and so its mass, grows with the rotor's size.
See: Lesson 6Related: Heat rejection, Inverter (motor controller)
Power loading (hover)
Thrust per unit power in hover, \(T/P = FM/v_h\): about 41.5 N/kW for the tiltrotor business jet at 800 N/m².
See: Lesson 7Related: Disk loading, Figure of merit \(FM\)
Powered-lift
14 CFR Part 1: heavier-than-air aircraft capable of vertical takeoff, vertical landing and low-speed flight that depend principally on engine-driven lift devices or engine thrust in those regimes and on nonrotating airfoils in horizontal flight [6].
See: Lesson 8Related: Special class (21.17(b)), SFAR No. 120
Propulsive efficiency
Thrust power over shaft power for a propeller or fan: typically about 0.80, the largest single loss in an electric chain.
See: Lesson 1Related: Chain efficiency \(\eta\)

R

Back to top
Reserve (battery)
Energy kept unused for a hold or diversion: \(gV_{mp}t/(e_b\eta\,L/D)\) of the takeoff weight. The business jet's 45-minute reserve would take 0.2406 of it at 200 Wh/kg.
See: Lesson 4Related: Sizing equation with a battery, Best-endurance speed \(V_{mp}\)
Rotor locking
Holding a motor's rotor still. If an electric engine can be locked, its special conditions require reliable locking and unlocking without hazard [2].
See: Lesson 6Related: Special conditions

S

Back to top
SC-VTOL-01
EASA's special condition for small-category VTOL aircraft (2 July 2019): up to 9 passengers and 3175 kg, unpressurized, \(M_{MO} \le 0.6\), with more than two lift/thrust units; categories Enhanced and Basic [5].
See: Lesson 8Related: Category Basic and Enhanced, Vertiport
Series hybrid
An engine that drives only a generator; the battery and the generator feed the motors. The engine can run at its best point, but every watt from the fuel is converted twice.
See: Lesson 1Related: Turboelectric, Parallel hybrid
SFAR No. 120
The Special Federal Aviation Regulation (new 14 CFR Part 194) adopted with the FAA's 2024 powered-lift rule, for training and certifying the first powered-lift pilots and for their operations [6].
See: Lesson 8Related: Powered-lift, Special class (21.17(b))
Sizing equation with a battery
\(W_0 = W_{\text{pay}}/(1 - W_e/W_0 - W_b/W_0)\), Module 3's equation with a battery in place of fuel; the battery fraction comes from the range and the reserve.
See: Lesson 4Related: Battery wall, Reserve (battery)
Special class (21.17(b))
The FAA's route to type-certificate aircraft that fit no existing airworthiness part, by applying criteria equivalent to existing standards; the 2024 rule uses it for powered-lift [6].
See: Lesson 8Related: Powered-lift
Special conditions
Extra airworthiness standards the FAA issues (14 CFR 21.16) when a design has a novel or unusual feature the rules do not cover: 33-022-SC for magniX's engines [2], 33-23-01-SC for Safran's [3].
See: Lesson 6Related: Electric engine, ASTM F3338-18
Specific energy \(e_b\)
Stored energy per unit mass: 12 000 Wh/kg for jet fuel, about 150–200 Wh/kg for today's packs, 400 in NASA's projection [8]. In these lessons, the pack's usable value.
See: Lesson 2Related: Usable specific energy, Energy density
Specific power
Power per unit mass (kW/kg), for a battery the power it can deliver, for a motor the power it can make.
See: Lesson 2Related: Electric drive specific power, C-rate

T

Back to top
Thermal runaway
A self-sustaining overheating of a lithium-ion cell that can spread to its neighbors; packs are divided and protected, and SC-VTOL-01 asks for independent energy storage systems [5].
See: Lesson 2Related: Pack, SC-VTOL-01
Tiltrotor
A VTOL aircraft whose rotors tilt from lifting to propelling: one system does both, but its large proprotors limit its cruise speed.
See: Lesson 8Related: Helical tip Mach number, Download, Tiltwing
Tiltwing
A VTOL aircraft whose wing tilts with its propellers, which reduces download but risks the wing stalling in transition.
See: Lesson 8Related: Tiltrotor
Turboelectric
A series hybrid without the battery: a gas turbine drives generators, and wires carry the power to motors that can be anywhere on the airframe.
See: Lesson 1Related: Series hybrid, Distributed electric propulsion

U

Back to top
Usable specific energy
\(e_b = e_{\text{cell}}f_{\text{use}}/k_{\text{pack}}\): the cell's rating times the usable share of its charge (its depth of discharge), over the pack's mass per cell mass. NASA's projection: 650 × 0.80/1.30 = 400 Wh/kg [8].
See: Lesson 2Related: Specific energy \(e_b\), Pack, Cell

V

Back to top
Vectored thrust
Turning an engine's exhaust downward to lift, as on the Harrier; the FAA's 2024 rule notes that such aircraft meet the powered-lift definition [6].
See: Lesson 8Related: Jet lift, Powered-lift
Vertical climb
Climbing straight up at \(V_c\): \(P = T(V_c + v_i)/FM\), with \(v_i = -V_c/2 + \sqrt{V_c^2/4 + v_h^2}\). A slow climb adds about half its \(TV_c\) to the hover power.
See: Lesson 7Related: Hover power, Induced velocity \(v_h\)
Vertical-lift system fraction \(f_v\)
The share of the takeoff weight added by a VTOL system (rotors, drives, booms, larger engines). In the sizing equation it takes up the margin left for payload: 0.12 grows the business jet from 6666 to 16 869 kg.
See: Lesson 8Related: Sizing equation with a battery, Tiltrotor
Vertiport
SC-VTOL-01: an area of land, water or structure used for the landing and takeoff of VTOL aircraft [5].
See: Lesson 8Related: SC-VTOL-01, Continued safe flight and landing

X

Back to top
X-57 Maxwell (SCEPTOR)
NASA's distributed-electric-propulsion demonstrator, a Tecnam P2006T with a new wing: 66.7 instead of 158.9 ft², aspect ratio 15, 12 high-lift propellers and wingtip cruise motors; estimated 4.8 times less energy at 150 KTAS [7], [9].
See: Lesson 5Related: Distributed electric propulsion, High-lift propellers
