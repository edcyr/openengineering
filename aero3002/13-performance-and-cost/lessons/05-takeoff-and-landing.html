Lesson 5 · 35 min

Takeoff and Landing

Module 5 set the business jet's wing loading and thrust from statistical field-length correlations. Now the aircraft is defined well enough to compute its field lengths segment by segment: the ground roll, the climb to the screen height, the case of an engine failing at the worst moment, and the landing. This lesson does so by Torenbeek's method, and finds that the baseline lands long.

Learning objectives

  • Compute the takeoff ground roll and airborne distance from the forces on the aircraft.
  • Explain the balanced field length and estimate it for a twin.
  • Compute the landing distance from 50 ft, and check both against the requirements.

The takeoff

On the ground roll the net force is the thrust less the drag and the rolling friction on the wheels. Torenbeek lumps the drag into an effective friction coefficient, so that the acceleration is nearly constant and the distance follows from \(V^2 = 2as\). After liftoff the aircraft rotates, flares into a climb and must cross a screen height of 35 ft (10.7 m) at its takeoff safety speed \(V_2\).

Takeoff, all engines (Torenbeek)

\[ \mu' = \mu + 0.72\,\frac{C_{D0}}{C_{L\max}}, \qquad s_G = \frac{V_{LOF}^2}{2g\,(T/W - \mu')} \] \[ s_A = \frac{V_{LOF}^2}{g\sqrt2} + \frac{h_{TO}}{\gamma}, \qquad \gamma = \frac{T}{W} - \frac{1}{L/D} \] \[ V_{LOF} = V_2\,\max\!\left[(1 + \gamma\sqrt2)^{-1/2},\ \frac{1.2}{1.3}\right], \qquad V_2 = 1.3\,V_S \]

\(\mu = 0.02\) on dry concrete; \(C_{D0}\) and \(L/D\) in the takeoff configuration; \(T\) the mean thrust on the run. After Torenbeek, Synthesis of Subsonic Airplane Design (1976), §5.4.5, as implemented in AeroSandbox.

The takeoff field length must also allow for an engine failing. If it fails before the decision speed \(V_1\), the pilot stops; after it, the takeoff continues on the remaining engines. The balanced field length is the distance at the \(V_1\) for which the two cases, accelerate-stop and accelerate-go, need the same runway. Part 25 (25.113) takes the takeoff field length as the greater of it and 115% of the all-engines distance.

Balanced field length (Torenbeek)

\[ \text{BFL} = \frac{V_{LOF}^2}{2g\,(1 + \bar\gamma/\bar a)}\left(\frac{1}{T/W - \mu'} + \frac{1}{\bar a}\right)\left(1 + \frac{2g\,h_{TO}}{V_{LOF}^2}\right) + t_i V_{LOF} \]

\(\bar\gamma = 0.06 + (\gamma_{OEI} - \gamma_{\min})\): the one-engine-out climb gradient's excess over the minimum (0.024 for a twin); \(\bar a = 0.37\): the braking deceleration in g on dry concrete; \(t_i = 4.5\) s: the time to recognize the failure and act. AeroSandbox's implementation of Torenbeek's eq. 5-89.

Example 5.1 — The baseline's takeoff

The baseline: \(W_0 = 9240\) kg, \(S = 36.96\) m², two engines of 15.66 kN, \(C_{L\max,TO} = 1.7\), \(L/D = 12.0\) with takeoff flaps (Module 9). Take the mean thrust on the run as 90% of the static thrust, and \(C_{D0} = 0.035\) with the flaps and gear down. Find the distance to 35 ft and the balanced field length.

Show solution

\(T/W = 0.9(31\,326)/(9240 \times 9.807) = 0.311\); \(V_S = 48.52\) m/s, \(V_2 = 63.08\) m/s; \(\gamma = 0.311 - 1/12 = 0.228\).

\((1 + 0.228\sqrt2)^{-1/2} = 0.870\), below \(1.2/1.3 = 0.923\), so \(V_{LOF} = 0.923(63.08) = 58.2\) m/s.

\[ \mu' = 0.02 + 0.72\,\frac{0.035}{1.7} = 0.0348 \] \[ s_G = \frac{58.2^2}{2(9.807)(0.311 - 0.0348)} = 626\ \text{m} \] \[ s_A = \frac{58.2^2}{9.807\sqrt2} + \frac{10.67}{0.228} = 245 + 47 = 291\ \text{m} \]

917 m to 35 ft. With one engine out, \(\gamma_{OEI} = 0.311/2 - 1/12 = 0.072\) and \(\bar\gamma = 0.06 + 0.048 = 0.108\):

\[ \text{BFL} = \frac{3391}{2(9.807)(1.292)}(3.619 + 2.703)(1.062) + 4.5(58.2) = 1160\ \text{m} \]

Greater than \(1.15 \times 917 = 1055\) m, so the takeoff field length is 1160 m: inside the 1200 m required. Module 5's correlation, \(2.34\) times the takeoff parameter, gave 995 m at this \(W/S\) and \(T/W\).

The landing

The landing distance is measured from 50 ft (15.2 m) above the threshold: an approach and flare in the air, from the approach speed \(V_A = 1.3\,V_S\) down to touchdown at about \(1.15\,V_S\), then a ground roll, of which the first seconds pass before the brakes and spoilers act.

Landing distance from 50 ft (Torenbeek)

\[ s_{\text{air}} = \frac{1}{\bar\gamma}\left(\frac{V_A^2 - V_{TD}^2}{2g} + h_L\right), \qquad \bar\gamma = 2\tan 3^\circ \] \[ s_{\text{ground}} = t_i V_{TD} + \frac{V_{TD}^2}{2g\,\bar a} \]

A 3° approach, \(t_i = 4.5\) s and \(\bar a = 0.37\) as for takeoff. Touchdown at \(1.15\,V_S\) is this module's assumption.

Example 5.2 — The baseline's landing

At its landing weight, \(0.9\,W_0 = 8316\) kg, with \(C_{L\max,L} = 2.1\). Find the landing distance and compare it with the 840 m required.

Show solution
\[ V_S = \sqrt{\frac{2(8316)(9.807)}{1.225(36.96)(2.1)}} = 41.4\ \text{m/s} \]

\(V_A = 53.8\) m/s, \(V_{TD} = 47.6\) m/s; \(\bar\gamma = 0.105\).

\[ s_{\text{air}} = \frac{1}{0.105}\left(\frac{53.8^2 - 47.6^2}{19.61} + 15.24\right) = 452\ \text{m} \] \[ s_{\text{ground}} = 4.5(47.6) + \frac{47.6^2}{2(9.807)(0.37)} = 214 + 313 = 527\ \text{m} \]

979 m: 17% more than the 840 m required. Module 5's correlation gave exactly 840 m at \(W/S = 250\); the segment method is less optimistic. With these settings, 840 m needs \(W/S \le 202\) kg/m², or more lift.

The landing distance depends on the stall speed squared, so on \((W/S)/C_{L\max}\): the fixes are a larger wing (lower \(W/S\)), more powerful flaps, or better stopping. Double-slotted flaps might raise \(C_{L\max,L}\) from 2.1 to about 2.4 (Module 9); thrust reversers and carbon brakes shorten the ground roll. Each has its price in weight, cost or complexity, which Lesson 8 weighs. The segment methods also rest on assumptions (the inertia time, the braking, the touchdown speed) that a real program would replace with test data.

Key takeaways

  • Ground roll \(s_G = V_{LOF}^2/[2g(T/W - \mu')]\); the airborne distance to 35 ft adds a flare and a climb at \(\gamma = T/W - 1/(L/D)\).
  • The balanced field length equalizes accelerate-stop and accelerate-go; the takeoff field length is the greater of it and 115% of the all-engines distance.
  • The baseline takes off in 1160 m (1200 required) and lands in 979 m (840 required): it lands long.
  • Landing distance scales with \((W/S)/C_{L\max,L}\): a larger wing, better flaps or better brakes, each at a cost.
