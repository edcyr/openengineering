How it is calculated

  • Requirement (Lesson 3): a turbofan's take-off thrust per engine \(T = (\TW)\,\Wo g/N\); a turboprop's take-off power per engine \(P = (P/W)\,\Wo/N\), with \(P/W\) in kW/kg.
  • Rubber engine (Lesson 3): \(SF = T/T_{\text{base}}\) (or \(P/P_{\text{base}}\)); length \(\propto SF^{0.4}\), height or diameter \(\propto SF^{0.5}\), weight \(\propto SF^{1.1}\). The exponents are for small changes: a scale factor outside 0.8 to 1.25 is flagged. Turboprops are scaled by power with the same exponents, an assumption.
  • Nacelle (Lesson 4): diameter and length as multiples of the rubber engine's height and length (1.1 and 1.2 by default, an allowance to be replaced by a nacelle design); wetted area \(\pi D_n l_n\) each.
  • Cruise (Lessons 1 and 2): thrust per engine \(\beta\Wo g/(N\,L/D)\). A turbofan's fuel flow is \(c_T T\); its mass flow \(\dot m = T/(V_j - V)\) for the chosen jet velocity, its propulsive efficiency \(2/(1 + V_j/V)\), and its capture area \(A_0 = \dot m/(\rho V)\) (Lesson 5). A turboprop's shaft power is \(TV/\eta_p\), and its fuel flow \(c_P P\).
  • Thrust at altitude (Lessons 2 and 8): the take-off thrust times the lapse \(\alpha = \sigma\) and times the high-bypass correlation \(\delta_0(1 - 0.49\sqrt M)\), against the cruise thrust (level flight, no climb margin).
  • Propeller (Lesson 6): helical tip Mach \(\sqrt{(\pi Dn)^2 + V^2}/a\) in cruise, the largest diameter for the tip-Mach limit, and the ideal (actuator-disk) static thrust \((2\rho AP^2)^{1/3}\) at sea level and cruise efficiency.
  • Engine data are from EASA type-certificate data sheets (see Lesson 3). The business jet preset is the module's; the turboprop and airliner presets are illustrative, not real types. Your inputs are saved in this browser.
