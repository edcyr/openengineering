A

Back to top
All-moving tail
A horizontal tail that pivots as a whole instead of carrying an elevator; its weight equation takes \(K_{uht} = 1.143\).
See: Lesson 3Related: Tail arm \(L_t\), Statistical group weight equations
Approximate group weights
A first estimate that gives the wing and tails a weight per square meter of exposed area, the fuselage per square meter of wetted area, and the gear and everything else as fractions of \(\Wo\). Weight per area grows with size, so the values fit only aircraft of the size they came from: for the business jet they give 36% to 73% of \(\Wo\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Statistical group weight equations, Empty weight \(\We\)
Auxiliary power unit (APU)
A small turbine that supplies electrical power and air on the ground; installed, it weighs 2.2 times its uninstalled weight. The business jet has none.
See: Lesson 5Related: Equipment group
Avionics installed and uninstalled
The aircraft's electronics. The equation turns the uninstalled weight \(W_{uav}\) into the installed one, \(1.73W_{uav}^{0.983}\) in pounds, adding racks, wiring, cooling and antennas: 136 kg uninstalled becomes 213.5 kg in the business jet.
See: Lesson 5Related: Equipment group, Electrical rating \(R_{kva}\)

B

Back to top
Basic operating weight
A manufacturer's operating empty weight for a typical aircraft ready to fly. The Citation M2, CJ3 and CJ4 carry 60% to 65% of their takeoff weight as basic operating weight [7].
See: Lesson 1Related: Operating empty weight, Empty weight \(\We\)

C

Back to top
Calibration factor \(k\)
The factor that makes the statistical equations reproduce a known aircraft: \(k = (W_{e,\text{target}} - W_{\text{catalog}})/W_{\text{statistical}}\). It keeps the equations' exponents and replaces their constant. The business jet's is 1.151.
See: Lesson 6Related: Catalog weight, Statistical group weight equations, Sizing loop
Cargo-door factor \(K_{\text{door}}\)
1.0 with no cargo door, 1.06 with one side door, 1.12 with two or an aft clamshell, 1.25 with two and a clamshell: large cutouts need heavy frames.
See: Lesson 4Related: Fuselage weight equation, Fuselage-gear factor \(K_{lg}\)
Catalog weight
A weight known from the manufacturer rather than estimated, such as the dry engines' 401.6 kg in the business jet; calibration does not scale it.
See: Lesson 6Related: Calibration factor \(k\), Dry engine weight
Center of gravity \(x_{cg}\)
The point where the whole weight can be taken to act: \(x_{cg} = \sum W_ix_i/\sum W_i\), with each station measured from a datum.
See: Lesson 7Related: Datum, Moment table, Percent MAC
CG range
The span of CG positions the aircraft can have in service, from the forward and aft extremes of its loading diagram. With its wing moved, the business jet's is 17.1% to 32.9% of the MAC; Module 8 assumed 15% to 35%.
See: Lesson 8Related: Loading diagram, Wing shift \(\Delta x\), Percent MAC
Composite factors
Factors on the aluminum estimate for advanced composites: wing 0.85–0.90, tails 0.83–0.88, fuselage and nacelles 0.90–0.95, landing gear 0.95–1.0 ([1], Table 15.4). An all-composite structure saves the business jet 202 kg, and the sizing loop turns that into more than 530 kg of takeoff weight.
See: Lesson 6Related: Growth factor, Calibration factor \(k\)
Control-surface area \(S_{csw}\)
The area of the wing's flaps and control surfaces. Their hinges, tracks and actuators add weight: \(S_{csw}^{0.1}\). The business jet's is 6.48 m².
See: Lesson 3Related: Wing weight equation

D

Back to top
Datum
The reference point from which stations are measured; here the nose, with \(x\) positive aft.
See: Lesson 7Related: Center of gravity \(x_{cg}\), Moment table
Design gross weight \(W_{dg}\)
The weight the structure is designed at, the maximum takeoff weight here: 6943 kg (15 307 lb) for the business jet.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 3Related: Takeoff weight \(\Wo\), Ultimate load factor \(N_z\)
Design loop
The cycle in which every change feeds the others: layout, drag and \((L/D)_{\max}\), fuel fraction, takeoff weight, group weights, CG, and back to the layout. Moving the business jet's wing enlarges its tails, which raises \(C_{D0}\) from 0.0202 to 0.0205 and \(\Wo\) by about 80 kg; Module 14 runs the loop to convergence.
See: Lesson 8, Lesson 6Related: Sizing loop, Wing shift \(\Delta x\)
Dry engine weight
An engine's weight without its fluids and nacelle, from its type certificate: 200.8 kg for each of the business jet's scaled engines (Module 7).
See: Lesson 5Related: Engine with contents \(W_{ec}\), Catalog weight

E

Back to top
Electrical rating \(R_{kva}\)
The electrical system's rating in kilovolt-amperes; with the routing distance and the number of generators it sets the electrical system's weight. The business jet's two 300-ampere starter-generators give 16.8 kV·A and 114 kg.
See: Lesson 5Related: Avionics, Equipment group
Empty CG
The CG of the empty aircraft, the start of every loading. The business jet's is at 7.520 m, 52.1% of the MAC with Module 6's wing: its rear engines make it tail-heavy.
See: Lesson 7Related: Center of gravity \(x_{cg}\), Wing shift \(\Delta x\)
Empty weight \(\We\)
The aircraft as built, with its unusable fuel and operating fluids (14 CFR 25.29 [3]): structure, propulsion and equipment. The business jet's is 3972 kg calibrated, 3504 kg from the equations alone.
See: Lesson 1Related: Weight statement, Operating empty weight
Engine with contents \(W_{ec}\)
\(2.331W_{\text{engine}}^{0.901}K_pK_{tr}\) in pounds: the engine with its accessories and fluids, \(\times 1.4\) with a propeller and \(\times 1.18\) with a reverser. 256.1 kg for each of the business jet's 200.8 kg engines.
See: Lesson 5Related: Nacelle group, Dry engine weight
Equipment group
Flight controls, APU, instruments, hydraulics, electrical system, avionics, furnishings, seats, lavatories, air conditioning, anti-icing and handling gear: 993 kg of the business jet's equation weight. Small aircraft carry far more of it per seat than airliners.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 5Related: Structure group, Propulsion group

F

Back to top
Factor of safety
The factor of 1.5 between the limit and ultimate loads (14 CFR 25.303 [3]).
See: Lesson 2Related: Limit load, Ultimate load
Flight-control functions \(N_f\)
The number of functions the flight controls perform (typically 4 to 7), \(N_m\) of them mechanical. The business jet's 5, with pitch, roll and yaw by cables, give 248 kg of flight controls.
See: Lesson 5Related: Equipment group
Fuselage fineness \(L/D\)
The fuselage's structural length over its depth in the fuselage weight equation, not the lift-to-drag ratio: 9.71 for the business jet.
See: Lesson 4Related: Fuselage weight equation
Fuselage weight equation
\(0.3280K_{\text{door}}K_{lg}(W_{dg}N_z)^{0.5}L^{0.25}S_f^{0.302}(1 + K_{ws})^{0.04}(L/D)^{0.10}\) in pounds and feet: 1011 kg for the business jet, its heaviest group.
See: Lesson 4Related: Cargo-door factor \(K_{\text{door}}\), Wing-sweep factor \(K_{ws}\), Fuselage fineness \(L/D\)
Fuselage-gear factor \(K_{lg}\)
1.12 when the main landing gear is mounted on the fuselage, otherwise 1.
See: Lesson 4Related: Fuselage weight equation, Cargo-door factor \(K_{\text{door}}\)

G

Back to top
Gear load factor \(N_{\text{gear}}\)
The gear's peak force in a landing over the landing weight: 3 for the business jet (Module 8). The weight equations use the ultimate value \(N_l = 1.5N_{\text{gear}} = 4.5\).
See: Lesson 2Related: Landing weight \(W_l\), Main gear weight equation
Growth factor
How many kilograms of takeoff weight one kilogram of empty weight costs once the sizing loop is closed: about 2.6 for the business jet.
See: Lesson 6Related: Sizing loop, Composite factors
Gust load factor
The load factor a vertical gust gives (Module 11). The business jet's, 3.255 at 20 000 ft at its takeoff weight, exceeds its maneuvering 3.048, so its design ultimate load factor is 4.882, the one its weight equations take (Lesson 2).
See: Lesson 2Related: Ultimate load factor \(N_z\), Limit maneuvering load factor \(n_{\text{limit}}\)

I

Back to top
Integral tank
A fuel tank made by sealing the wing box itself. Integral tanks halve the fuel system's weight against bladder tanks: the business jet's three weigh 48 kg.
See: Lesson 5Related: Propulsion group

K

Back to top
Kneeling gear
Landing gear that can lower the aircraft on the ground, for loading; factors of 1.126 (main) and 1.15 (nose).
See: Lesson 4Related: Main gear weight equation, Nose gear weight equation

L

Back to top
Landing weight \(W_l\)
The weight the landing gear and structure are designed to land at: 90% of \(\Wo\), 6249 kg, for the business jet. The gear equations use it, not \(\Wo\).
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 4Related: Gear load factor \(N_{\text{gear}}\), Main gear weight equation
Limit load
The largest load expected in service; the structure must carry it without permanent deformation.
See: Lesson 2Related: Ultimate load, Factor of safety
Limit maneuvering load factor \(n_{\text{limit}}\)
For transports, \(2.1 + 24\,000/(W + 10\,000)\) with \(W\) in pounds, between 2.5 and 3.8 (14 CFR 25.337 [3]): 3.048 for the business jet, 2.5 for any transport above about 23 t.
See: Lesson 2Related: Ultimate load factor \(N_z\), Load factor \(n\)
Load factor \(n\)
Lift over weight: 1 in level flight, 2 in a level turn at 60° of bank.
See: Lesson 2Related: Limit maneuvering load factor \(n_{\text{limit}}\), Ultimate load factor \(N_z\)
Loading diagram
Weight against CG as the aircraft is loaded: crew, passengers front row first and back row first, baggage and fuel. Its extremes are the CG range.
See: Lesson 8Related: CG range, Empty CG

M

Main gear weight equation
\(0.0106K_{mp}W_l^{0.888}N_l^{0.25}L_m^{0.4}N_{mw}^{0.321}N_{mss}^{-0.5}V_{\text{stall}}^{0.1}\) in pounds, inches and knots: 162.5 kg for the business jet. It follows the landing weight almost in proportion.
See: Lesson 4Related: Landing weight \(W_l\), Nose gear weight equation, Kneeling gear
Mean aerodynamic chord \(\cbar\)
The chord of the equivalent rectangular wing, the reference for stability and for CG positions: 2.015 m long, leading edge at 6.471 m, for the business jet with Module 6's wing.
See: Lesson 7Related: Percent MAC, Center of gravity \(x_{cg}\)
Moment table
The weight statement with each group's station and moment \(W_ix_i\); the sum of the moments over the sum of the weights is the CG.
See: Lesson 7Related: Center of gravity \(x_{cg}\), Weight statement

N

Nacelle group
The nacelles, mounts and inlets, from the nacelle's size, the load factor and the engine with its contents: 133.7 kg for the business jet's two.
See: Lesson 5Related: Engine with contents \(W_{ec}\), Propulsion group
Nose gear weight equation
\(0.032K_{np}W_l^{0.646}N_l^{0.2}L_n^{0.5}N_{nw}^{0.45}\) in pounds and inches: 48.1 kg for the business jet.
See: Lesson 4Related: Main gear weight equation

Operating empty weight
The empty weight with the crew and the operator's items. The business jet's, with 180 kg of crew, is 4152 kg, 59.8% of \(\Wo\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Empty weight \(\We\), Basic operating weight, Zero-fuel weight

Percent MAC
A CG position as a share of the mean aerodynamic chord: \(100(x_{cg} - x_{\text{LE,MAC}})/\cbar\).
See: Lesson 7Related: Mean aerodynamic chord \(\cbar\), Center of gravity \(x_{cg}\), CG range
Propulsion group
The engines with their nacelles, controls, starters and fuel system: 628.8 kg for the business jet, 1.57 times its dry engines.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 5Related: Nacelle group, Dry engine weight

R

Back to top
Radius of gyration \(K_y\), \(K_z\)
The distance at which the aircraft's mass would have its moment of inertia; the tail equations take about \(0.3L_t\) in pitch and \(L_t\) in yaw.
See: Lesson 3Related: Tail arm \(L_t\)
Ramp weight
The takeoff weight plus the fuel burned while taxiing.
See: Lesson 1Related: Takeoff weight \(\Wo\)

S

Back to top
Sensitivity (exponent)
In a power law each exponent is the percentage change in weight per percent change in its variable: the wing's 0.557 on \(W_{dg}N_z\) means 10% more load adds about 5.6% of wing.
See: Lesson 3Related: Statistical group weight equations
Sizing loop
Solving \(\Wo = W_{\text{crew}} + W_{\text{payload}} + (W_f/\Wo)\Wo + \We(\Wo)\) with the group weights of a design resized at each guess. Uncalibrated, the business jet closes at 5743 kg; calibrated, at 6943 kg.
See: Lesson 6Related: Growth factor, Calibration factor \(k\)
Statistical group weight equations
Power laws fitted to the group weights of aircraft of the 1950s and 1960s [4], one per group, in pounds and feet. Their exponents carry the physics; their constants need calibrating for modern aircraft.
See: Lesson 3Related: Sensitivity (exponent), Calibration factor \(k\), Approximate group weights
Structure group
The wing, tails, fuselage and landing gear: 1882 kg of the business jet's equation weight.
See: Lesson 1Related: Propulsion group, Equipment group

T

Back to top
T-tail
A horizontal tail on top of the fin, \(H_t/H_v = 1\): the fin must carry the tail's loads, which makes it \(2^{0.225} = 1.169\) times heavier.
See: Lesson 3Related: Tail arm \(L_t\)
Tail arm \(L_t\)
The distance from the wing's to the tail's quarter-MAC. A longer arm lightens the tails twice over: the equations fall with it, and less area is needed. The business jet's shortens from 7.285 to 6.734 m when its wing moves aft.
See: Lesson 3, Lesson 8Related: Radius of gyration \(K_y\), \(K_z\), T-tail, Wing shift \(\Delta x\)
Takeoff weight \(\Wo\)
The aircraft with its crew, payload and fuel at the start of the takeoff; its maximum is the design gross weight. The course's business jet: 6943 kg.
See: Lesson 1Related: Design gross weight \(W_{dg}\), Sizing loop

U

Back to top
Ultimate load
The limit load times the factor of safety; the structure must carry it without breaking. Structures are sized at ultimate load.
See: Lesson 2Related: Limit load, Ultimate load factor \(N_z\)
Ultimate load factor \(N_z\)
1.5 times the largest limit load factor, from maneuvers or gusts: the load factor the structural weight equations use. The business jet's is 4.882, from the gust (4.573 from maneuvers alone); an airliner's 3.75.
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 2Related: Limit maneuvering load factor \(n_{\text{limit}}\), Gust load factor, Ultimate load

W

Back to top
Weight statement
The empty weight listed by groups, in a fixed order, so that designs can be compared and each group has a budget.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 5Related: Structure group, Propulsion group, Equipment group
Wing group \(W_{wg}\)
Everything that moves when the wing moves: the wing, the main gear on it, the fuel system, the flight controls, the anti-icing and the fuel. 1188 kg, 30% of the business jet's empty weight.
See: Lesson 8Related: Wing shift \(\Delta x\)
Wing shift \(\Delta x\)
Moving the wing to place the CG range: \(\Delta x = (x_{cg} - x_{\text{LE,MAC}} - p\cbar)/(1 - W_{wg}/W)\). The business jet's wing moves 0.55 m aft, to 48.6% of the fuselage.
See: Lesson 8Related: Wing group \(W_{wg}\), CG range, Empty CG
Wing weight equation
\(0.0051(W_{dg}N_z)^{0.557}S_w^{0.649}A^{0.5}(t/c)^{-0.4}(1+\lambda)^{0.1}(\cos\Lambda)^{-1}S_{csw}^{0.1}\) in pounds and square feet: 559 kg for the business jet, 8.1% of \(\Wo\).
See: Lesson 3Related: Sensitivity (exponent), Control-surface area \(S_{csw}\)
Wing-sweep factor \(K_{ws}\)
\(0.75[(1 + 2\lambda)/(1 + \lambda)]B_w\tan\Lambda/L\): a swept wing carries its root bending into a longer stretch of fuselage. The business jet's is 0.425.
See: Lesson 4Related: Fuselage weight equation

Z

Back to top
Zero-fuel weight
The operating weight plus the payload. Its maximum limits the bending at the wing root, since fuel in the wing relieves it: 4952 kg for the business jet with its full payload.
See: Lesson 1Related: Operating empty weight, Takeoff weight \(\Wo\)
