Lesson 8 · 40 min

Loading and Placing the Wing

An aircraft flies at every CG its loading allows, not only at its empty one. This lesson follows the business jet's CG as it is loaded, finds the whole range, and moves the wing so the range falls where the landing gear, and later the stability, need it. Then it follows what the move sets off: new tails, new drag, a new weight, and another turn of the design loop.

Learning objectives

  • Follow the CG as an aircraft is loaded, and draw its loading diagram.
  • Find the forward and aft extremes of the CG from the loading orders.
  • Find the wing shift that puts the CG range where it is wanted.
  • Check what moving the wing does to the landing gear and the tails.
  • Trace how a change to the layout feeds back into the drag, the lift-to-drag ratio and the weight estimate.

The loading diagram

Each item loaded moves the CG toward its own station. Start from the empty aircraft and add the crew, then the passengers row by row, the baggage and the fuel, plotting the weight against the CG after each. The passengers can board in any order, so the diagram takes the extremes: front row first and back row first. The two paths make the loop of a loading diagram [2], and the extremes of every path are the CG range the aircraft must fly with.

For the business jet, take each passenger as 80 kg in the seat and 20 kg of baggage in the compartment behind the cabin, at 10.38 m. Its four rows of two seats are at 5.45, 6.42, 7.38 and 8.35 m; the crew sit at 2.88 m; the fuel, 1991 kg, is in the wing at 7.076 m.

Example 8.1 — The first steps of loading

From the empty business jet, 3972.4 kg at 7.520 m, load the crew, then the front row. Find the CG after each step, as a percentage of the MAC (leading edge 6.471 m, length 2.015 m).

Show solution
\[ x_1 = \frac{3972.4(7.520) + 180(2.880)}{4152.4} = \frac{30\,392}{4152.4} = 7.319\ \text{m} = 42.1\%\ \text{MAC} \] \[ x_2 = \frac{30\,392 + 160(5.450)}{4312.4} = \frac{31\,264}{4312.4} = 7.250\ \text{m} = 38.6\%\ \text{MAC} \]

Every load ahead of the CG pulls it forward. Loading front to back, the CG reaches 37.2% after the second row, then drifts back as the rear rows fill; the baggage takes it to 44.3% and the fuel, near the CG, to 40.2%.

With Module 6's wing the CG ranges from 35.0% to 52.1% of the MAC: 17% wide, but all of it behind Module 8's assumed range. The width is the loading's; where it sits is the wing's.

Placing the wing

Moving the wing aft by \(\Delta x\) carries the wing group with it: the wing, the main landing gear on it, the fuel system, the flight controls and anti-icing in it, and the fuel. The rest stays. The CG moves aft by only the wing group's share of \(\Delta x\), but the MAC, the reference, moves by all of it:

The wing shift for a wanted CG position

\[ \Delta x = \frac{x_{cg} - x_{\text{LE,MAC}} - p\,\cbar}{1 - W_{wg}/W} \]

\(p\), the wanted CG as a fraction of the MAC; \(W_{wg}\), the wing group's weight in the loading condition \(W\). For a whole loading range, center it: since \(W_{wg}/W\) varies with the loading, iterate.

Example 8.2 — Centering the business jet's range

The range runs from 35.0% to 52.1% of the MAC. The empty aircraft's wing group is 1188.3 kg of its 3972.4 kg. Estimate the wing shift that centers the range on 25%.

Show solution

The range's middle is at \((35.0 + 52.1)/2 = 43.5\%\): it must move 18.5% of the MAC, \(0.185(2.015) = 0.373\) m, relative to the wing. With the empty aircraft's wing-group share:

\[ \Delta x = \frac{0.373}{1 - 1188.3/3972.4} = \frac{0.373}{0.701} = 0.532\ \text{m} \]

Iterating with every loading condition's own share gives 0.551 m. The wing's quarter-MAC moves from 6.975 to 7.526 m, from 45% to 48.6% of the fuselage, and the CG range becomes 17.1% to 32.9% of the MAC.

What moving the wing changes

The wing does not move alone. Its main landing gear moves with it, to 8.20 m, and Module 8's checks must be made again with the computed range. With Module 6's wing and Module 8's gear at 7.65 m, the empty jet would carry 2.2% of its weight on its nose wheel and tip back at \(6.2^\circ\): it would sit on its tail. With the wing moved, the nose wheel carries 8.1 to 13.0% and the tip-back angle at the aft CG is \(23.3^\circ\), inside Module 8's 8 to 15% and above its \(15^\circ\).

The tails stay where they are, so their arm shortens from 7.285 to 6.734 m. At Module 6's volume coefficients the horizontal tail grows from 5.42 to 5.87 m² and the fin from 3.45 to 3.73 m², a few kilograms more of tail. Module 13 sizes the tails for stability and control, and sets the aft CG limit that this range must respect.

Around the design loop again

Moving the wing settles the CG, but it unsettles everything that depended on the old layout. The shorter tail arm needs larger tails; larger tails add wetted area, so they add zero-lift drag (Module 10) and weight (Lesson 3); more drag lowers the lift-to-drag ratio, which raises the fuel fraction (Module 3); a higher fuel fraction raises the takeoff weight; a heavier aircraft needs a larger wing at the same wing loading (Module 5) and larger engines at the same thrust-to-weight ratio (Module 7), which change the group weights and their CG once more; and the wing may have to move again. Every number a design has is an input to some other number.

Example 8.3 — What the larger tails cost

Module 10's build-up gives the business jet's horizontal tail and fin drag coefficients of 0.00189 and 0.00114 (on the wing's reference area), with 5% added for leakage and protuberances, and \(K = 0.04909\). With the wing moved, the tails grow by 8.2% each. Estimate the new zero-lift drag and \((L/D)_{\max}\).

Show solution

Each tail's drag grows with its wetted area, so with its planform area:

\[ \Delta C_{D0} = 1.05\,(0.00189 + 0.00114)(0.082) = 0.00026 \] \[ C_{D0} = 0.02023 + 0.00026 = 0.02049 \] \[ \left(\frac LD\right)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{0.02049(0.04909)}} = 15.77 \]

The full build-up, in which the larger tails' longer chords give them slightly less friction, gives an increase of 0.00023 and 15.78; the cruise \(L/D\) at 12 km falls from 10.80 to 10.69. Run through Module 3's sizing with the tails' extra 5.5 kg, the lower \(L/D\) raises \(\Wo\) by about 80 kg: 1.1%, 47 kg of it from the drag. Small, but not zero, and the larger aircraft starts the next turn of the loop.

One turn of this loop is small. The larger turn is still to come: every weight in Lessons 1 to 8 rests on Module 3's fuel fraction, which assumed a cruise \(L/D\) of 14.6, while Module 10 found the business jet cruising at 10.8 at 12 km. Fed through the sizing, that alone would take the takeoff weight to about 10 700 kg (Module 10, Lesson 8), and every group weight, the CG and the wing's position would follow. That is why conceptual design iterates: no estimate is final until all of them agree.

What the course carries forward

The business jet's weights after this module: an empty weight of 3972 kg, calibrated from statistical group weights that alone give 3504 kg (\(k = 1.151\)); structure 2166, propulsion 663 and equipment 1143 kg. Its wing moves aft 0.55 m, to a quarter-MAC station of 7.526 m (48.6% of the fuselage), with the main gear at 8.20 m. Its CG then ranges from 17.1% to 32.9% of the MAC: 32.9% empty, forward of that once loaded. The tail arm shortens to 6.734 m. These changes are not free: the larger tails raise the zero-lift drag to about 0.0205 and lower \((L/D)_{\max}\) to 15.78, and the weights still rest on Module 3's fuel fraction, not on Module 10's cruise \(L/D\). The earlier modules' numbers are the design as it stood then; this module's changes, and Module 10's, have yet to go around the loop. Module 13 finds the neutral point and sizes the tails, which will confirm or move the aft limit; Module 14 brings the drag, the weights and the engines together and closes the design.

Key takeaways

  • A loading diagram follows the CG through loading; front-to-back and back-to-front loading bound it.
  • The business jet's loading moves its CG over 17% of the MAC; with Module 6's wing the whole range lies aft of 35.0%.
  • Moving the wing moves the MAC fully and the CG only by the wing group's share: \(\Delta x = (x_{cg} - x_{\text{LE,MAC}} - p\cbar)/(1 - W_{wg}/W)\).
  • The wing moves 0.55 m aft, to 48.6% of the fuselage: the range becomes 17.1 to 32.9%, and the gear checks pass again with the main gear at 8.20 m.
  • A shorter tail arm needs larger tails: Module 13 sizes them and sets the aft limit.
  • Every layout change goes around the design loop: larger tails add drag and weight, lower \((L/D)_{\max}\) (15.87 to 15.78), raise the fuel fraction and the takeoff weight, and move the CG again. Module 10's cruise \(L/D\) has yet to go around it; Module 14 closes the loop.
