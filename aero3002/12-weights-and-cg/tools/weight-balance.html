How it is calculated

  • Geometry (Module 6): the chosen layout, with Module 7's nacelles on the business jet. At a takeoff weight other than the preset's, the wing scales with \(\Wo\) at the same wing loading, the tails at the same volume coefficients, the engines as Module 7's rubber engine and the fuel at the same fraction; the fuselage is kept.
  • Load factors (Lesson 2): \(n_{\text{limit}} = 2.1 + 24\,000/(W + 10\,000)\) with \(W\) in pounds, between 2.5 and 3.8 [3]; \(N_z = 1.5\,n\), with \(n\) the larger of \(n_{\text{limit}}\) and, for the business jet, Module 11's gust load factor at its design cruising speed, found again for the wing at the takeoff weight entered (4.882 at 6943 kg); the other three presets have no gust case and keep \(n_{\text{limit}}\); \(N_l = 1.5\,N_{\text{gear}}\) at the landing weight.
  • Group weights (Lessons 3 to 5): the statistical equations for transport aircraft [1], in pounds and feet, with the seats and lavatories added; the engines' dry weight from the catalog.
  • Calibration and composites (Lesson 6): the statistical groups multiplied by \(k\), either 1, the factor that reproduces the empty weight the preset's fuel fraction and payload leave, or your own; composites as the middle of each range, on the share of the structure chosen.
  • CG (Lessons 7 and 8): the positions of Lesson 7 [6]; loading from the empty aircraft with the crew, each row of passengers (80% of their mass in the seat, 20% as baggage in the holds), then the fuel at the wing's CG. Moving the wing moves the wing, main gear, fuel system, flight controls, anti-icing and fuel, and the MAC.
  • Sizing loop (Lesson 6): \(\Wo = W_{\text{crew}} + W_{\text{payload}} + (W_f/\Wo)\Wo + \We(\Wo)\) with this calibration and composite share.
  • A long cabin over a short MAC, like the turboprop's, takes the CG far outside any envelope when it is loaded strictly front to back or back to front; operators load such aircraft by seating rules.
  • The business jet preset is the design you follow through the lessons; the other three are illustrative, not real types. Several of its inputs (control functions, electrical rating, avionics, gear lengths) are early estimates that a later design stage would refine. Your inputs are saved in this browser.
