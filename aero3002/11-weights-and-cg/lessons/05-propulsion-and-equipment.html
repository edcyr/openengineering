Propulsion and Equipment Weights

An engine's catalog weight is only part of what it costs the aircraft: it needs a nacelle, mounts, controls, a starter and a fuel system. And the aircraft needs systems to fly, to talk and to keep its passengers comfortable. This lesson completes the business jet's weight statement with both groups.

Learning objectives

  • Estimate the installed propulsion group from the dry engine weight.
  • Estimate the equipment groups, and say which design decisions set each one's inputs.
  • Assemble a complete weight statement and explain why small aircraft carry more equipment per passenger.

The propulsion group

The engines' dry weight comes from the manufacturer: the business jet's two scaled FJ44-3A engines weigh 200.8 kg each (Module 7). Around them:

Propulsion group, transport aircraft (lb)

\[ \begin{aligned} W_{\text{nacelle group}} = 0.6724\,K_{ng}N_{Lt}^{0.10}N_w^{0.294}N_z^{0.119} \\ \times W_{ec}^{0.611}N_{en}^{0.984}S_n^{0.224} \end{aligned} \qquad W_{ec} = 2.331\,W_{\text{engine}}^{0.901}K_pK_{tr} \] \[ W_{\text{engine controls}} = 5.0\,N_{en} + 0.80\,L_{ec}, \qquad W_{\text{starter}} = 49.19\left(\frac{N_{en}W_{\text{engine}}}{1000}\right)^{0.541} \] \[ W_{\text{fuel system}} = 2.405\,V_t^{0.606}\left(1 + \frac{V_i}{V_t}\right)^{-1}\left(1 + \frac{V_p}{V_t}\right)N_t^{0.5} \]

\(N_{Lt}\), \(N_w\), \(S_n\): each nacelle's length, width (ft) and wetted area (ft²); \(W_{ec}\), the weight of an engine with its contents; \(K_{ng} = 1.017\) for pylon-mounted nacelles; \(K_p = 1.4\) with a propeller; \(K_{tr} = 1.18\) with a thrust reverser; \(L_{ec}\), the length from the cockpit to each engine's front, summed (ft); \(V_t\), \(V_i\), \(V_p\): the total, integral and protected (self-sealing) fuel volumes (US gal); \(N_t\), the number of tanks. Source: Raymer, Section 15.3.

Example 5.1 — The business jet's propulsion group

Each engine weighs 442.7 lb dry, in a pylon-mounted nacelle 1.770 m long and 0.797 m in diameter (5.81 by 2.62 ft, \(S_n = 47.7\) ft²), with no reverser. The engines' fronts are 8.34 m behind the cockpit. The fuel, 2.488 m³ (657 gal), is all in integral tanks: two in the wing and one in its center box. Find the group's weight.

Show solution
\[ W_{ec} = 2.331(442.7)^{0.901} = 564.5\ \text{lb} \] \[ \begin{aligned} W_{\text{nacelle group}} &= 0.6724(1.017)(1.192)(1.327) \\ &\quad\times(1.208)(48.00)(1.978)(2.377) = 294.8\ \text{lb} \end{aligned} \] \[ W_{\text{controls}} = 5(2) + 0.80(2)(27.36) = 53.8\ \text{lb} \] \[ W_{\text{starter}} = 49.19(0.8854)^{0.541} = 46.1\ \text{lb} \] \[ W_{\text{fuel system}} = 2.405(657.3)^{0.606}(2)^{-1}(1)(3)^{0.5} = 106.2\ \text{lb} \]

In kilograms: 133.7, 24.4, 20.9 and 48.2, with 401.6 for the engines: 628.8 kg, 1.57 times the dry engines. Integral tanks halve the fuel system's weight against bladder tanks, which would make \(V_i = 0\).

The equipment group

Each equipment equation turns a few design decisions into a weight. The decisions are the designer's to make, and at this stage they are estimates:

Equipment, transport aircraft (lb)

\[ W_{\text{flight controls}} = 145.9\,N_f^{0.554}\left(1 + \frac{N_m}{N_f}\right)^{-1}S_{cs}^{0.20}\left(I_y \times 10^{-6}\right)^{0.07} \] \[ W_{\text{instruments}} = 4.509\,K_rK_{tp}N_c^{0.541}N_{en}(L_f + B_w)^{0.5} \] \[ W_{\text{hydraulics}} = 0.2673\,N_f(L_f + B_w)^{0.937} \] \[ W_{\text{electrical}} = 7.291\,R_{kva}^{0.782}L_a^{0.346}N_{\text{gen}}^{0.10}, \qquad W_{\text{avionics}} = 1.73\,W_{uav}^{0.983} \] \[ W_{\text{furnishings}} = 0.0577\,N_c^{0.1}W_c^{0.393}S_f^{0.75} \] \[ W_{\text{air conditioning}} = 62.36\,N_p^{0.25}\left(\frac{V_{pr}}{1000}\right)^{0.604}W_{uav}^{0.10} \] \[ W_{\text{APU}} = 2.2\,W_{\text{APU,uninstalled}} \] \[ W_{\text{anti-icing}} = 0.002\,W_{dg}, \qquad W_{\text{handling gear}} = 3.0\times10^{-4}\,W_{dg} \]

\(N_f\), the number of functions the flight controls perform (typically 4 to 7) and \(N_m\) how many are mechanical; \(S_{cs}\), all control surfaces (ft²); \(I_y\), the pitching moment of inertia (lb ft²); \(N_c\), the flight crew; \(K_r = 1.133\) for piston engines and \(K_{tp} = 0.793\) for turboprops; \(L_f\), the fuselage length (ft); \(R_{kva}\), the electrical rating (kV·A); \(L_a\), the electrical routing distance (ft); \(W_{uav}\), the uninstalled avionics (lb); \(W_c\), the maximum cargo (lb); \(N_p\), everyone aboard; \(V_{pr}\), the pressurized volume (ft³). The furnishings exclude the seats (32 lb per passenger, 60 lb per flight-deck seat) and lavatories (\(3.90N_{\text{pax}}^{1.33}\) lb in a business jet; 0.31 short-haul, 1.11 long-haul). Source: Raymer, Section 15.3 and Table 15.3.

For the business jet this module assumes five control functions, three of them mechanical (pitch, roll and yaw by cables; flaps and speed brakes powered), with \(I_y\) from a radius of gyration of \(0.3L_t\); two 28-volt, 300-ampere starter-generators, 16.8 kV·A; 136 kg (300 lb) of avionics before installation; no auxiliary power unit; and 160 kg of baggage as the cargo.

Example 5.2 — Avionics, electrical and cabin

With the assumptions above, a routing distance of 11.85 m (38.9 ft), a fuselage wetted area of 725.3 ft² and 8 passengers, estimate the avionics, the electrical system and everything in the cabin.

Show solution
\[ W_{\text{avionics}} = 1.73(299.8)^{0.983} = 470.8\ \text{lb} \] \[ W_{\text{electrical}} = 7.291(16.8)^{0.782}(38.9)^{0.346}(2)^{0.10} = 251.8\ \text{lb} \] \[ W_{\text{furnishings}} = 0.0577(2)^{0.1}(352.7)^{0.393}(725.3)^{0.75} = 86.6\ \text{lb} \] \[ W_{\text{seats}} = 8(32) + 2(60) = 376\ \text{lb}, \qquad W_{\text{lavatory}} = 3.90(8)^{1.33} = 62.0\ \text{lb} \]

In kilograms: avionics 213.5, electrical 114.2, and 238.0 for the cabin. Installation more than doubles the avionics' weight: racks, wiring, cooling and antennas.

The business jet's weight statement

With every group estimated, the statement is complete: 1882 kg of structure, 629 kg of propulsion and 993 kg of equipment, 3504 kg in all.

Key takeaways

  • The installed propulsion group weighs about 1.5 times the dry engines: nacelles, controls, starters and the fuel system.
  • Equipment weights follow the designer's decisions: control functions, electrical rating, avionics, cabin and pressurized volume.
  • The business jet's statement from the equations: structure 1882, propulsion 629, equipment 993, total 3504 kg.
  • Small aircraft carry much more equipment per seat than airliners.
