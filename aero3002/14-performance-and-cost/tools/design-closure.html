How it is calculated

  • The loop (Lesson 1): from a guess of \(W_0\), the wing at the wing loading chosen, rubber engines at the \(T/W\) chosen (Module 7: weight ∝ thrust\(^{1.1}\)), tails at Module 13's volume coefficients, the fuselage of Module 6; Module 10's drag build-up; Module 3's mission flown with that polar, the cruise in 20 steps at constant altitude and Mach; Module 12's group weights with its calibration \(k = 1.151\), at Module 11's design load factor (the larger of the maneuvering and gust limits, recomputed for each wing); \(W_0 = 980 + W_f + W_e\), repeated until it changes by less than 0.05 kg.
  • Sizing the engines: with "sized to the requirements", \(T/W\) is raised or lowered until the most demanding of cruise (Mach and altitude chosen, from \(0.9555\,W_0\)), the cruise ceiling (the climb rate chosen at the cruise altitude or 12 km, whichever is higher), the 2.4% one-engine-out gradient of §25.121 [3] (\(L/D = 12\) with takeoff flaps) and the takeoff field length is just met. Thrust falls with altitude as \(\sigma\).
  • Field lengths (Lesson 5): Torenbeek's method [2] as implemented in AeroSandbox, an open-source aircraft-design library [5], with 90% of the static thrust on the run, \(C_{D0} = 0.035\) with flaps and gear down, \(\mu = 0.02\), 4.5 s to react and 0.37 g of braking; landing at \(0.9\,W_0\) from 50 ft on a 3° approach, touchdown at \(1.15\,V_S\).
  • Cost (Lessons 6 and 7): DAPCA IV [1] in metric form at the maximum level speed between 6 and 10 km, in 2024 dollars; the engines priced in proportion to thrust; the operating cost per flight of the design mission after AeroSandbox's model [5], with a loan repaid over the life.
  • Not iterated: the CG and loading (Module 12), the scissor plot (Module 13) and the landing gear (Module 8) for the new size, and the weight of more powerful flaps. Your inputs are saved in this browser.
