Lesson 4 · 30 min

Trim and the Elevator

A stable aircraft must also be trimmed: held at zero pitching moment at the lift it needs, without the pilot pushing or pulling. The horizontal tail does it, usually by pushing down. This lesson finds how hard the business jet's tail must push, what that costs in drag, and what the elevator can add.

Learning objectives

  • Find the tail lift coefficient that trims the aircraft at a given lift coefficient and CG position.
  • Explain the trim drag of a tail that pushes down, and why a forward CG costs drag in cruise.
  • Estimate an elevator's effectiveness from its chord, and the tail lift it can add.

The tail lift for trim

Set Lesson 2's pitching moment to zero and write the wing's lift as the total less the tail's. The tail lift coefficient that trims the aircraft at the total lift coefficient \(C_L\) is then:

Trim

\[ C_{L,H} = \frac{C_{M,W} + C_L\,\bar x_{cg-ac} + C_{M,E}}{\eta_H\,(S_H/S)\,(l_H/\cbar)} \]

Source: Scholz, Aircraft Design lecture notes, Ch. 11, without the fuselage's moment; for control sizing leaving it out is conservative.

With the CG ahead of the wing's aerodynamic center (\(\bar x_{cg-ac} \lt 0\)) and a nose-down \(C_{M,W}\), the numerator is negative: the tail must push down. The wing then carries the aircraft's weight plus the tail's download, so it flies at a higher lift coefficient, with more induced drag: the trim drag. The further forward the CG, the larger the download and the drag.

Example 4.1 — The business jet trimmed in cruise

The business jet cruises at \(C_L = 0.253\) (Module 9) with Module 11's tail: \(S_H/S = 0.2095\), \(l_H/\cbar = 3.342\), \(C_{M,W} = -0.067\), \(\eta_H = 0.9\). Find the tail lift coefficient, and the wing's share of the lift, at the forward CG (17.1%).

Show solution
\[ C_{L,H} = \frac{-0.067 + 0.253(0.171 - 0.25)}{0.9(0.2095)(3.342)} = \frac{-0.0870}{0.630} = -0.138 \] \[ \frac{L_H}{L} = \frac{\eta_H(S_H/S)C_{L,H}}{C_L} = \frac{0.9(0.2095)(-0.138)}{0.253} = -0.103 \]

The tail pushes down with 10.3% of the weight, so the wing lifts 110.3% of it: 21.6% more induced drag than if it carried the weight alone. At the aft CG the tail pushes 5.6%, and the wing's induced drag rises 11.4%. Forward CG costs cruise drag; aft CG costs stability.

Landing at the forward CG, Module 11's tail would need \(C_{L,H} = -0.63\): more than a tail should be sized to give. That is the control problem of Lesson 5.

The elevator

The tail's lift is set in two ways: by its incidence on the fuselage, fixed (or set by a trimming stabilizer), and by the elevator, a plain flap on its trailing edge. Deflecting a flap changes the section's zero-lift angle, as if the whole surface had turned by \(\tau\delta\):

Control-surface effectiveness

\[ \tau = 1.576\left(\frac{c_f}{c}\right)^3 - 3.458\left(\frac{c_f}{c}\right)^2 + 2.882\,\frac{c_f}{c}, \qquad \Delta C_{L,H} = C_{L\alpha,H}\,\tau\,\delta_e \]

\(c_f/c\): the elevator's share of the tail's chord. Source: Raymer's equations as given in Iowa State's open notes.

Elevators travel about \(\pm 25^\circ\) to \(30^\circ\), and should not be needed near their limits for trim, which must leave room for maneuvering. Effectiveness grows with the chord share, but less than in proportion: the first 20% of chord gives more than half of what a whole moving tail would.

Example 4.2 — What the elevator can add

The business jet's elevator takes 30% of the tail's chord (Module 11). How much tail lift coefficient does \(25^\circ\) of trailing-edge-up elevator add, with \(C_{L\alpha,H} = 3.583\) per radian?

Show solution
\[ \tau = 1.576(0.027) - 3.458(0.09) + 2.882(0.3) = 0.596 \] \[ \Delta C_{L,H} = 3.583(0.596)\left(-\frac{25\pi}{180}\right) = -0.932 \]

Enough to reach the \(-0.63\) of a forward-CG landing from a trimmed cruise, but the tail's own angle of attack changes too, with the downwash of the flaps; sizing therefore asks less of the tail, \(-0.5\), and keeps the rest in reserve.

Key takeaways

  • Trim: \(C_{L,H} = (C_{M,W} + C_L\bar x_{cg-ac} + C_{M,E})/(\eta_H(S_H/S)(l_H/\cbar))\); a forward CG and a nose-down wing make the tail push down.
  • The download is trim drag: at the business jet's forward CG in cruise the wing carries 110% of the weight, with 22% more induced drag.
  • Control surfaces: \(\tau = 1.576(c_f/c)^3 - 3.458(c_f/c)^2 + 2.882(c_f/c)\); 0.596 for a 30% elevator.
  • Landing at the forward CG, Module 11's tail would need \(C_{L,H} = -0.63\), more than sizing allows.
