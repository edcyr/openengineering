Lesson 7 · 30 min

Compressibility and Wave Drag

As a jet approaches the speed of sound, the air over its wing reaches it first. Shocks form, the boundary layer behind them thickens, and the drag climbs steeply. This lesson finds where that drag rise starts for the business jet, using the drag-divergence estimate of Module 4, and how close to it the business jet cruises.

Learning objectives

  • Define the critical and drag-divergence Mach numbers.
  • Estimate them with Korn's equation, and the wave drag above the critical Mach number.
  • Explain how supercritical airfoils, sweep and area ruling delay the drag rise.
  • Find the Mach number of best range at a given altitude.

Critical Mach number and drag divergence

The critical Mach number \(M_{\text{crit}}\) is the flight Mach number at which the flow somewhere on the aircraft, usually over the wing's upper surface, first reaches the speed of sound. Above it a pocket of supersonic flow ends in a shock, which adds wave drag and, as it strengthens, separates the boundary layer. The drag-divergence Mach number \(\MDD\) is where the drag rise becomes steep, by one definition where \(dC_D/dM = 0.1\). Transports cruise near it: much below wastes speed, much above wastes fuel.

Korn's equation, and a first wave-drag estimate

\[ \MDD = \frac{\kappa_A}{\cos\Lambda} - \frac{t/c}{\cos^2\Lambda} - \frac{C_L}{10\cos^3\Lambda}, \qquad M_{\text{crit}} = \MDD - \left(\frac{0.1}{80}\right)^{1/3} \] \[ \Delta C_{D,\text{wave}} = 20\,(M - M_{\text{crit}})^4 \quad (M > M_{\text{crit}}) \]

Korn's equation is used as in Module 4 [3]: \(\kappa_A\) is 0.87 for conventional airfoils and 0.95 for supercritical ones, and \(\Lambda\) is the wing's sweep. The fourth-power rise is an empirical shape whose slope reaches 0.1 at \(\MDD\); it is a first estimate, not a substitute for wind-tunnel data.

Example 7.1 — The business jet's drag rise

At mid-cruise the business jet flies at \(C_L = 0.253\) (Lesson 1). With its 12% supercritical wing swept \(25^\circ\), find \(\MDD\), \(M_{\text{crit}}\) and the wave drag at Mach 0.80. What would a conventional airfoil of the same thickness give?

Show solution
\[ \MDD = \frac{0.95}{0.9063} - \frac{0.12}{0.8214} - \frac{0.253}{10(0.7444)} = 1.0482 - 0.1461 - 0.0340 = 0.868 \] \[ M_{\text{crit}} = 0.868 - 0.108 = 0.760, \qquad \Delta C_{D,\text{wave}} = 20(0.80 - 0.760)^4 = 0.00005 \]

Negligible: the business jet cruises below its drag rise, with room to fly faster. With \(\kappa_A = 0.87\), \(\MDD = 0.780\) and \(M_{\text{crit}} = 0.672\), so at Mach 0.80 the wave drag would be \(20(0.128)^4 = 0.0053\), a quarter of the zero-lift drag. The supercritical airfoil chosen in Module 4 is worth it.

Delaying the drag rise

  • Supercritical airfoils have a flat upper surface that keeps the supersonic pocket weak and ends it in a mild shock, and aft camber that recovers the lift. They raise \(\kappa_A\) from about 0.87 to 0.95, which a designer can spend on speed, on less sweep, or on a thicker, lighter wing with more fuel volume.
  • Sweep lowers the Mach number normal to the leading edge (Module 4), at the cost of a heavier wing, a lower maximum lift and tip stall.
  • Thinner wings and lower \(C_L\) both raise \(\MDD\), as Korn's equation shows.
  • Area ruling: the wave drag of the whole aircraft depends on how smoothly its total cross-sectional area grows and shrinks along its length. Narrowing the fuselage where the wing joins, or adding fairings, smooths it. It matters most near Mach 1, but transports use it for their wing-body fairings and nacelle positions.

Example 7.2 — Best range at 12 km

At 12 km, at what Mach number does the business jet's lift coefficient equal its best-range value of 0.371 (Lesson 6)? How does \(M\,L/D\) there compare with Mach 0.80?

Show solution

\(C_L\) varies as \(1/M^2\): from 0.253 at Mach 0.80,

\[ M = 0.80\sqrt{\frac{0.253}{0.371}} = 0.66 \]

There \(L/D = 13.74\) and \(M\,L/D = 9.1\); at Mach 0.80 it is \(0.80(10.8) = 8.6\). At 12 km the business jet would go about 5% farther on the same fuel by flying at Mach 0.66, but 17% slower. Lesson 8 looks at the other way to raise \(C_L\): flying higher.

Key takeaways

  • \(M_{\text{crit}}\): the first sonic flow on the aircraft; \(\MDD\): where the drag rise is steep.
  • Korn: \(\MDD = \kappa_A/\cos\Lambda - (t/c)/\cos^2\Lambda - C_L/(10\cos^3\Lambda)\); \(M_{\text{crit}} = \MDD - 0.108\); wave drag about \(20(M - M_{\text{crit}})^4\).
  • The business jet: \(\MDD = 0.868\), \(M_{\text{crit}} = 0.760\); negligible wave drag at Mach 0.80 with its supercritical airfoil, a quarter of \(C_{D0}\) with a conventional one.
  • At a fixed altitude, \(M\,L/D\) has a best Mach number; at 12 km the business jet's is about 0.66.
