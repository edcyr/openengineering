How it is calculated

  • Cabin width (Lesson 3): the seat benches (the seat width times the seats, or the economy, business or first-class bench widths when the seat width is 0), the aisles and two wall gaps, \(\dI\). Wall: \(\Delta d = 0.084 + 0.045\,\dI\) in meters, \(\dF = \dI + \Delta d\). The floor is drawn \(0.42\,\dI\) below the axis for small cabins, rising to \(0.12\,\dI\) for large ones.
  • Cabin length (Lesson 4): rows times pitch, plus 40 in per lavatory and 30 in per galley, plus the allowance for doors and stowage. Exits on each side by CS/FAR 25.807 (simplified); cabin crew one per 50 seats above 19.
  • Fuselage (Lesson 5): \(\lF = l_{\text{cockpit}} + l_{\text{cabin}} + (k_{TC} - k_{\text{overlap}})\,\dF\), rounded up to the next 0.1 m; tail angle \(\arctan(1/k_{TC})\).
  • Wing and tails (Lesson 6): the wing's quarter-MAC at the chosen share of \(\lF\); both tail arms the chosen share of \(\lF\), so the tails' quarter-MAC points sit one arm behind the wing's. \(S_{HT} = c_{HT}\bar c_WS_W/L_{HT}\) and \(S_{VT} = c_{VT}b_WS_W/L_{VT}\). A T-tail's horizontal surface sits on the fin's tip, and its area is found on its own, longer arm.
  • Fuel (Lesson 7): \(V_f = m_f/\rho_f\) against the wing tank volume \(0.54(S^{1.5}/\sqrt A)(t/c)_r(1 + \lambda\sqrt\tau + \lambda^2\tau)/(1 + \lambda)^2\).
  • Wetted areas and \(\LDmax\) (Lesson 8): the fuselage formula for a cylindrical middle part (valid for fineness ratios of 4.5 or more), the wing's exposed area outside the fuselage, the tails' whole areas, nacelles as cylinders; \(\LDmax = \tfrac12\sqrt{\pi Ae/(C_{fe}\Swet/\Sref)}\).
  • The drawings are schematic: the nose, tail cone and nacelle shapes are drawn from the lengths above, not designed. Engines are drawn in pairs on the rear fuselage or the wing, or as one propeller in the nose. The business jet preset is the module's; the others are illustrative designs, not real types.
