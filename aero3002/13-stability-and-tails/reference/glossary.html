A

Back to top
Aerodynamic center
The point about which a lifting surface's pitching moment does not change with angle of attack. For wings of aspect ratio 5 or more and moderate sweep it lies close to the quarter-chord of the mean aerodynamic chord, \(\bar x_{ac,W} \approx 0.25\).
See: Lesson 2Related: Wing pitching moment \(C_{M,W}\), Neutral point
Aft CG limit
The rearmost CG the aircraft may fly with: the required static margin ahead of the neutral point, in the most critical flight condition (cruise, for the business jet). After this module, the business jet's is 37.1% of the MAC.
See: Lesson 3, Lesson 6Related: Static margin, Neutral point, Stability line
Anhedral
Negative dihedral: wing tips lower than the root. Swept high wings, whose position and sweep already give a strong dihedral effect, often use it to keep the dihedral effect from growing too large.
See: Lesson 7Related: Dihedral \(\Gamma\), Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\)

C

Back to top
Control line
The horizontal tail area that trims the aircraft at a forward CG in its critical condition, with the tail at \(C_{L,H} = -0.5\): a straight line falling as the CG moves aft. It sets the forward limit of the scissor plot.
See: Lesson 5, Lesson 6Related: Forward CG limit, Scissor plot, Stability line
Control-surface effectiveness \(\tau\)
The fraction of a whole-surface rotation that a plain flap's deflection gives: \(\tau = 1.576(c_f/c)^3 - 3.458(c_f/c)^2 + 2.882(c_f/c)\); 0.596 for a 30% elevator or rudder.
See: Lesson 4Related: Elevator, Rudder, Rudder power \(C_{n\delta_r}\)
Crosswind case
Holding the aircraft aligned with the runway in a crosswind of 20% of the takeoff speed, a sideslip of \(11.3^\circ\), with the rudder within about \(20^\circ\): \(\delta_r = C_{n\beta}\beta/C_{n\delta_r}\). The business jet needs \(17.9^\circ\). More directional stability needs more rudder.
See: Lesson 8Related: Rudder, Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), Sideslip \(\beta\)

D

Back to top
DATCOM
The US Air Force's Stability and Control Data Compendium, the source of the downwash formula, the sidewash fit and the second fuselage \(C_{n\beta}\) method of the lessons [9].
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 7Related: Downwash \(\varepsilon\), Sidewash \(\sigma\), Fuselage yawing moment \(C_{n\beta,F}\)
Design loop
The cycle in which every change feeds the others. The scissor plot enlarged the business jet's horizontal tail to 6.75 m² and moved its wing; the larger tails raise \(C_{D0}\) to 0.0207, lower \((L/D)_{\max}\) to 15.67 and add about 172 kg to \(\Wo\) in one turn, which changes the CG range, and the scissor plot, again. Module 14 runs the loop to the end.
See: Lesson 8Related: Scissor plot, Wing position
Dihedral \(\Gamma\)
The upward angle of the wing from root to tip. Its rolling moment in a sideslip is \(-(C_{L\alpha,W}\Gamma/4)\cdot 2(1 + 2\lambda)/(3(1 + \lambda))\), per radian of \(\Gamma\). The business jet has \(3^\circ\).
See: Lesson 7Related: Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\), Anhedral, Wing position (rolling moment)
Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\)
The rolling-moment derivative in a sideslip, per radian; negative is stable (the wing into the wind rises). It adds the dihedral's term, the wing's position on the fuselage and the fin's. The business jet's is \(-0.071\) with \(3^\circ\) of dihedral; wing sweep, which the estimate leaves out, adds more.
See: Lesson 7Related: Dihedral \(\Gamma\), Wing position (rolling moment), Lateral stability, Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\)
Directional stability
The tendency of an aircraft in a sideslip to turn its nose back into the wind, as a weathervane does. Measured by \(C_{n\beta} \gt 0\); Part 25 (25.177 [8]) requires it to be positive with the rudder free.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 7Related: Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), Vertical tail (fin), Lateral stability
Downwash \(\varepsilon\)
The downward turn the wing gives the flow at the tail. Its gradient \(\partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha\) from DATCOM's formula grows with the wing's lift slope and falls as the tail moves up out of the wake; the tail's angle grows by only \(1 - \partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha\) for each degree of the aircraft's. The business jet's: 0.335 at low speed, 0.443 in cruise.
See: Lesson 2Related: Tail dynamic-pressure ratio \(\eta_H\), Tail term \(T\), T-tail
Dutch roll
A combined yawing and rolling oscillation; a large dihedral effect with weak directional stability makes it lightly damped. Part 25 requires it to be positively damped.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 7Related: Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\), Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), Spiral mode
Dynamic stability
What the motion does after a disturbance: whether it dies out (stable) or grows (unstable). Static stability is necessary for it, but not enough.
See: Lesson 1Related: Static stability, Short-period oscillation

E

Back to top
Effective aspect ratio (fin)
The aspect ratio a fin acts with, larger than its geometric one because the fuselage below and the horizontal tail act as end plates. Take it as 1.55 times the geometric aspect ratio [6]; the business jet's fin, with \(A_V = 1.5\), has \(C_{L\alpha,V} = 2.693\) per radian.
See: Lesson 7Related: Vertical tail (fin), Lift-curve slope \(C_{L\alpha}\)
Elevator
The plain flap on the horizontal tail's trailing edge. It travels about \(\pm 25^\circ\) to \(30^\circ\); the business jet's takes 30% of the chord, so \(\tau = 0.596\) and \(25^\circ\) adds \(\Delta C_{L,H} = -0.932\).
See: Lesson 4Related: Control-surface effectiveness \(\tau\), Tail lift coefficient \(C_{L,H}\), Trim
Engine pitching moment \(C_{M,E}\)
The thrust's moment about the CG: \(C_{M,E} = -Tz_E/(qS\cbar)\), with \(z_E\) the thrust line's height above the CG. Largest at low speed and full thrust: \(-0.098\) for the business jet in a go-around.
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 5Related: Go-around, Forward CG limit
Engine-out yawing moment \(N_E\)
The live engine's thrust times its lateral distance from the plane of symmetry, \(N_E = Ty_E\). The business jet's rear engines, 1.254 m out, give 13 245 N·m.
See: Lesson 8Related: Windmilling drag \(N_D\), Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\), Rudder

F

Back to top
Flap pitching moment \(\Delta c_m\)
The nose-down moment flaps add to the section: \(\Delta c_m = \Delta c_l(0.25 - 0.44\,c'/c)\) for Fowler and slotted flaps [5]. The method assumes full-span flaps, so the lessons scale it by the flapped share of the wing. The business jet's: \(-0.383\).
See: Lesson 5Related: Wing pitching moment \(C_{M,W}\), Control line
Forward CG limit
The foremost CG the aircraft may fly with: the tail must still hold the nose up at high lift with flaps down. Control, through the control line, sets it. After this module, the business jet's is 21.5% of the MAC.
See: Lesson 5Related: Control line, Go-around, Aft CG limit
Fuselage factor \(K_f\)
An empirical factor for the fuselage's pitching-moment slope, \(0.01 - 0.063\,x_0 + 0.211\,x_0^2\) per degree, with \(x_0\) the wing root's quarter-chord as a fraction of the fuselage's length (0.1 to 0.62). Moving the wing aft raises it. The fit is FAST-GA's [11], of Raymer's chart [1].
See: Lesson 2Related: Fuselage pitching-moment slope \(C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}}\)
Fuselage pitching-moment slope \(C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}}\)
The fuselage's contribution to \(C_{M\alpha}\), always destabilizing: \(K_fW_f^2L_f/(\cbar S)\) per degree. The business jet's is \(+0.707\) per radian.
See: Lesson 2Related: Fuselage factor \(K_f\), Pitch stiffness \(C_{M\alpha}\)
Fuselage yawing moment \(C_{n\beta,F}\)
The fuselage's destabilizing yawing-moment derivative. The volume method gives \(-1.3\,\text{Vol}_F/(Sb)\,(d/w)\); DATCOM's, \(-(180/\pi)k_Nk_{Rl}l_F^2d/(Sb)\). For the business jet they give \(-0.068\) and \(-0.117\): they differ by a factor of 1.7.
See: Lesson 7Related: Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), DATCOM

G

Back to top
Go-around missed approach
Climbing away from an aborted landing with full flaps and takeoff thrust. For rear engines, whose thrust line is above the CG, it is the critical control case. The lessons fly it at \(1.3V_S\), \(C_L = C_{L\max}/1.69\), at the landing weight.
See: Lesson 5Related: Engine pitching moment \(C_{M,E}\), Forward CG limit, Control line

H

Back to top
Horizontal tail volume coefficient \(c_{HT}\)
\(c_{HT} = S_Hl_H/(S\cbar)\): the tail's area times its arm, made dimensionless. Module 4 took 0.70 from statistics; the scissor plot gives the business jet 0.82.
See: Lesson 6Related: Scissor plot, Tail arm \(l_H\)

L

Back to top
Lateral stability
The tendency of an aircraft in a sideslip to roll its low wing back up, away from the slip. Measured by \(C_{l\beta} \lt 0\).
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 7Related: Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\), Directional stability
Lift-curve slope \(C_{L\alpha}\)
The rate at which a surface's lift coefficient grows with angle of attack, per radian, from Module 4's formula with the half-chord sweep and the Mach number. The business jet's wing: 4.507 at Mach 0.2, 5.969 at Mach 0.80.
See: Lesson 2Related: Downwash \(\varepsilon\), Neutral point
Longitudinal stability
Stability in pitch: a nose-up disturbance must bring a nose-down moment. The horizontal tail does most of the work.
See: Lesson 1Related: Pitch stiffness \(C_{M\alpha}\), Static margin

M

Back to top
Mean aerodynamic chord (MAC)
The reference chord, \(\cbar\), along which CG and neutral-point positions are given, as fractions \(\bar x\) (or percentages) from its leading edge. The business jet's is 2.015 m.
See: Lesson 1Related: Static margin, Neutral point
Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\)
The lowest speed at which full rudder (with no more than \(5^\circ\) of bank) can hold the aircraft straight with an engine failed. 14 CFR 25.149 [8] requires it no higher than \(1.13\,V_{SR}\); 25.107 puts the rotation and takeoff safety speeds at least 5% and 10% above it. The business jet's: 54.6 m/s.
See: Lesson 8Related: Rudder, Engine-out yawing moment \(N_E\), Reference stall speed \(V_{SR}\)
Moment at zero angle of attack \(C_{M0}\)
The pitching-moment coefficient about the CG at zero angle of attack. With \(C_{M\alpha} \lt 0\), a positive \(C_{M0}\) puts the trim point at a positive angle of attack, where the wing lifts.
See: Lesson 1Related: Pitching-moment curve, Pitch stiffness \(C_{M\alpha}\)

N

Back to top
Neutral point stick-fixed
The CG position at which \(C_{M\alpha} = 0\): the aerodynamic center of the whole aircraft, a weighted average of the wing's and tail's, pulled forward by the fuselage. Higher Mach moves it forward. The business jet's, after this module: 46.7% of the MAC at low speed, 42.1% in cruise.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 3Related: Static margin, Tail term \(T\), Stick-free neutral point
Neutral stability
No moment results from a disturbance: the aircraft stays where it was put. In pitch, the CG at the neutral point.
See: Lesson 1Related: Static stability, Neutral point

P

Back to top
Part 25 stability rules 14 CFR 25.171–25.181
The transport rules state stability as what the pilot feels: a stick-force gradient in pitch (25.173), recovery from a skid and a rising low wing in a sideslip (25.177), and damped short-period and Dutch-roll oscillations (25.181) [8].
See: Lesson 1Related: Stick-force gradient, Directional stability, Dutch roll
Pitch stiffness \(C_{M\alpha}\)
The slope of the pitching-moment curve about the CG, per radian; negative is stable. \(C_{M\alpha} = -C_{L\alpha}\,\text{SM}\). The business jet's, with Module 12's layout at its aft CG, is \(-0.401\) at low speed.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 3Related: Static margin, Pitching-moment curve, Neutral point
Pitching-moment curve
\(C_{M,cg}\) plotted against the angle of attack. The aircraft trims where it crosses zero; its slope is \(C_{M\alpha}\), and its value at zero angle of attack \(C_{M0}\).
See: Lesson 1Related: Pitch stiffness \(C_{M\alpha}\), Moment at zero angle of attack \(C_{M0}\), Trim

R

Back to top
Reference stall speed \(V_{SR}\)
The stall speed in the configuration considered, \(\sqrt{2W/(\rho_0SC_{L\max})}\); in the takeoff configuration at the maximum takeoff weight it bounds \(V_{MC}\). The business jet's: 48.33 m/s.
See: Lesson 8Related: Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\)
Rudder
The plain flap on the fin. With an engine failed at \(V_{MC}\) it must balance \(N_E + N_D\) within about \(25^\circ\): \(\delta_r = (N_E + N_D)/(qSb\,C_{n\delta_r})\). The business jet's 30% rudder needs \(14.9^\circ\).
See: Lesson 8Related: Rudder power \(C_{n\delta_r}\), Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\), Crosswind case
Rudder power \(C_{n\delta_r}\)
The yawing moment per radian of rudder: \(0.9\,C_{L\alpha,V}(S_V/S)(l_V/b)\tau_r\). The business jet's is 0.0834.
See: Lesson 8Related: Rudder, Control-surface effectiveness \(\tau\), Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\)

S

Back to top
Scissor plot
The control line and the stability line drawn together against the CG position. They open like scissors; the smallest tail is the one at which the gap is as wide as the CG range the loading needs. The business jet's: 6.75 m², with the CG from 21.5% to 37.1% of the MAC.
See: Lesson 6Related: Control line, Stability line, Wing position
Short-period oscillation
A quick pitching oscillation at nearly constant speed; Part 25 requires it to be heavily damped.
See: Lesson 1Related: Dynamic stability, Dutch roll
Sideslip \(\beta\)
The angle between the relative wind and the plane of symmetry, positive when the wind comes from the right.
See: Lesson 7Related: Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\), Crosswind case
Sidewash \(\sigma\)
The sideways turn the wing and fuselage give the flow at the fin. With the fin's dynamic-pressure ratio it is estimated from a DATCOM fit: \((1 + \partial\sigma/\partial\beta)\eta_V = 0.724 + 3.06(S_V/S)/(1 + \cos\Lambda) + 0.4z_w/d + 0.009A\); 1.156 for the business jet.
See: Lesson 7Related: Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), Vertical tail (fin), DATCOM
Spiral mode
A slow divergence in which the aircraft drifts into a tightening, descending turn; a small dihedral effect with strong directional stability encourages it.
See: Lesson 7Related: Dutch roll, Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\)
Stability line
The horizontal tail area that keeps the required static margin behind an aft CG; it rises as the CG moves aft. It must hold at low speed and in cruise; for the business jet, cruise decides. It sets the aft limit of the scissor plot.
See: Lesson 6Related: Scissor plot, Control line, Aft CG limit
Static margin
The distance from the CG forward to the neutral point, as a fraction of the MAC: \(\text{SM} = \bar x_{np} - \bar x_{cg}\). Business jets and transports need 5% at the aft CG, light propeller aircraft 10% ([5], after [2]); engines take 1% to 3% of it on jets.
See: Lesson 1Related: Neutral point, Pitch stiffness \(C_{M\alpha}\), Aft CG limit
Static stability
The first tendency after a disturbance: stable if the first moment pushes the aircraft back toward where it was, unstable if it pushes it further away.
See: Lesson 1Related: Dynamic stability, Neutral stability
Stick-force gradient
The pull needed per knot to fly slower than the trim speed. Part 25 (25.173 [8]) requires at least 1 lb per 6 knots on average; near the neutral point it vanishes.
See: Lesson 1, Lesson 3Related: Static margin, Part 25 stability rules
Stick-free neutral point
The neutral point with the elevator free to float with the flow: the tail does a little less, so it lies a few percent of the MAC ahead of the stick-fixed one.
See: Lesson 3Related: Neutral point

T

Back to top
T-tail
A horizontal tail on top of the fin, out of the wing's wake and downwash: a smaller \(\partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha\) and a higher \(\eta_H\).
See: Lesson 2Related: Downwash \(\varepsilon\), Tail dynamic-pressure ratio \(\eta_H\)
Tail arm \(l_H\)
The distance from the wing's aerodynamic center to the tail's. Moving the wing changes it; the business jet's is 6.855 m after this module.
See: Lesson 2, Lesson 6Related: Horizontal tail volume coefficient \(c_{HT}\), Tail term \(T\)
Tail dynamic-pressure ratio \(\eta_H\)
\(q_H/q\): the wing's wake lowers the dynamic pressure at the tail, to 0.85–0.95 of the free stream, typically 0.9 [5].
See: Lesson 2Related: Downwash \(\varepsilon\), Tail term \(T\)
Tail lift coefficient \(C_{L,H}\)
The horizontal tail's lift coefficient on its own area; negative when it pushes down. Control sizing asks no more than \(C_{L,H} = -0.5\) [5], which keeps the tail well short of its stall.
See: Lesson 4, Lesson 5Related: Trim, Control line
Tail term \(T\)
The horizontal tail's weight in the neutral-point equation: \(T = \eta_H(S_H/S)C_{L\alpha,H}(1 - \partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha)\); 0.449 for the business jet at low speed with Module 12's tail.
See: Lesson 3Related: Neutral point, Downwash \(\varepsilon\), Tail dynamic-pressure ratio \(\eta_H\)
Trim
Flight at zero pitching moment about the CG at the lift needed, with no force on the controls. The tail lift for trim is \(C_{L,H} = (C_{M,W} + C_L\bar x_{cg-ac} + C_{M,E})/(\eta_H(S_H/S)(l_H/\cbar))\).
See: Lesson 4Related: Tail lift coefficient \(C_{L,H}\), Trim drag, Pitching-moment curve
Trim drag
The extra induced drag when the tail pushes down and the wing must lift more than the weight. At the business jet's forward CG in cruise the wing carries 110.3% of the weight, with 21.6% more induced drag.
See: Lesson 4Related: Trim, Tail lift coefficient \(C_{L,H}\)

V

Back to top
Vertical tail (fin)
The surface that provides directional stability and carries the rudder. It is sized by the largest of directional stability, engine failure and crosswind. The business jet's, 3.67 m² at Module 4's volume coefficient of 0.060, meets all three.
See: Lesson 7, Lesson 8Related: Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\), Rudder, Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\)

W

Back to top
Weathercock derivative \(C_{n\beta}\)
The yawing-moment derivative in a sideslip, per radian; positive turns the nose into the wind. The wing adds a little, the fuselage subtracts, the fin provides the rest. Target: at least 0.0571 (0.001 per degree). The business jet's is 0.064 to 0.113 at low speed.
See: Lesson 7Related: Directional stability, Fuselage yawing moment \(C_{n\beta,F}\), Sidewash \(\sigma\), Vertical tail (fin)
Windmilling drag \(N_D\)
The failed engine's drag, which yaws the aircraft the same way as the live engine's thrust: \(N_D = k_DN_E\), with \(k_D\) about 0.15 for low-bypass and 0.25 for high-bypass turbofans [5]; the business jet takes 0.25.
See: Lesson 8Related: Engine-out yawing moment \(N_E\), Minimum control speed \(V_{MC}\)
Wing pitching moment \(C_{M,W}\)
The wing's moment about its aerodynamic center: the airfoil's \(c_{m,0}\) times \(A\cos^2\Lambda/(A + 2\cos\Lambda)\). With \(c_{m,0} = -0.10\) for its supercritical airfoil, the business jet's is \(-0.067\); with flaps down, \(-0.228\).
See: Lesson 2Related: Aerodynamic center, Flap pitching moment \(\Delta c_m\)
Wing position
Moving the wing moves the CG range along the MAC (Module 12), and changes the tail's arm and \(K_f\), so the tail and the wing are iterated together. The business jet's wing ends at 7.405 m, 47.8% of the fuselage, 0.12 m forward of Module 12's place.
See: Lesson 6Related: Scissor plot, Design loop, Tail arm \(l_H\)
Wing position (rolling moment)
A wing low on the fuselage makes a destabilizing rolling moment in a sideslip, a high wing a stabilizing one: \(1.2\sqrt A(z_w/b)((d + w)/b)\), with \(z_w\) positive below the axis. \(+0.029\) for the business jet's low wing.
See: Lesson 7Related: Dihedral effect \(C_{l\beta}\), Dihedral \(\Gamma\), Anhedral
