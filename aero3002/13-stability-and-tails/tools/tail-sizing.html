How it is calculated

  • Geometry and weights (Modules 6 and 12): the chosen layout with Module 7's nacelles on the business jet, Module 12's calibrated weight statement and loading diagram, and Module 12's method for moving the wing (with the main gear and the wing-mounted groups). The tails' arm changes with the wing; the vertical tail keeps Module 4's volume coefficient.
  • Neutral point (Lessons 2 and 3): the wing's lift slope from Module 4's formula at low speed (Mach 0.2) and in cruise; the fuselage's \(C_{M\alpha}\) from \(K_f\); downwash from the DATCOM formula [9], scaled with the wing's lift slope; \(\eta_H = 0.9\).
  • Stability line (Lesson 6): the tail area that puts the neutral point the required static margin behind the aft CG, at low speed and in cruise; the larger holds.
  • Control line (Lesson 5): the tail area that trims at the forward CG with full flaps, with the tail at \(C_{L,H}\) as chosen: in a go-around at \(1.3\,V_S\) with takeoff thrust, and on landing at \(C_{L\max}\). The wing's moment includes the flaps', scaled by the flapped share of the wing; the thrust acts \(z_E\) above the CG.
  • Scissor plot (Lesson 6): the smallest tail whose stable, controllable CG range is as wide as the range needed; "Iterate the wing" moves the wing until the loading range sits in that range.
  • Vertical tail (Lessons 7 and 8): \(C_{n\beta}\) at low speed, the aft CG and \(C_L = 0.253\), with the fuselage by the volume method or DATCOM's, target 0.0571 per rad [5]; engine failure at \(V_{MC} = 1.13\,V_{SR}\) [8] in the takeoff configuration with rudder no more than \(25^\circ\); crosswind of 20% of the takeoff speed [6] at \(C_L = C_{L\max,TO}/1.21\), with the larger of the two fuselage estimates (more directional stability needs more rudder). The fuselage method chosen applies to the stability check.
  • The business jet preset is the design you follow through the lessons; the other three are illustrative, not real types. The turboprop presets follow seating rules that hold their CG range to 30% of the MAC. Typical values worth varying: \(c_{m0}\), the flaps' moment, \(C_{L,H}\), the effective aspect ratio of the fin (1.55 times the geometric) and the windmilling drag.
