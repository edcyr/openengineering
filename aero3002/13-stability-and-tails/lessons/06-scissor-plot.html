Sizing the Horizontal Tail

Stability sets how far aft the CG may go for a given tail, and control how far forward. Drawn together against the CG position, the two requirements open like a pair of scissors: the tail must be large enough that the whole CG range of the loading diagram fits between the blades. This lesson sizes the business jet's horizontal tail, and finds where its wing should be.

Learning objectives

  • Draw the stability line and the control line on one diagram, the scissor plot.
  • Find the smallest horizontal tail whose stable, controllable CG range is as wide as the loading needs.
  • Iterate the tail's size with the wing's position, and check the result against the landing gear.

Two lines

The control line is Lesson 5's: the tail area that holds the nose up at a forward CG, falling as the CG moves aft. The stability line comes from the neutral point: the tail area that puts the neutral point a static margin behind an aft CG. Solving Lesson 3's neutral-point equation for the tail area:

Horizontal tail area for stability

\[ \frac{S_H}{S} = \frac{C_{L\alpha,W}\left(\bar x_{cg} + \text{SM} - \bar x_{ac,W}\right) + C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}}}{\eta_H\,C_{L\alpha,H}\left(1 - \dfrac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial\alpha}\right)\left(\bar x_{ac,H} - \bar x_{cg} - \text{SM}\right)} \]

It rises as the CG moves aft. It must hold at every speed: for the business jet, cruise is the more demanding. After [5], Chapter 11, with the fuselage term.

Example 6.1 — The stability line at Module 12's aft CG

In cruise, with Module 12's layout: \(C_{L\alpha,W} = 5.969\), \(\eta_H C_{L\alpha,H}(1 - \partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha) = 0.9(4.326)(0.557) = 2.168\), \(C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}} = 0.707\), \(\bar x_{ac,H} = 3.592\). What tail does the aft CG, 32.9%, need with a 5% margin?

Show solution
\[ \frac{S_H}{S} = \frac{5.969(0.329 + 0.05 - 0.25) + 0.707}{2.168(3.592 - 0.329 - 0.05)} = \frac{1.477}{6.966} = 0.212 \]

\(S_H = 5.94\) m², just more than Module 12's 5.87 (Lesson 3). The control line at the forward CG asked for 7.89 m² (Lesson 5).

The scissor plot

For a tail of a given area, the CG may lie anywhere between the control line (forward) and the stability line (aft). The loading diagram fixes the width of the CG range the aircraft needs, 15.8% of the MAC for the business jet; the smallest tail is the one at which the gap between the lines is just that wide. Where that gap lies, along the MAC, says where the CG range should be, and the CG range is moved by moving the wing (Module 12).

With Module 12's wing the smallest tail is 6.98 m², and the range it allows runs from 21.0% to 36.9%: four percent of the MAC aft of where the loading puts it. Moving the wing forward moves the loading range aft along the MAC. But moving the wing also changes the tail's arm and the fuselage's \(K_f\), so the lines move too: the tail and the wing must be iterated together.

Example 6.2 — Iterating the tail and the wing

Start from Module 12's wing (0.551 m aft of Module 6's position). At each step, find the smallest tail and the CG range it allows, move the wing (with Module 12's wing-shift method) to center the loading range on it, and repeat.

Three steps converge: the wing's quarter-MAC at 7.405 m, 47.8% of the fuselage (0.12 m forward of Module 12's, 0.43 m aft of Module 6's), and a horizontal tail of 6.75 m². Its volume coefficient is \(6.75(6.855)/(28 \times 2.015) = 0.82\), against the 0.70 Module 4 took from statistics.

The design checks out. The neutral point is at 46.7% of the MAC at low speed and 42.1% in cruise: exactly 5% behind the aft CG, 37.1%. The main gear moves with the wing, to 8.08 m: the nose wheel carries 6.9% to 11.9% of the weight and the tip-back angle at the aft CG is \(19.8^\circ\), inside Module 8's limits of 5% to 20% and at least \(15^\circ\).

The procedure

Estimate the tail from a volume coefficient (Module 4); compute its weight and the CG (Module 12); place the wing for a reasonable CG range; size the tail on the scissor plot; if it differs from the estimate by more than about 10%, recompute its weight; if the CG range is not where the scissor plot puts it, move the wing, and repeat. The procedure follows [5], Chapter 11, after [2].

Key takeaways

  • The scissor plot: the control line (forward limit) and the stability line (aft limit) against the CG position; the tail must make the gap as wide as the loading range.
  • The stability line must hold at every speed; for the business jet, cruise decides.
  • The business jet's horizontal tail is 6.75 m² (volume coefficient 0.82), with the wing at 47.8% of the fuselage and the CG range at 21.5% to 37.1%.
  • Tail size and wing position are iterated together; the landing gear moves with the wing and still passes Module 8's checks.
