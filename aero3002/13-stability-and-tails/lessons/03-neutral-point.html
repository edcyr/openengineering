The Neutral Point and Static Margin

The neutral point is the CG position at which the contributions of Lesson 2 cancel. Finding it tells the designer how far aft the CG may go, and whether the tail is large enough for the CG range the loading needs. For the business jet, the answer changes between takeoff and cruise.

Learning objectives

  • Compute the stick-fixed neutral point from the wing, fuselage and tail contributions.
  • Find the static margin at the aft CG, at low speed and in cruise, and compare it with the requirement.
  • Explain how the tail's size and arm, the downwash and the Mach number move the neutral point.

Where the contributions cancel

Set \(C_{M\alpha} = 0\) in Lesson 2's equation and solve for the CG position. With the tail's term written \(T = \eta_H(S_H/S)C_{L\alpha,H}(1 - \partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha)\):

Stick-fixed neutral point

\[ \bar x_{np} = \frac{C_{L\alpha,W}\,\bar x_{ac,W} - C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}} + T\,\bar x_{ac,H}}{C_{L\alpha,W} + T}, \qquad C_{M\alpha} = -\left(C_{L\alpha,W} + T\right)\left(\bar x_{np} - \bar x_{cg}\right) \]

\(\bar x_{ac,W}\) and \(\bar x_{ac,H}\): the wing's and tail's aerodynamic centers along the MAC, as fractions of it from its leading edge. Source: Raymer's equations [1], as given in [6].

The neutral point is a weighted average of the wing's and the tail's aerodynamic centers, pulled forward by the fuselage. "Stick-fixed" means with the elevator held still; with the stick free, the elevator floats with the flow and the tail does a little less, so the neutral point moves a few percent forward.

Example 3.1 — The business jet at low speed

With Module 12's layout: \(C_{L\alpha,W} = 4.507\), \(C_{L\alpha,H} = 3.583\) per radian, \(\partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha = 0.335\), \(\eta_H = 0.9\), \(S_H = 5.866\) m² on \(S = 28\) m², \(C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}} = 0.707\). The MAC's leading edge is at 7.022 m and the tail's aerodynamic center at 14.26 m, with \(\cbar = 2.015\) m. Find the neutral point and the static margin at the aft CG, 32.9%.

Show solution
\[ T = 0.9(0.2095)(3.583)(0.665) = 0.449, \qquad \bar x_{ac,H} = \frac{14.26 - 7.022}{2.015} = 3.592 \] \[ \bar x_{np} = \frac{4.507(0.25) - 0.707 + 0.449(3.592)}{4.507 + 0.449} = \frac{2.033}{4.956} = 0.410 \]

The neutral point is at 41.0% of the MAC: 8.1% behind the aft CG, more than the 5% a business jet needs.

The neutral point in cruise

Compressibility raises every lift-curve slope, the wing's more than the tail's because its aspect ratio is higher, and the downwash grows with the wing's slope. The tail's share of the total falls, and the neutral point moves toward the wing's aerodynamic center.

Example 3.2 — The business jet at Mach 0.80

At Mach 0.80: \(C_{L\alpha,W} = 5.969\), \(C_{L\alpha,H} = 4.326\) per radian, \(\partial\varepsilon/\partial\alpha = 0.443\). Find the neutral point and the static margin at the aft CG.

Show solution
\[ T = 0.9(0.2095)(4.326)(0.557) = 0.454 \] \[ \bar x_{np} = \frac{5.969(0.25) - 0.707 + 0.454(3.592)}{5.969 + 0.454} = 0.376 \]

The static margin at the aft CG is \(0.376 - 0.329 = 0.047\): just under the 5% required, before the engines take their 1% to 3%. The tail is too small for Module 12's CG range in cruise; Lesson 6 resizes it.

The neutral point moves with every term of its equation: a larger tail, a longer arm, less downwash or a smaller fuselage move it aft; a wing of higher aspect ratio, or the wing moved aft along the fuselage (which raises \(K_f\)), move it forward relative to the wing's aerodynamic center.

What the margin feels like

For a trimmed aircraft, \(C_M\) changes with \(C_L\) at the rate \(-\text{SM}\). A large margin means a large nose-down moment for every bit of extra lift: the pilot must pull hard to fly slower, and the elevator travels far between speeds. Near the neutral point the aircraft drifts from its trim speed with little to bring it back, which the stick-force rule of 14 CFR 25.173 [8] forbids.

Key takeaways

  • \(\bar x_{np} = (C_{L\alpha,W}\bar x_{ac,W} - C_{M\alpha,\text{fus}} + T\bar x_{ac,H})/(C_{L\alpha,W} + T)\): a weighted average of the aerodynamic centers, pulled forward by the fuselage.
  • The business jet with Module 12's layout: neutral point 41.0% at low speed, 37.6% in cruise; static margin at the aft CG 8.1% and 4.7%.
  • Compressibility moves the neutral point forward: the cruise case decides the aft limit.
  • The tail is slightly too small for Module 12's CG range in cruise.
