Control at the Forward CG

Stability limits how far aft the CG may go. Control limits how far forward: with the CG far ahead of the wing, the flaps down and the engines at full thrust, the tail may not be able to hold the nose up. This lesson finds the tail the business jet needs for its forward CG.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the flight conditions that limit the forward CG, and why rear engines make the go-around critical.
  • Estimate the flaps' nose-down moment and the engines' moment.
  • Find the horizontal tail area that the control requirement needs at a forward CG.

What limits the forward CG

The tail must trim the aircraft at the highest lift it flies at, with the most nose-down moment, at the forward CG. The flaps make it worse: they move the wing's lift aft and add a large nose-down moment. So do engines whose thrust line passes above the CG, at full thrust and low speed. The critical cases are therefore:

  • For engines below the CG, under the wing: the landing approach with full flaps at the forward CG, where idle thrust takes away their nose-up help.
  • For engines above the CG, at the rear: the missed approach (go-around) with full flaps at the forward CG, where takeoff thrust adds a nose-down moment just when the pilot pulls up.
  • Takeoff rotation, which the tail must achieve about the main wheels at the rotation speed (no less than 105% of \(V_{MC}\), 14 CFR 25.107 [8]), and the landing flare, both in ground effect. They need a separate analysis.

The control requirement

Trim at the critical condition, Lesson 4's equation solved for the tail's area instead of its lift:

Horizontal tail area for control

\[ \frac{S_H}{S} = \frac{C_L}{C_{L,H}\,\eta_H\,(l_H/\cbar)}\,\bar x_{cg-ac} + \frac{C_{M,W} + C_{M,E}}{C_{L,H}\,\eta_H\,(l_H/\cbar)}, \qquad C_{L,H} = -0.5 \]

A tail asked for no more than \(C_{L,H} = -0.5\) stays well away from its own stall. Source: [5], Chapter 11.

It is a straight line in the CG position: the further forward the CG, the larger the tail. With flaps the wing's moment uses the flapped section's:

\[ c_{m,0,\text{flapped}} = c_{m,0} + \Delta c_m, \qquad \Delta c_m = \Delta c_l\left(\frac{x_{ac}}{\cbar} - 0.44\,\frac{c'}{c}\right) \]

for Fowler and slotted flaps [5], with \(\Delta c_l\) the flaps' increment in section lift. The method assumes the flaps span the whole wing, which overstates the moment, so scale \(\Delta c_m\) by the flapped share of the wing area (Module 10). The engines' moment is \(C_{M,E} = -Tz_E/(qS\cbar)\).

Example 5.1 — The flapped wing and the engines in a go-around

The business jet's Fowler flaps give \(\Delta c_l = 1.3(1.15) = 1.495\) with \(c'/c = 1.15\), over 62.8% of the wing (Module 10). It goes around at \(1.3V_S\) in the landing configuration (\(\CLmax = 2.1\)) at its landing weight, 6249 kg, with both engines at takeoff thrust, 21.12 kN, 0.459 m above its CG. Find \(C_{M,W}\) and \(C_{M,E}\).

Show solution
\[ \Delta c_m = 1.495\,(0.25 - 0.44(1.15)) = -0.383 \] \[ C_{M,W} = \left(-0.10 - 0.628(0.383)\right)(0.670) = -0.228 \] \[ C_L = \frac{2.1}{1.3^2} = 1.243, \qquad V = \sqrt{\frac{2(6249)(9.807)}{1.225(28)(1.243)}} = 53.6\ \text{m/s} \] \[ C_{M,E} = -\frac{21\,120(0.459)}{0.5(1.225)(53.6)^2(28)(2.015)} = -0.098 \]

Both nose-down, and together 0.326: the tail must answer them.

Example 5.2 — The tail the forward CG needs

With Example 5.1's values and \(l_H/\cbar = 3.342\), find the tail area the go-around needs at Module 12's forward CG, 17.1% of the MAC, and compare the landing at \(\CLmax\) with idle thrust.

Show solution
\[ \frac{S_H}{S} = \frac{1.243(0.171 - 0.25) - 0.228 - 0.098}{-0.5(0.9)(3.342)} = \frac{-0.424}{-1.504} = 0.2819 \]

\(S_H = 0.2819(28) = 7.89\) m². Landing at \(\CLmax\) with idle thrust needs \((2.1(-0.079) - 0.228)/(-1.504) = 0.262\), 7.34 m²: the go-around decides, as it does for rear engines. Module 12's tail, 5.87 m², is too small for its forward CG.

Where the nose-down moment comes from

At the forward CG in a go-around, every term pitches the business jet nose-down: the wing's lift acting behind the CG, the flaps' moment and the engines' thrust. Moving the engines under the wing would turn their term around, which is why airliners' control limits come from the landing instead.

Key takeaways

  • Control limits the forward CG: at high lift with flaps the tail must hold the nose up.
  • \(S_H/S = [C_L\bar x_{cg-ac} + C_{M,W} + C_{M,E}]/(C_{L,H}\eta_H l_H/\cbar)\), with \(C_{L,H} = -0.5\) for sizing.
  • Rear engines make the go-around critical: the business jet needs 7.89 m² at Module 12's forward CG, against 5.87 m².
  • Engines under the wing help in a go-around; their critical case is the landing.
