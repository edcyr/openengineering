How it works

  • Work on your own with a calculator and scrap paper. Try it without notes first: that shows you what you really know.
  • Nothing is marked until you press Submit quiz. Until then you can move between questions and change any answer.
  • Each question is either right or wrong. When a question has several boxes, every box must be right.
  • After you submit, you see your score, a review of every question with a worked solution, and a results sheet you can print or save as a PDF.
  • Use SI units, \(\mu = 3.986\times10^{14}\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}^2\), \(R_E = 6378\ \text{km}\) and \(g_0 = 9.80665\ \text{m/s}^2\). Each box shows its unit.

How close is close enough?

Answers are accepted within \(1\%\) of the exact value, so three or four significant figures are plenty. Typing the calculation itself, such as 2*sqrt(3.986e14/6.778e6)*sin(2.5*pi/180), avoids rounding errors. Watch the units: radii in meters from Earth's center (add 6378 km to the altitude), angles in degrees converted inside sin and cos, and specific impulse times \(g_0\) for the exhaust velocity.
