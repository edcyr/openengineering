How it is calculated

  • \(\Delta v\) budget (Lesson 4): the circular speed \(\sqrt{\mu/r}\) at the orbit's altitude, relative to the rotating site, \(v_{\text{rel}} = \sqrt{v^2 - 2vv_E\sin A_z + v_E^2}\) with \(\sin A_z = \cos i/\cos\phi\). Add the gravity, drag and steering losses you enter. A sun-synchronous inclination comes from the \(J_2\) regression of the node (Lesson 2).
  • Staging (Lesson 5): the optimal split with a Lagrange multiplier, \(n_i = (c_i\eta - 1)/(c_i\varepsilon_i\eta)\), and the stage masses from the top down. Each stage's \(I_{sp}\) is its mean over its burn; for the first stage the sea-level and vacuum values set its thrust at liftoff and in vacuum.
  • Thrust (Lesson 6): \(T = (T/W)\,m_0g_0\) at ignition (sea level for the first stage), burn time \(m_p/\dot m\), and the acceleration at burnout in vacuum.
  • Bottom-up inert mass (Lessons 7 and 8): tanks sized for ullage pressure (300 kPa; a solid motor case for 6 MPa) plus the head at liftoff, at the protoflight or prototype factors of NASA-STD-5001B [6], no thinner than the minimum gauge, times a factor for stiffeners and insulation. Add engines by their thrust-to-weight ratio (a nozzle fraction for solids), 1% residual propellant, the stage's allowance for structure, avionics and pressurization, and the fairing on the stage that carries it. Closing the loop repeats the staging with these structural coefficients until they stop changing.
  • The other three launchers are hypothetical designs for comparison. The propellant data, the materials and the mass allowances are typical values.
